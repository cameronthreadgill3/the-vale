# Chapter 12
## The Road Beneath

Lucas discovered that being told not to walk was significantly worse than being told not to fight. Fighting had alternatives. You could train mana, study equipment, argue with Owen, or annoy Sarah by asking medically specific questions until she threatened to sedate you with a rock. Walking was harder to replace.

For three days after the Stonehide Ravager encounter, Lucas’s world shrank to the medical shelter, the nearest fire pit, and whatever portion of Hearth he could reach without Sarah noticing the limp getting worse.

The thigh wound was the problem.

Megan’s healing had done enough to keep the torn flesh from becoming a disaster, but enough was not the same thing as fixed. The tusk had opened the outer side of his left thigh in a deep crescent. Sarah had cleaned it, closed what she could, wrapped it, checked it, unwrapped it, checked it again, and developed a personal relationship with the word no.

His shoulder hurt less each morning, and his ribs only complained when he laughed, which Owen treated as a professional challenge. His right palm had scabbed over and the side cut was mostly an inconvenience. The leg dictated everything, and Lucas hated that.

On the fourth morning, Sarah watched him walk from the medical shelter to Grant’s board and back.

No spear.

No pack.

No hurry.

Lucas made a point of not limping.

Sarah made a point of not being fooled.

“You’re compensating,” she said.

“I am walking.”

“You’re shortening the left stride.”

“Maybe my right leg is ambitious.”

“Lucas.”

He stopped.

Sarah folded her arms.

The morning sun had barely reached through the ashwood canopy. Hearth was awake behind them. Smoke, conversation, the knock of tools. Daniel’s crews had nearly finished replacing the section of northern barricade damaged by the fleeing grazer. Hunters were moving east and west now, exactly as Grant had ordered.

North remained empty.

Nobody wanted to meet the Ravager.

Good.

Lucas had no objection to learning from a near-death experience exactly once.

Sarah looked at the bandage under his pants.

“No fresh bleeding yesterday.”

“Correct.”

“No fever.”

“Correct.”

“Pain?”

“Three walking. One sitting.”

“After ten minutes?”

“Four.”

Sarah’s expression remained neutral.

Lucas knew he was negotiating.

She knew he knew.

“I want to go west.”

“No.”

“You don’t know where.”

“You said go.”

“Observe west.”

“That sentence has walking in it wearing a fake mustache.”

Lucas almost smiled.

Ribs.

Bad idea.

He continued. “The Sunken Road is less than two hours from the western watch line.”

“According to?”

“Owen.”

Sarah glanced past him.

Owen was standing thirty feet away pretending to study Grant’s board.

He immediately looked at the sky.

Traitor.

Lucas said, “The route is established. No northern territory. No hunting. No deliberate monster engagement.”

“No.”

“Daniel is going.”

Sarah’s eyes narrowed.

That was new information.

Lucas had saved it.

Not because he was manipulative.

Because information had order.

“Why Daniel?”

“Because a road built before humanity is construction.”

“Everything becomes construction if Daniel looks at it long enough.”

“That is why he’s useful.”

Sarah sighed.

“How many?”

“Five.”

“Names.”

“Me. Owen. Daniel. Emily. Priya.”

“Combat?”

“Owen has Ranger. I have Mana Spearman.”

“You have a hole in your leg.”

“Technically less of one now.”

Sarah stared.

Lucas surrendered first.

“Sorry.”

“Good instinct.”

She looked toward the west.

The Sunken Road had become the most irritating rumor in Hearth over the last two days.

A scouting pair had found it weeks ago, before the settlement had enough people or food to care about ancient stone. They had reported a strip of fitted rock crossing the western forest, then everyone had promptly become occupied with not starving.

More recent western hunting parties had crossed it twice.

Straight.

Too straight.

No one had followed it far.

Lucas had been thinking about it since Chapter One of his new life.

Not because old roads were automatically important.

Because this one should not exist.

The Hollow Reach was a Trial Realm.

Humanity had arrived through the Accession.

Yet somebody had built infrastructure here before them.

That changed the shape of the place.

Trial Realm stopped sounding like empty wilderness with monsters.

It started sounding reused.

Sarah said, “You come back the moment the wound starts throbbing.”

Lucas nodded.

“You do not run.”

“Yes.”

“You do not fight unless the alternative is being eaten.”

“Yes.”

“No Kinetic Thrust.”

Lucas hesitated.

Sarah’s eyebrows rose.

“Yes.”

“No experimenting with whether a higher Vitality score makes you bleed slower.”

“I was not going to—”

“You were thinking about it.”

Lucas closed his mouth.

Fair.

She pointed at him.

“And somebody else carries the pack.”

Lucas nodded.

Sarah exhaled.

“Two hours out. Two hours back. You rest there. If Daniel tells me you ignored the leg, I will make him carry you home.”

Daniel’s voice came from behind Lucas.

“I’m comfortable with that.”

Lucas turned.

Daniel stood with a small pack over one shoulder and a coil of braided plant-fiber rope across his chest.

“You people coordinate too much.”

“It’s called community,” Daniel said.

“I dislike it.”

“Community dislikes you too.”

Owen finally stopped pretending not to listen.

“Beautiful. Can we go look at the impossible road now?”

Sarah pointed at all of them.

“Bring him back with the same number of holes.”

Emily frowned.

“Does the mouth count?”

“No,” Sarah said.

Owen nodded. “Excellent.”

Lucas picked up the Channelwood Spear.

Sarah looked at it.

He held up his free hand.

“Walking aid.”

“That is an F-Rank Uncommon weapon.”

“Multifunctional.”

“Do not stab the ground with it.”

Lucas looked offended.

“I have standards.”

Twenty minutes later, they left Hearth westward.

Slowly.

That part was non-negotiable.

Priya took point with Owen.

Daniel walked beside Lucas for the first mile, which Lucas suspected was surveillance disguised as conversation.

Emily carried a cloth-wrapped bundle of sample containers made from repurposed bottles, bark tubes, and one carefully cleaned metal lunch container someone had arrived with during the Accession.

Human civilization survived in strange objects.

The western forest differed subtly from the northern route.

The ashwoods grew wider apart.

Low ferns covered the ground in silver-green sheets.

Rock appeared more often between roots, dark and flat beneath moss.

Lucas watched for movement.

Habit.

Perception fourteen helped.

So did terror nine levels tall and covered in stone plates.

Every broken branch looked more informative now.

Every patch of silence deserved attention.

They encountered nothing larger than a Needle Rat.

The creature watched them from a fallen trunk.

Identify.

Needle Rat

Beast — Lv. 2

F-Rank

It squeaked.

Lucas kept walking.

Owen looked back.

“Progression opportunity.”

Lucas raised one finger.

Owen smiled.

The joke worked because neither of them meant it anymore.

Two days earlier, Lucas might have thought about whether an easy kill was worth the time.

After the Ravager, the question felt embarrassing.

They crossed the western watch line.

Hearth’s signs disappeared behind them.

From there, Priya followed faded marks cut into ashwood trunks by earlier scouts.

A slash.

Two slashes.

A triangle where the route turned south around a marshy patch.

Human markings.

Recent.

Crude.

Useful.

Lucas noticed something about fifteen minutes later.

“Stop.”

Everyone stopped.

Sarah’s rules had apparently made the group jumpy.

Lucas pointed at the ground.

A dark stone sat beneath leaves.

Rectangular.

Daniel crouched.

He brushed away moss.

Then more.

The exposed surface widened.

Three feet.

Five.

Eight.

Owen helped clear leaves with his boot.

A second stone appeared beside the first.

Then a third.

Fitted.

Lucas’s heartbeat changed.

Daniel traced the seam between two slabs.

“Not natural.”

Emily crouched on the other side.

“You’re sure?”

Daniel looked up.

“Yes.”

“How sure?”

“Ninety-nine percent.”

Owen said, “What’s the one?”

“Nature is weird.”

Lucas liked Daniel.

They cleared enough ground to reveal two parallel edges.

The stones ran east-west.

Mostly buried.

No obvious mortar.

Each slab was roughly rectangular but not identical. Their shapes interlocked irregularly, like somebody had cut them to fit available material instead of forcing uniform blocks.

Lucas touched one.

Cold.

Identify.

Weathered Roadstone

F-Rank Material

Worked stone showing extensive environmental exposure.

Additional identification unavailable.

He read it twice.

Worked stone.

Not speculation.

System-confirmed.

Emily received the same result.

Her expression sharpened.

“Well.”

Owen looked at Lucas.

“You’re doing the face.”

“This seems like a reasonable face event.”

They followed the buried stones.

Within fifty yards, the forest floor began to slope down.

The road emerged with it.

At first only edges.

Then full slabs.

Then a complete strip nearly twelve feet wide descending into a shallow cut between two ridges.

Sunken Road.

The name suddenly made sense.

Not because the road had sunk.

Because whoever built it had cut the route down into the land.

Banks rose five to eight feet on either side.

Roots hung from packed earth.

Ashwoods leaned above the cut like spectators.

The road itself remained unnaturally level.

Lucas stopped at the top.

The place felt wrong.

Not dangerous.

Ordered.

Everything else in the Hollow Reach bent around terrain.

Streams curved.

Game trails wandered.

Trees grew wherever they managed.

The road rejected all of that.

It went straight.

Daniel walked ahead ten yards.

His expression had changed.

No jokes.

He knelt near the edge and pulled moss away from a narrow groove running parallel to the road.

“Drainage.”

Emily moved beside him.

“Definitely?”

Daniel pointed uphill.

“Water comes down the bank. This catches it before it washes across the road.”

The groove led toward a rectangular opening beneath the stone shoulder.

A culvert.

Lucas stared.

Not paving.

Infrastructure.

Drainage.

Grading.

Load management.

Somebody had expected this road to last.

And it had.

How long?

No way to know.

Long enough for mature ashwoods to grow above parts of it.

Long enough for soil to bury sections.

Long enough for the builders to be gone.

Priya stood in the center.

“Human?”

Nobody answered.

The dimensions were human-compatible.

That meant almost nothing.

Lucas had already seen monsters whose bodies ignored Earth’s familiar proportions.

Why would people be different?

Emily studied the stone.

“No tool marks.”

Daniel nodded.

“Not normal ones.”

Lucas walked carefully downhill.

His thigh pulled with every third step.

He shortened the stride.

Daniel noticed.

Lucas pretended he had not.

The road descended another hundred yards and leveled.

There, they found the first thing that made “old road” become something larger.

A post stood at the right shoulder.

Gray-black stone.

Six feet tall.

Square at the base.

Tapering slightly toward the top.

Half covered in pale lichen.

Owen circled it.

“Marker?”

Daniel wiped the face with a cloth.

Lines appeared.

Not cracks.

Carving.

Lucas moved closer.

The marks were shallow but deliberate.

Three vertical grooves.

A hooked shape crossing them.

Below that, a series of angular symbols arranged in two neat rows.

Writing.

Or something close enough.

Lucas used Identify.

Road Marker

F-Rank Construct

Purpose: Wayfinding / Boundary Reference

Language: Unrecognized

Condition: Heavily Weathered

Lucas went still.

Construct.

Language.

Unrecognized.

He repeated the identification aloud.

Emily immediately tried her own.

Same result.

Priya touched the carved symbols without pressing.

“Not human.”

“We don’t know that,” Daniel said.

She looked at him.

Daniel nodded toward the stone.

“We know the System doesn’t recognize the language for us. Humans had thousands of languages before this. Could be one none of us knows.”

Lucas shook his head slowly.

“That is technically possible.”

Owen smiled.

“You said technically.”

“Yes.”

“So you think alien road.”

Lucas looked along the perfectly graded stone disappearing between trees.

“I think civilization.”

That word landed differently.

Civilization meant planning beyond one person.

Labor.

Standards.

Knowledge passed between builders.

Travel important enough to justify a road.

Destinations.

The Hollow Reach had not merely contained someone before humanity.

Someone had moved through it.

Regularly.

Emily said, “Can Identify date it?”

“No.”

“Material?”

“Roadstone.”

“Which is impressively useless.”

Daniel inspected the marker base.

“It was replaced.”

Lucas looked over.

“What?”

“The post.”

Daniel pointed at the joints.

The paving stones around the marker were different.

Slightly darker.

Smaller pieces.

“Someone opened the road here after the original surface was laid. See the patch?”

Lucas crouched as much as the thigh allowed.

Pain.

He stopped halfway.

Daniel gave him a look.

Lucas remained standing.

Fine.

From above, he could see it.

The main slabs interlocked broadly.

Around the marker, narrower cut stones formed a rough rectangle.

Repair.

Modification.

Another layer of intent.

“Could have been installed later,” Emily said.

“Exactly.”

“Meaning the road existed long enough to get upgraded.”

“Or the marker broke.”

“Still means maintenance.”

Owen looked down the road.

“So where did it go?”

Nobody answered.

They continued west.

Slower now.

Lucas forgot the leg for nearly five minutes.

Then the leg reminded him.

Hard.

A deep throb spread from the wound up toward his hip.

He stopped.

Not because Sarah had said to.

Because the Ravager had taught him one useful lesson about warnings.

Ignoring them did not make you brave.

It made the next decision worse.

“Break,” Lucas said.

Owen looked surprised.

Then suspicious.

“You voluntarily said that.”

“I am evolving.”

“Terrifying.”

They sat beside the road.

Daniel checked the bandage without removing it.

No fresh blood visible.

Lucas drank.

Emily took tiny samples of moss from the paving joints, then a scraping of mineral deposit from the drainage channel.

Priya sketched the marker symbols on a piece of flattened bark using charcoal.

Lucas watched.

“Good?”

She held it up.

The hooked central symbol looked like a bent spear crossing rain.

“Enough to compare if we find another.”

Lucas looked at the marker.

Another.

That assumption mattered.

A road marker implied a system of markers.

Wayfinding implied multiple destinations.

Boundary reference implied boundaries worth marking.

He opened Notifications.

Nothing.

No achievement.

No discovery reward.

He was oddly pleased.

The System was not going to applaud every time he noticed something old.

This was real whether it rewarded him or not.

After fifteen minutes, they continued.

West.

The road curved.

Lucas stopped again.

Not because of pain.

The road curved.

It was the first bend they had seen.

Gentle.

Intentional.

And completely unnecessary based on the terrain.

The ground ahead was flat.

No rock outcrop.

No ravine.

No giant tree old enough to predate construction.

Yet the road angled northwest.

Daniel stared.

“What would make you do that?”

“Destination,” Priya said.

Owen pointed through the trees.

“To what?”

They moved to the outside of the curve.

There, almost buried under fern growth, stood a second marker.

Broken.

Only the bottom three feet remained.

The upper portion lay several yards away.

Lucas examined the surviving face.

Different symbols.

Same style.

Identify returned:

Road Junction Marker

F-Rank Construct

Purpose: Wayfinding

Language: Unrecognized

Condition: Broken / Heavily Weathered

Junction.

Lucas looked at the road.

“One road?”

Daniel had already begun clearing brush from the inside of the curve.

“No.”

They helped.

Five minutes later, another line of stones emerged.

Narrower.

Eight feet wide.

It split from the main road and climbed northwestern ground.

A branch.

Almost entirely buried.

Lucas’s pulse accelerated.

Owen watched him.

“No.”

Lucas looked at him.

“You don’t know what I was going to say.”

“Yes, I do. You were going to say we should follow it.”

“We should.”

“No.”

“You sound like Sarah.”

“That is the most hurtful thing you have ever said to me.”

Daniel straightened.

“He’s right.”

Lucas stared.

Daniel pointed at Lucas’s leg.

“You’re done.”

“I am currently standing.”

“Poorly.”

Lucas looked at Priya for support.

She examined the branch road.

Then him.

“Tomorrow.”

“Traitors.”

Emily said, “We also need the light.”

The canopy made the afternoon seem later than it was, but she was right.

The journey back would take longer because Lucas had already used most of what Sarah would call his walking allowance.

Lucas studied the junction.

The main Sunken Road continued west.

The branch climbed northwest.

Toward higher ground.

Nothing visible beyond trees.

No ruins.

No building.

Nothing visible beyond the trees. That would have to be enough for one injured afternoon.

Lucas limped to the broken marker.

The fallen upper half lay face-down.

He looked at Daniel.

“Help me turn it?”

Daniel considered the size.

“No.”

“That was fast.”

“It weighs several hundred pounds.”

“Kinetic Thrust.”

Four people said, “No.”

Lucas sighed.

Community.

They used branches as levers instead.

Daniel found a stable pivot stone.

Owen and Priya pulled the rope.

Emily watched the placement and moved fingers clear.

Lucas provided moral support, which everyone agreed was his safest current specialization.

The stone shifted.

Rolled once.

Stopped face-up.

More symbols covered the broken surface.

And one carving.

Not writing.

A shape.

A long horizontal line intersected by a smaller branch.

At the end of the branch was a square.

Inside the square, three short vertical marks.

A map.

Crude.

Stylized.

But a map.

Owen crouched.

“Road. Branch.”

Priya pointed to the square.

“And that?”

Lucas felt the answer arrive before he allowed himself to say it.

A destination.

Not necessarily a building.

Not necessarily ruins.

But something deliberately represented.

The main horizontal line continued past the branch.

Another smaller symbol sat farther west.

Two destinations.

Maybe more.

Daniel traced the grooves in the air.

“This is wayfinding.”

Identify did not update.

It did not need to.

Emily looked at Lucas.

“If the square is something built…”

“We don’t know.”

“You’re learning.”

“I nearly died yesterday.”

“Effective teacher.”

Lucas looked uphill along the buried branch road.

Trees.

Fern.

Stone.

Nothing else.

Yet.

He memorized the angle.

Priya added the symbol to her bark sketch.

Then Lucas noticed one detail.

The three vertical marks inside the square matched the three vertical grooves from the first intact marker.

Not exactly.

Close enough to bother him.

He pointed.

Priya compared the sketches.

“You’re right.”

Daniel frowned.

“Place name?”

“Maybe.”

Owen said, “Boundary?”

“Maybe.”

Emily smiled.

“Ancient alien gas station.”

Lucas looked at her.

She shrugged.

“Someone needed to say it.”

They took rubbings of the clearest symbols using charcoal and cloth.

No one removed the marker.

No one chipped stone.

Lucas approved.

Information first.

Looting later, preferably never unless there was a reason.

The return trip began with sunlight still above the trees.

It became unpleasant quickly.

Lucas’s thigh stiffened.

The shoulder started aching because he used the Channelwood Spear too much as a support despite promising not to stab the ground.

He did not stab it.

He placed the butt cap carefully.

Technical compliance.

Daniel noticed anyway.

He said nothing.

Which was worse.

Halfway back, they heard something moving in the brush.

Everyone stopped.

Lucas’s hand tightened on the spear.

Pain ran through the palm.

Owen moved ahead quietly.

Thirty seconds.

A minute.

He returned.

“Two grazers.”

Lucas exhaled.

“Levels?”

“Three and four.”

“Direction?”

“South.”

Daniel looked at Lucas.

“No.”

“I said nothing.”

“You were thinking.”

“This is becoming oppressive.”

They let the grazers pass.

No fight.

No progression.

No regret.

By the time Hearth’s western barricade appeared, Lucas’s leg hurt enough that the last hundred yards became a test of pride.

He failed it voluntarily.

“Daniel.”

Daniel looked over.

“I need the shoulder.”

Daniel did not smile.

That kindness almost annoyed Lucas.

He let Lucas put an arm across his shoulders and took enough weight to reduce the limp.

They entered Hearth that way.

Sarah stood at the gate.

Of course.

She looked at Lucas.

Then Daniel.

Then the spear.

Then Lucas’s leg.

“How far?”

Lucas considered lying.

Daniel said, “Four hours total walking.”

Lucas glared.

Sarah’s expression became terrifyingly calm.

“With breaks,” Daniel added.

Coward.

Sarah pointed toward medical.

Lucas went.

The bandage had not reopened.

Good.

The swelling had increased.

Bad.

Sarah made her opinion known with very few words.

Lucas sat through it.

Worth it.

After she rewrapped the leg, Grant arrived with Owen, Priya’s bark sketches, Daniel’s notes, and Emily’s samples.

The medical shelter became an accidental planning room.

Grant studied the copied symbols.

“System says language?”

“Unrecognized,” Lucas said.

“And construct?”

“Road Marker. Then Road Junction Marker.”

Grant’s eyes sharpened.

That mattered to him too.

Not old rocks.

System-recognized constructed infrastructure.

Daniel explained the drainage, grading, patchwork around the marker, and branch route.

Emily explained that she had no useful dating method and refused to pretend otherwise.

Priya laid out the junction sketch.

Grant stared at the square.

“You think something is there.”

Lucas leaned back against the support post.

“I think whoever made the marker wanted travelers to associate that branch with whatever that symbol means.”

“That was almost cautious.”

“I’m injured. It’s affecting my personality.”

Grant ignored him.

“How far did you follow the branch?”

“We didn’t.”

Grant looked surprised.

Owen said, “I would like that officially recorded.”

Sarah, from across the shelter, said, “I already have witnesses.”

Lucas rubbed his face.

Grant traced the drawn branch.

“Tomorrow?”

“No,” Sarah said.

Nobody argued.

Grant looked at Lucas.

“Day after?”

Sarah turned.

Grant revised.

“When medically appropriate.”

“Look at you learning,” Lucas said.

Grant smiled.

It disappeared quickly.

“If that road connects settlements, structures, old trial sites…”

“Or graves,” Emily said.

“Or graves.”

The room went quiet.

Lucas had not considered graves.

Civilization included those too.

People built roads because they had somewhere to go.

They marked boundaries because something lay on each side.

They repaired stone because they expected a future.

And eventually, apparently, they vanished.

What happened to them?

The Hollow Reach did not answer.

Lucas opened his interface once Grant left.

Progression.

Human — Lv. 3
Progress: 42%

Mana Spearman — Lv. 4
Progress: 8%

Profession: Not Acquired

Human progress had moved three percent.

Not Class.

Race.

Maybe from meaningful adaptation.

Maybe discovery.

Maybe surviving a day of walking with a hole in his leg was technically racial development.

He had no formula.

Good.

A notification waited.

DISCOVERY CONTRIBUTION RECORDED

You have participated in the verified identification and documentation of pre-existing constructed infrastructure within the Trial Realm.

Professional development conditions influenced.

Lucas stared.

Profession.

Not acquired.

No options.

No prompt.

Just conditions influenced.

He dismissed it slowly.

Whatever the System was building toward, it was not ready to name it.

He looked at his six unspent Attribute Points.

Still six.

Today had given him better data than the Ravager fight.

His leg had limited him before his stamina did.

Vitality would likely help recovery and resilience.

Endurance would help sustained travel.

Wisdom continued to matter for mana control.

Perception had already proved valuable.

There were arguments everywhere.

Lucas did not spend anything.

Not yet.

He wanted one more clean day.

One more comparison between what his body lacked and what pain merely exaggerated.

Iterative Insight approved of nothing.

It did not have opinions.

But when Lucas reviewed the walk, the difference between fatigue and injury felt clearer than it would have days ago.

His breathing had remained manageable.

His general stamina had been fine.

The leg had failed first.

That mattered.

It was evidence.

Not a command.

Outside, evening settled over Hearth.

Owen leaned into the shelter.

“Good news.”

“What?”

“Priya improved the map.”

Lucas sat straighter.

Sarah looked over.

He sat less straight.

Owen held up a larger piece of bark.

The Sunken Road ran west.

The branch angled northwest.

The square symbol waited at the end of an unfinished line.

Priya had written one word beneath it in charcoal.

UNKNOWN.

Lucas smiled.

Perfect.

Not ruin.

Not dungeon.

Not city.

Unknown.

Tomorrow he would heal.

The day after, if Sarah permitted it, they would follow the branch far enough to replace that word with something better.

The road beneath the Hollow Reach had already proven one thing.

Humanity was not the first civilization to walk this forest.

Now Lucas wanted to know who had.
