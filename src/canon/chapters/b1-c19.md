# Chapter 19
## The Cost Below

The second descent began with better preparation and worse expectations. Lucas considered that progress.

The eight-person Party stood at Stonefield's quarry breach just after sunrise. Lucas. Owen. Marcus. Tessa. Emily. Rachel. Daniel. Megan. Eight names in the Party interface. Eight people breathing cold air from the Glassroot Depths.

Behind them, Stonefield's support team had doubled. Two stretchers instead of one. More rope. More torches. More water. No one commented on the extra stretcher. That made it worse.

Rachel checked the knot on her pack. "Thomas wants us out before dark."

Marcus glanced toward the breach. "Grant wants the same thing."

Owen sighed. "Finally, inter-settlement cooperation."

Daniel handed him a coil of rope. "Carry this."

"Cooperation ruined."

Lucas checked his thigh. Pain one. No swelling yet. His Race level had improved his baseline resilience without erasing what the Ravager had done. He could feel the difference now—recovery happened faster, fatigue arrived later. The scar still existed. Good. Consequences should have memory.

Sarah had cleared him with the same expression she used when authorizing something she disliked but could not reasonably forbid. Her instructions had been short. "If the leg changes, you leave." Not "slow down." Not "consider leaving." Leave. Lucas had agreed. That probably worried her more than an argument would have.

The Party entered. The Glassroot threshold chamber met them with pale blue light and no visible monsters. Lucas disliked the word "welcomed." Nothing here had shown hospitality.

Formal dungeon participation resumed as they crossed beyond the threshold. Field Record rebuilt the known route around him: left shelf, first Skitterer fight site, Dungeon Conduit Channel, Transit Arch, elevated mana corridor, defended basin. Everything beyond that remained blank. Today, the blank was the point.

Marcus led with shield raised. Lucas kept the Channelwood Spear angled ahead. Owen and Tessa watched ceiling and rear. Rachel moved just behind Lucas. Daniel carried the spare shafts and most of the climbing hardware. Emily looked at every Glassroot formation like she was trying to marry it academically. Megan watched the people.

It took forty minutes to reach the site of the first fight. No corpses. Only dark stains and a few broken carapace fragments. Emily crouched. "Less than yesterday."

Lucas looked. She was right. Several fragments they had deliberately left had disappeared. No drag marks. No obvious scavenger prints. Glassroot veins near the stain pulsed faintly.

Owen looked toward the wall. "Still voting dungeon eats bugs."

"No evidence," Emily said.

"Emotionally, I have evidence."

They moved on. The transit arch remained unchanged. The three-line-like square sat above the descending route. Lucas stopped long enough to inspect it again. Identify returned the same result.

Dungeon Transit Arch

F-Rank Construct

Condition: Active / Damaged

Language markers detected.

Language: Unrecognized.

No translation. No new revelation because he had slept on it. Excellent.

They continued toward the defended basin. The mana density increased. Lucas noticed his regeneration improving again—not dramatically, but enough to matter across hours. The Glassroot network also felt busier today. More pulses. Some weak. Some directional. Iterative Insight helped him separate them: fast pulse, slow background flow, another fast pulse, two directions. No meaning attached. Lucas had become better at noticing the grammar of a language he could not read. That was dangerous if he started pretending patterns were definitions. He did not.

At the edge of the basin, the group stopped. Six Skitterers had been visible yesterday. Today, three. The bones remained. The shells remained. The larger shape did not show itself.

Marcus whispered, "Better."

Lucas looked at the Glassroot. "Maybe."

Marcus exhaled. "There it is."

Owen grinned.

The basin's roots pulsed. Three Skitterers turned toward them. One Lv.4. Two Lv.5. No immediate charge.

Rachel pointed left. A narrow ledge circled above part of the basin. "If we don't go through them?"

Owen studied it. "Possible."

Daniel looked at the stone. "Single file."

Tessa said, "Bad if they climb."

Skitterers climbed. They had established that.

Lucas watched the creatures. Their attention seemed fixed on the group, but they remained inside the basin. Territorial boundary? Nest defense? Something else? He looked at the ledge. It began before the basin line and rejoined the route beyond it. Field Record knew only the first ten feet. Unknown after that.

"Test distance," Lucas said.

Marcus nodded. They backed away first. The Skitterers relaxed slightly. Then Owen moved left toward the ledge while everyone else remained still. The nearest Skitterer pivoted. No pursuit. Owen moved farther. Still none.

"Route?" Rachel whispered.

"Maybe."

Lucas hated that Marcus smiled.

They used the ledge. Marcus first. Then Lucas. Tessa. Emily. Megan. Daniel. Rachel. Owen last. The path narrowed to barely two feet over part of the basin. Lucas kept his body toward the wall. No rush. No heroics. Below them, the Skitterers tracked their movement. One clicked. Glassroot pulsed. Another clicked. A second pulse answered deeper in the dungeon. Lucas felt his skin tighten. Communication still unconfirmed. But whatever the pulses did, the network was responding.

Halfway across, the larger shape appeared. It moved from behind the central roots. Lucas stopped. Identify. Failed. Obscured. It stepped forward. More of it emerged. Broad body. Eight legs. Not six. A rounded front shell split by a vertical seam. Long hooked forelimbs folded against its body. The back rose almost to Marcus's chest.

Emily whispered, "That is not a Skitterer."

The creature's head moved. Its front shell opened slightly. Inside, pale blue light. Identify.

Glassroot Broodguard

Beast — Lv. 9

F-Rank

Dungeon-adapted guardian organism.

Additional information unavailable.

Lucas felt his stomach drop. Level nine. Again. The Ravager had been level nine. His body remembered before his mind finished reading. Scar tissue tightened. Breath shortened. Not panic. Memory. Iterative Insight did not soothe him. It simply made the comparison obvious. Different creature. Different terrain. Different team. Same mistake available if fear became aggression.

The Broodguard looked up. Its forelimbs unfolded.

Tessa whispered, "Keep moving."

Correct. They were already on the ledge. Retreat would require reversing single file. Forward was closer.

Marcus moved. The Broodguard clicked. The sound was deeper than the Skitterers. Every Glassroot strand around the basin flashed. The three Skitterers surged toward the wall. They began climbing.

"Move," Marcus said.

The Party moved. Not running. Almost. Lucas reached the far end. Tessa behind him. Emily. Megan. Daniel. Rachel. Owen. Marcus remained last now, shield between the climbing Skitterers and the group. One Skitterer reached the ledge. Marcus slammed his shield into it. The creature fell. Another climbed around. Tessa fired. Arrow into a leg joint. It dropped.

The Broodguard did not climb. It moved to the wall and struck a Glassroot trunk with one hooked limb. The dungeon pulsed. The ledge shook. Not from the strike. From beneath.

Daniel shouted, "Go!"

The stone cracked behind Owen. A section of ledge collapsed. Owen jumped. Rachel grabbed his pack strap and pulled. Marcus leaped last. The ledge fell away behind them in a rain of stone. The Skitterers vanished with it. The Broodguard remained below. Watching. No pursuit.

Lucas breathed. "Everyone?"

Megan answered first. "Eight."

Rachel laughed once. Nervous. "We keep saying that."

"Keep saying it," Daniel replied.

The route beyond the basin descended. There was no ledge back. Not anymore. Lucas looked at the broken gap. Ten feet across at the narrowest point. Possible with rope. Possible later. Their primary retreat path had changed. That mattered more than the discovery.

"Turn back?" Megan asked.

Marcus looked at the gap. "We can rig rope."

Daniel nodded. "Yes."

Lucas checked time. They had gone farther than yesterday but were still well inside the turn-back window. The basin was now behind them. The direct route back required a rope traverse above a Lv.9 Broodguard. That was terrible. A second retreat route mattered. Field Record held one option—the unexplored transit arch branch they had passed before the basin. Except it was behind them too.

"No," Lucas said. Everyone looked at him. "We don't go deeper until we know we can get back."

Daniel nodded. They rigged the rope. Two lines. One anchored around a Glassroot-thickened stone pillar. One around a worked support block. Daniel tested both. Marcus crossed first. The Broodguard moved below. No attack. Then Tessa. Emily. Megan. Rachel. Owen. Daniel. Lucas last. His thigh burned as he braced against the rope. The old wound held.

Halfway over, the dungeon lights went out. Again. Darkness. The blue network vanished. Torches remained on the far side. Lucas hung above the basin in black. Someone shouted his name.

"I'm fine."

Then the pulse came. Huge. Not from below. From all directions. The rope anchor jerked. Lucas slammed against the wall. Pain exploded through his thigh. He nearly lost one hand. Mana Reinforcement. Low. Instant. He reinforced forearm, shoulder, grip. Not the whole body. Controlled. The anchor held. The lights returned. Below, the Broodguard was moving. Toward the wall.

"Lucas!" Marcus shouted.

Lucas climbed. Fast. Too fast. The Broodguard struck upward. Its hook missed his boot by inches. He rolled onto the ledge. Megan grabbed his arm.

"Leg?"

"Later."

"Now."

"Functional."

She hated that answer. So did he.

They moved. Retreat had become the mission. The clicking started behind them. More than three sources. Many. Field Record guided Lucas through the known corridor. Glassroot columns. Conduit channel. Transit arch. The pulses were faster now. The dungeon felt awake.

At the arch, Daniel stopped. "Wait." A Skitterer stood ahead on the shelf route. Lv.5. Another emerged behind it. Two more above. Their known retreat path had been occupied.

Marcus raised his shield. "We go through."

Lucas looked at the transit arch descending beside them. Unknown route. Unknown danger. Known route with four hostiles and more clicking behind. Not much of a choice.

"Arch."

Marcus turned. "You sure?"

"No."

Correct answer.

They descended. Formal path unknown. Field Record started fresh. Twelve stone steps. Left bend. Narrow corridor. Glassroot embedded under floor. The air became colder. Behind them, Skitterers clicked at the arch. None followed immediately. Interesting.

The corridor opened into a long chamber. Worked stone. More deliberate than the upper dungeon. Low rectangular platforms lined the walls. Each had shallow grooves filled with dead Glassroot.

Emily whispered, "Processing?"

"Maybe."

Lucas used Identify.

Dungeon Resource Station

F-Rank Construct

Condition: Inactive / Depleted

Function: Resource sorting and conditioning

That was actual information. Not enough to matter now. They kept moving. The chamber had two exits. Left climbed. Right descended.

Owen checked airflow. "Left."

"Why?" Marcus asked.

"Cool air down. Warmer up. Surface usually warmer."

Usually. Not certainty. Still useful. Left.

The passage climbed steeply. Then they heard shouting ahead. Human. Everyone froze.

Rachel looked shocked. "Support team?"

"No way," Daniel said.

Then the voice came again. "Help!"

Stonefield accent. Rachel moved first. "That's Evan."

She ran. Lucas swore. "Rachel!" Too late. The Party followed.

The passage opened into another quarry-like chamber. A vertical crack in the ceiling showed daylight. Not the main breach. A secondary fissure. Stonefield people had apparently found it from above while expanding perimeter checks. Two support scouts stood on a shelf near the crack. One was bleeding from the head. The other pointed down. "Evan fell!"

Rachel reached the edge. Below, a man lay on broken stone. Alive. Barely. Megan moved. Daniel stopped her. "Route?"

A sloped path reached the lower floor. Rachel was already descending. Lucas felt the Glassroot pulse. One. Two. Three. Too fast.

"Rachel, stop."

She looked back. Then the floor moved. Not floor. Shells. Skitterers emerged from shallow pits around the fallen man. Five. Six. Eight. The support scouts screamed. Rachel was halfway down.

"Back!" Lucas shouted.

She turned. A Skitterer dropped from the wall behind her. Lv.6. Higher than any they had fought. Rachel swung her spear. The creature knocked it aside. Its hooked limb punched into her upper chest. The sound was small. That was the worst part. Rachel looked down. The hook came out red.

Megan moved. Marcus caught her. "No."

"She's alive!"

Rachel fell to one knee. Lucas acted. Kinetic Thrust. Mana loaded. Timing. He had failed early yesterday. Try. Observe. Change. The Skitterer turned. Lucas aimed not at shell. Leg joint. He thrust. Pulse. Closer. Not perfect. The spear accelerated at the correct half-beat. The point smashed through the upper joint. The Skitterer collapsed sideways. Mana Spearman progress flared somewhere he ignored.

Rachel tried to stand. Another Skitterer reached her. Tessa shot it through an eye cluster. Owen hurled a spear. Marcus charged down three steps. Megan followed. Lucas grabbed her shoulder.

"We can't hold eight."

"We can't leave her!"

Rachel looked up. Blood at her mouth. She understood before Megan did. "Don't."

Megan froze. Rachel's chest moved wrong. The wound was too deep. Too central. Limited healing could slow bleeding. Not repair what had been destroyed. Megan knew it. Her face changed.

Rachel said, "Get Evan."

Then the Skitterers surged. Marcus retreated. Tessa fired again. Daniel dragged Megan backward. Lucas saw Rachel one final time. She pulled her utility knife. Not toward herself. Toward the Skitterer climbing over the dead one. Defiant. Then bodies blocked her from view.

The Party ran. Seven. The number entered Lucas's mind like a wound. Seven.

They reached the upper shelf. The two support scouts were panicking. Daniel seized one by the shirt. "Where does the crack exit?"

"Quarry north edge. Rope above."

"Go."

Megan pointed down. "Evan."

Lucas looked. The fallen man still moved. Eight Skitterers between them and him. Rachel gone. No. Not gone. Dead. Probably. The distinction hurt.

Marcus looked at Lucas. One second. Decision. "We cannot."

Lucas hated him for being right. The support scout who had called Evan's name sobbed. "We can't leave him."

Owen said, "We don't have a path."

The Glassroot flashed. More clicking. The Broodguard's deeper call echoed somewhere behind. Retreat collapsing. Lucas grabbed the scout. "If we go down, more people die."

The words felt disgusting. Necessary.

They climbed toward the fissure. The crack narrowed. Daniel went first to secure rope. Tessa. Emily. The injured support scout. Megan. Owen. Marcus. Lucas. Seven Party members. Two support scouts. Below, Skitterers reached Evan. Lucas did not look long. He looked anyway.

Permanent death had never been theoretical. Today it had names. Rachel Moore. Evan, whose last name Lucas did not know. That felt like a failure of its own.

The fissure exit was ugly. Rope hauled them through one at a time. Stone scraped Lucas's shoulders. His thigh throbbed. No one cared. They emerged fifty yards north of the quarry breach. Stonefield workers surrounded them. Thomas was there. He counted. His face changed.

"Where's Rachel?"

No one answered quickly enough. Thomas already knew. Megan sat on the ground. Blood covered her hands. Not hers. She had never reached Rachel. That was somehow worse.

Thomas looked at Lucas. "Where?"

Lucas forced himself to answer. "Secondary chamber below this fissure. Skitterer swarm. She was hit in the chest. We couldn't retrieve her."

"Dead?"

Lucas swallowed. "I believe so."

Thomas looked at the crack. Marcus stepped between him and it. "Don't."

Thomas's jaw tightened. "My people are down there."

"Yes."

"You left them."

Marcus did not flinch. "Yes."

The word almost started a fight. Daniel stepped in. "If we go in now, we add bodies."

Thomas looked at him. Then at Megan. Then at the seven-person Party. The math won. Not emotionally. Practically. It was the ugliest kind of victory.

They closed the secondary fissure with a guard line. No one entered. The main breach remained guarded too. The dungeon had now produced alternate exits. That changed everything.

Lucas opened his Party interface. Rachel Moore's name was gray. Status unavailable. Then, after several minutes, it disappeared. Party membership ended. No death notification. No dramatic announcement. Just absence. Seven names.

Lucas hated the System for being neutral. Then realized neutrality was exactly what he had always wanted from it. That did not make neutrality kind.

They returned to Hearth late. Nobody talked much. Sarah met them at the gate. She counted. Seven. Her face changed.

"Who?"

"Rachel."

Sarah closed her eyes for half a second. Then she became medical. Lucas's thigh first. Megan's hands. Marcus's shoulder bruise. Owen's scraped palms. Tessa's strained wrist. Daniel's bruised hip. Emily physically fine. Seven living people with manageable injuries. One dead. Possibly two if Evan had not already been dead when they left.

Grant listened to the report standing. No table. No jokes. No interruptions. Lucas described the basin crossing. The Broodguard. The collapse. The dungeon state fluctuation. Blocked retreat route. Transit arch. Resource station. Secondary fissure. Skitterer swarm. Rachel. Evan.

Grant asked one question at the end. "Did you break your own turn-back rule?"

Lucas thought. "No."

That hurt too. They had not been reckless by their standards. The dungeon had changed. Retreat had changed. A support team had opened another access. Good decisions had still produced death. That was important. Danger did not require stupidity.

Grant looked at Priya's map. "We close both entrances."

Thomas had sent the same message through the returning support scouts. Not forever. For now. Until they understood the alternate passages and could prevent people from wandering into new breaches. Lucas agreed. No argument.

Progression waited in his interface. He had almost forgotten.

Human — Lv.5

Progress: 9%

Mana Spearman — Lv.5

Progress: 28%

Explorer — Lv.4

Progress: 16%

Three levels crossed. Not from Rachel dying. The System did not reward tragedy. From combat. Danger. Route adaptation. Dungeon discovery. Retreat. Survival. The distinction mattered to Lucas.

Human Lv.5 automatic growth:

+1 Vitality

+1 Endurance

Mana Spearman Lv.5 automatic growth:

+1 Agility

+1 Intelligence

+1 Wisdom

Free Class Attribute Points gained:

+2

Explorer Lv.4 automatic growth:

+1 Perception

+1 Endurance

Free Profession Attribute Point gained:

+1

Available Attribute Points:

10

Lucas stared at the ten. He had been saving them because each situation argued for something different. Today had argued for movement. Control. Durability. Awareness. Everything. Still not clean. But one thing had become obvious. His speed had not been enough to reach Rachel. That did not mean Agility would have saved her. He refused that lie. The formation, distance, swarm, terrain, and injury had mattered more. Dumping points into Agility because grief wanted a lever would be fear pretending to be strategy. He spent nothing. Not tonight.

Marcus sat beside him. For once, no sarcasm. "We could've gone back."

Lucas knew which moment he meant. After the ledge collapse. "We did."

"I mean before crossing."

"Yes."

Marcus stared at the fire. "We could've stayed out entirely."

"Yes."

"Helpful."

Lucas looked at him. "Do you want me to tell you there was a correct answer?"

Marcus did not answer.

"There wasn't," Lucas said. "We made the route safer than yesterday. We had support. We had rules. We retreated when the route changed. Then the dungeon changed again."

"Rachel still died."

"Yes."

Marcus's jaw tightened. Lucas did not soften the word. Death deserved accuracy.

Across the fire, Megan sat alone. Sarah eventually joined her. No healing Skill could fix that conversation.

Priya's new map stopped at the secondary fissure. A heavy black mark crossed both dungeon entrances. CLOSED PENDING REVIEW. Below it, she had written two names. RACHEL MOORE. EVAN PARKER. Someone had supplied the last name. Lucas was glad. The map should remember.

Field Record did too. Not the people as icons. The route. The place. Where Rachel had fallen. Where they had turned away from Evan. Lucas wished the Skill could forget. Instead, it did exactly what it was meant to do. It kept what he had observed. The Hollow Reach was teaching him that knowledge had weight.

Tomorrow they would argue about whether to reopen the Glassroot Depths. They would study the pulses. They would reconsider support teams. They would find a better way to mark alternate exits. They would learn. That was what survival required. But tonight, eight had gone in. Seven had come back. And for the first time since the Accession, Lucas understood that a good plan could still end with an empty place beside the fire.

The next morning made everything worse by being ordinary. Hearth woke. People ate. Work crews formed. Someone cursed because a cooking pot cracked. Two hunters argued over arrows. The world continued.

Sarah checked Lucas's thigh. The old Ravager wound had swollen again, but the tissue remained closed. "Two days," she said.

Lucas looked at her. "Two quiet days?"

"Yes."

"That seems excessive."

Sarah waited. Lucas exhaled. "Two days."

At midday, Thomas Reed arrived from Stonefield with four people. Grant met him near the route board. Thomas asked for the dungeon team, and seven members gathered where eight had stood the day before. Thomas looked at the map. "Walk me through it again."

So they did. The basin. The collapsed ledge. The rope crossing. The blocked known route. The transit arch. The inactive resource station. The secondary chamber. The swarm. The fissure. Thomas asked practical questions. How wide was the alternate passage? How stable was the crack? Could creatures climb it? Had Glassroot appeared near the surface opening? Which routes had remained usable during the fluctuation? Nobody guessed when they did not know.

When the review ended, Thomas said, "The quarry stays closed."

Grant nodded. "For now."

"For now."

Daniel pointed at the north side of Priya's map. "We need surface sweeps for more fissures."

Thomas agreed. "Stonefield first. Then farther out."

"Lowbank too," Lucas said. "If the system extends under the region."

Nobody liked that possibility. Which meant it belonged on the list. Priya added a new symbol to the shared map for possible dungeon-linked openings.

Owen studied it. "Less haunted turnip."

Priya stared at him. "You complained about the last symbol."

"I am providing continuity."

She threw a piece of charcoal at him. For half a second, the group laughed. The sound felt strange. Useful.

Later, Lucas reviewed the expedition without opening his Status. Rachel had pulled Owen across the collapsing ledge. She had argued for Stonefield when the hunting routes overlapped. She had volunteered to walk into an unknown dungeon because her settlement had found it. Those facts mattered more than a gray Party slot. Field Record remembered locations. Lucas remembered people. Different systems. Both necessary.

That evening, Priya left Rachel's name on the dungeon map beside the secondary chamber. No symbol beside it. No summary. Just the name.

Lucas opened Status once. Ten available Attribute Points. He left them unspent. Not because the dungeon had taught him nothing. Because the last twenty-four hours had taught him too much at once. Agility had mattered. Perception had mattered. Vitality had mattered. Endurance had mattered. Control had mattered. Route discipline had mattered more than any one number. He did not trust grief to choose a build. Tomorrow, maybe. After sleep. After the loudest part of the day stopped trying to turn every regret into an Attribute. He closed the interface.

Across Hearth, Marcus repaired his shield. Tessa restrung her bow. Daniel rebuilt the rope kit. Megan sat with Sarah. Owen helped Priya redraw the dungeon route. The expedition had come back changed in ways the System did not quantify. Progression screens measured Lucas. They did not measure the empty place in the Party.

Before sleep, Lucas walked to the route board one last time. Priya had drawn a thick boundary around the Glassroot Depths access points. Not a wall. A warning. OPENINGS CLOSED PENDING REVIEW. Below it sat the routes they still trusted. Hearth to Stonefield. Stonefield to Lowbank. The Ironbound timber trail. The ordinary world around the dungeon.

Lucas realized he needed that reminder. The Glassroot Depths felt enormous because danger compressed attention. Every pulse, every corridor, every bad decision possibility filled the mind until nothing else seemed important. But settlements still needed food. Lowbank still had flood risk. The Ravager still lived north of Hearth. The Ridgeback Stalker still claimed fresh kills. The Sunken Road still continued west. The Waystation still had stairs descending into darkness. One dungeon had not become the world. It had simply become one more piece of it.

Lucas touched the edge of Priya's board. Tomorrow he would rest. After that, they would decide how to go back underground without pretending yesterday had been bad luck they could simply outgrow. A better route. Better fallback plans. Better understanding of Glassroot pulses. And, eventually, better movement. He could feel that last need clearly now. Not a Skill. Not yet. Just a problem. The dungeon had shown him where he was too slow. Chapter Twenty would have to answer it.

Lucas smiled without humor. One lesson at a time.
