# Chapter 28
## The Reach Of A Spear

Lucas discovered that a spear had two ranges.

The first was obvious.

He stood on the packed dirt outside Lowbank with the Channelwood Spear extended in both hands, point hovering over a chalk mark Daniel had scratched into a flat limestone slab.

Eight feet of wood, metal, hands, shoulders, stance.

Reach.

The second range existed only when he did something stupid.

“Again,” Megan said.

Lucas glanced at her.

She sat on an overturned fish basket with her arms folded. “Forward only.”

“I remember.”

“You remembered yesterday too. Then you asked whether forty-five degrees counted as forward.”

“It mostly does.”

“It mostly doesn’t.”

Owen, leaning against a willow, said, “I support the medical definition where directions are determined by how annoyed Megan gets.”

“That is the correct definition,” Megan said.

Lucas faced the chalk line again.

His right hip felt normal while standing. That was progress. The stiffness only appeared when he twisted hard or stepped too wide. Megan had cleared exactly one version of Mana Step: forward, flat ground, controlled intensity, no combat.

So Lucas set his left foot, chose a landing point six feet ahead, and gathered mana low through his legs.

Mana Step.

The impulse hit.

He moved.

Not vanished. Not blurred across impossible distance. His body simply accelerated through one committed step faster than muscle alone would have managed.

His lead foot struck the second chalk line.

His weight followed.

The Channelwood Spear stayed level.

His hip remained quiet.

Lucas stopped.

Megan pointed at him. “That was enough.”

“I only did one.”

“You did six before I got here.”

Lucas looked at Owen.

Owen raised both hands. “I’m medically transparent.”

“Traitor.”

“Alive traitor.”

Lucas let the mana settle.

The controlled step had felt cleaner than it had before the Broodmother fight. Some of that came from recovery. Some from Agility. Some from having finally learned that the Skill worked best when he decided where his body was going before he fed mana into the movement.

But it was still a movement Skill.

It did not solve the question bothering him.

He turned toward the western ridge.

Beyond Lowbank’s reeds and the closed section of old route, the limestone rose in pale shelves. Somewhere beyond the nearest fold lay the exposed Buried Conduit Casing they had found yesterday.

Environmental Resource Routing.

A function without a known resource.

A constructed channel with pale blue material inside.

And, twice now, illumination beneath ground that had occurred in the same broad window as pulses near Stonefield.

Lucas had spent most of the night thinking about distance.

Not the distance between Stonefield and Lowbank.

The distance between his spearpoint and everything beyond it.

Kinetic Thrust taught him to move mana through a spear-aligned attack.

Focused Mana Reinforcement taught him that mana could be concentrated along a chosen force path.

The Glassroot fragment had responded when he concentrated mana near it through the Channelwood Spear without touching it.

None of those facts meant he could throw mana.

They did mean he had reasons to test whether the spear’s useful path had to end at the metal point.

He lifted the weapon.

Owen saw his expression.

“No.”

Lucas frowned. “You don’t know what I’m thinking.”

“You’re staring at empty air like it owes you an explanation.”

“That is fair.”

Megan stood. “Whatever you’re about to do, does it involve the hip?”

“No.”

“Does it involve Mana Step?”

“No.”

“Does it involve making something explode?”

Lucas considered.

Megan closed her eyes.

“Not intentionally.”

“Wonderful.”

Daniel arrived before Lucas could begin, carrying three pieces of scrap wood and a bundle of rope.

Behind him came Marcus Reed and Nia Patel.

Marcus looked at the chalk lines. “You cleared?”

“For forward practice.”

“So not fighting.”

“No.”

Marcus nodded toward the western ridge. “Good. Because Claire wants another look at the conduit before anybody decides whether we can reroute carts north of it.”

Nia held up a folded sheet of Priya’s map. “And the western ground dropped another inch overnight.”

That ended practice.

Twenty minutes later, six of them headed west: Lucas, Owen, Marcus, Nia, Daniel, and Megan.

Priya stayed at Lowbank to update the shared route map from reports coming in from Stonefield. Tessa had gone east with a relay team. Claire had made it clear that Lowbank’s fish carts were not moving anywhere near the western sag until someone could explain what the ground intended to do.

Lucas appreciated that Claire phrased structural uncertainty as if the ground had bad intentions.

It felt accurate.

They followed the stony-rise detour, then left it where Nia had marked a new line of reeds with strips of white cloth.

The western landscape had changed subtly.

Yesterday the sag around the blue-lit void had been broad and shallow.

Today one edge had sharpened.

A crack ran through the dry soil for perhaps thirty feet, then disappeared beneath grass.

Hazard Recognition tugged at Lucas’s attention.

Not danger.

Evidence.

The crack widened toward the conduit site.

He crouched without stepping off the tested stone.

“Change.”

Everyone stopped.

Marcus scanned the ridge.

Owen looked behind them.

Daniel focused on the ground.

“What changed?” Nia asked.

“The crack has direction now.” Lucas pointed. “Yesterday it spread around the sag. Today the widest section trends northwest.”

Daniel crouched beside him.

“And the west lip is lower.”

Nia unfolded the map.

Lucas opened Field Record.

WESTERN VOID — DAY TWO

Known:

Perimeter slump increased.

Primary fracture widening northwest. Western lip lower than prior observation. Buried conduit lies near projected fracture path.

Unknown:

Whether ground movement follows conduit. Whether conduit damage follows ground movement. Whether either relates to Glassroot illumination.

They continued along the tested perimeter.

The Buried Conduit Casing emerged from the hillside exactly where they had left it, a strip of worked gray material under broken limestone.

No one had excavated it.

Good.

Someone had added stakes twenty feet around it.

Better.

Daniel checked the nearest stake.

“Moved.”

“How much?” Marcus asked.

“Two inches downhill.”

“Ground?”

“Probably.”

Lucas looked at him.

Daniel sighed. “Fine. The stake has moved two inches downhill. Cause unconfirmed.”

“Beautiful.”

“I hate what you’ve done to all of us.”

Nia laughed.

Then the conduit lit.

Blue-white light appeared inside the damaged seam.

No relay signal had arrived.

No one had touched it.

“Change,” Owen said immediately.

They backed away.

The light moved west.

Not quickly.

A pale line traveled through the visible seam and disappeared beneath stone.

Lucas counted seconds.

One.

Two.

Three.

The ground gave a faint tremor.

A limestone chip dropped from the exposed casing.

Megan said, “Farther.”

Nobody argued.

They retreated another twenty feet.

A red cloth appeared on the distant ridge behind them.

Relay.

Nia squinted. “Stonefield strong pulse.”

Lucas checked the timing.

The local conduit had lit before they received the human relay.

That meant almost nothing about actual sequence because the relay itself took time.

Still worth recording.

He did.

Marcus stared at the exposed seam.

“If that thing runs under the route, we need to know where.”

“Yes,” Lucas said.

“And if it runs toward Hearth?”

“We need to know that too.”

Marcus glanced at him. “That sounded suspiciously like agreement.”

“Don’t get used to it.”

Daniel moved north along the exclusion edge, testing the ground with a long pole.

Ten minutes later he called them over.

Worked stone showed beneath a thin layer of soil.

Not conduit casing this time.

A rectangular capstone.

Identify gave Lucas only:

CONDUIT ACCESS PLATE

F-Rank Construct Condition: Jammed / Damaged Function: Maintenance Access

Marcus whistled softly.

“That’s useful.”

“Potentially,” Lucas said.

Daniel pointed his probe at him. “We are not opening it.”

“I didn’t say we should.”

“You made the face.”

“What face?”

“The one Owen warned me about.”

Owen nodded solemnly. “Empty air owes him answers.”

They marked the plate and continued west.

The route climbed.

For the first time, the terrain gave them a clear view beyond the ridge.

A shallow valley stretched northwest, broken by limestone teeth and low brush. Near its center, a line of unusually straight depressions crossed the grass.

Lucas stopped.

Field Record had no match.

New ground.

Explorer responded to that awareness with a faint sense of professional attention, not direction. The Profession did not tell him where to go. It made the act of observing matter more sharply.

Nia pointed. “Those dips are too regular.”

Daniel agreed.

They descended only far enough to inspect the nearest.

Another access plate.

Then another, eighty yards beyond.

A maintenance line.

The conduit was not an isolated piece.

Still not proof of one continent-spanning network.

But definitely infrastructure.

Lucas recorded each point.

The third plate had been broken open long ago.

Not recently.

Weathering softened the edges. Dirt filled most of the cavity beneath it.

Inside, a narrow channel descended at an angle.

Blue material threaded the bottom.

Glassroot-like.

Lucas did not call it Glassroot.

Identify did.

CONDITIONED GLASSROOT CONDUIT

F-Rank Construct / Material Interface Condition: Damaged / Partially Active Function: Environmental Resource Routing

Additional information unavailable.

Nobody spoke for several seconds.

Then Marcus said, “Well. That’s less ambiguous.”

“Somewhat,” Lucas said.

“It literally says Glassroot.”

“It says this conduit uses conditioned Glassroot. It doesn’t say every wild formation is part of this system.”

Marcus rubbed his face. “You are exhausting.”

“I’m right.”

“That makes it worse.”

Lucas crouched at the edge.

The conditioned Glassroot was different from the quarry growth.

Straighter.

Thinner.

Contained inside worked channels rather than forcing itself through stone.

He could sense mana in it.

Faint.

Structured.

A direction without movement.

He extended the Channelwood Spear toward the opening but stopped a foot short.

Megan said, “What are you doing?”

“Not touching it.”

“That is not an answer.”

Lucas activated Focused Mana Reinforcement.

Mana gathered through his hands and into the spear.

He narrowed it toward the forward third of the shaft.

Not the tip.

Near it.

He had learned that hard boundaries wasted control, so he tapered the concentration.

The spear felt different under the Skill now. Not stronger everywhere. Ready along one chosen line.

He held the point twelve inches from the conditioned Glassroot.

Nothing happened.

Good.

He shifted the mana forward.

Still nothing.

He thought about Kinetic Thrust.

That Skill did not simply reinforce the spear. It timed a pulse with motion.

Force and mana traveled together.

What if motion was not required all the way to contact?

He made a tiny thrust.

Stopped the spear before the opening.

At the same instant, he pushed a narrow mana pulse forward.

The pulse collapsed at the spearpoint.

Nothing.

Iterative Insight returned the failure to him with irritating clarity.

He had pushed mana into the spear.

Not beyond it.

Again.

This time he tried to continue the intention past the point.

Mana spilled sideways.

His fingers tingled.

“Bad?” Owen asked.

“Wasteful.”

“Different kind of bad.”

Lucas reset.

The problem was conceptual.

Kinetic Thrust used the spear as a path for acceleration.

Focused Mana Reinforcement let him define where mana density mattered.

He was still treating the spearpoint as a wall.

The Channelwood Spear was conductive.

A path.

The point was only where the wood ended.

He narrowed reinforcement through his rear hand, forward hand, shaft, and spearhead.

A connected line.

Then he thrust slowly into empty air.

Not toward the conduit.

Parallel to it.

Mana moved with the weapon.

At the final inch of motion, Lucas did not stop the pulse at the point.

He let the physical thrust stop.

He asked the mana to keep going.

Something snapped through the air.

A blue-white line extended perhaps eighteen inches beyond the spearhead and vanished.

Everyone froze.

Owen said, “You have got to be kidding me.”

Lucas stared.

A shallow mark had appeared in the dirt.

Not a crater.

A cut.

Two inches long.

Marcus walked over and crouched.

“That was you?”

“I think so.”

Megan said, “Do it away from the ancient magical plumbing.”

“That seems wise.”

They moved fifty yards uphill to bare stone.

Lucas’s heart had accelerated.

Not fear.

Possibility.

He planted his feet.

No Mana Step.

He repeated the sequence.

Focused line.

Spear-aligned motion.

Kinetic principle without fully invoking Kinetic Thrust.

Continue mana past the point.

The second attempt produced nothing.

The third produced a blue flicker that veered left and died.

The fourth made his wrist ache.

Megan lifted one finger.

Lucas lowered the spear.

“I know.”

“You get three more attempts after ten minutes.”

“That is strangely generous.”

“I’m curious too.”

They ate while waiting.

Nia drew the maintenance plates onto Priya’s map copy. Daniel measured spacing. Marcus watched the western valley.

Owen sat beside Lucas.

“So what are we calling your tiny angry flashlight?”

“Nothing.”

“Mana Poke.”

“No.”

“Pointy Beam.”

“No.”

“Spearlight.”

Lucas looked at him.

“That one almost got you.”

“It did not.”

Ten minutes passed.

Lucas tried again.

First attempt: failure.

Second: a short line, maybe a foot.

Third: clean.

Blue-white mana snapped from the spearpoint and struck the limestone three feet away.

A chip jumped from the surface.

System text appeared.

Technique pathway recognized.

Mana Spearman principles integrated:

Focused mana concentration.

Spear-aligned projection.

Kinetic pulse timing.

Repeated independent control.

Skill acquisition conditions satisfied.

ARC LANCE

F-Rank Class Skill

Project a short spear-aligned lance of concentrated mana beyond the weapon point.

Effective range, stability, penetration, and mana cost depend on concentration, timing, weapon compatibility, control, and user development.

Rapid repetition significantly reduces efficiency and stability.

Arc Lance does not require physical contact, but loses cohesion with distance.

Lucas read it twice.

Then a third time.

Owen watched his face.

“You got the thing.”

“Arc Lance.”

Owen looked offended. “The System is better at names than I am.”

“Low bar.”

Marcus stood. “How far?”

“Unknown.”

“How hard?”

“Unknown.”

“Useful?”

Lucas looked at the chip in the limestone.

“Probably.”

Megan pointed toward his wrist. “Pain?”

“Mild.”

“Then probably later.”

Lucas wanted to argue.

He did not.

That lasted eleven minutes.

The first real problem arrived from the west.

Owen noticed movement on the ridge.

“Company.”

The Ridgeback Stalker stood between two limestone shelves.

Same level.

Lv.8.

Whether it was the same animal as yesterday remained impossible to prove, but the pale foreleg scar was visible.

That made it likely.

It watched them.

Marcus raised his shield.

Lucas held the spear low.

Nobody fired an arrow because nobody had brought an idiot this time.

The Stalker paced sideways.

Nia whispered, “We’re between it and the valley.”

Owen scanned behind them. “And our retreat is uphill.”

Hazard Recognition emphasized the obvious combination.

Narrow route.

Predator controlling lateral space.

Six people.

Megan in the center.

Unknown conduit behavior beneath them.

“Back uphill,” Lucas said.

They moved.

The Stalker followed.

Not charging.

Testing.

Marcus said, “If it commits?”

“Same as yesterday. Redirect. Don’t stop it.”

“Your hip?”

“No Step.”

“Good.”

The Stalker accelerated.

Not full charge.

A feint.

It stopped fifteen yards away.

Lucas felt mana gathering through its limbs.

Then the conditioned Glassroot beneath the broken maintenance plate behind them flashed.

The Stalker flinched.

This time it did not retreat.

It charged.

Marcus planted on the right.

Lucas took left.

The animal came low, jaw wide.

Too far for the spear.

Not too far for Arc Lance.

Lucas gathered mana.

Focused Mana Reinforcement narrowed through his grip and shaft.

Arc Lance formed at the point.

He aimed for the ground in front of the Stalker.

Not the animal.

He released.

The blue-white line crossed perhaps twelve feet.

Shorter than he wanted.

It struck stone two yards before the Stalker’s lead foot and burst into sparks.

The predator jerked sideways.

Not wounded.

Surprised.

Marcus moved with the change.

His shield clipped the Stalker’s shoulder rather than taking the charge square.

Lucas stepped in.

Physical range now.

Spearpoint toward foreleg.

Focused Mana Reinforcement moved along the expected force path.

The Stalker twisted.

Lucas saw the change early.

He shifted the focus before impact.

Better.

The spear scraped the bony ridge instead of catching the leg.

The force rolled through his wrists without the previous jolt.

The Stalker snapped at the shaft.

Lucas released his rear hand and pivoted.

No Mana Step.

Marcus struck its flank.

Owen’s arrow hit dirt beside its hind foot, intentionally closing the easiest pursuit line.

The Stalker bounded away.

It stopped twenty yards downhill.

Lucas had one clean Arc Lance in him.

Maybe two.

He could feel the Skill’s cost now.

The projection had consumed more mana than a normal Kinetic Thrust and far more than a short reinforcement adjustment.

Distance was expensive.

Cohesion was worse.

The first combat use had not even touched the enemy.

It had still mattered.

The Stalker watched them.

Blue light pulsed beneath the valley.

Strong.

The animal’s ears flattened.

A second, weaker glow appeared farther north.

The Stalker turned toward it.

Then ran west.

Nobody followed.

Lucas exhaled.

Marcus looked at him.

“You missed.”

“Yes.”

“That was useful.”

“Yes.”

Marcus grinned. “You hate that.”

“A little.”

Megan checked Lucas’s hip before she checked anything else.

“No Step?”

“No Step.”

“Pain?”

“Hip is fine. Wrist is annoyed.”

“Good. You may keep both.”

They waited until the valley quieted.

Then Lucas checked progression.

Human — Lv.5

Progress: 77%

Mana Spearman — Lv.7

Progress: 86%

Explorer — Lv.6

Progress: 29%

The Skill development and field use had pushed his Class hard.

Not a level.

Close.

Good.

Arc Lance was not a magic rifle.

It was a spear attack whose last piece did not require the spear to arrive.

That distinction mattered.

They returned to the broken conduit only after Owen confirmed the Stalker had continued west.

No one touched the conditioned Glassroot.

Lucas stood outside the exclusion line and looked at the channel.

He understood something now that he had not that morning.

Range changed choices before it changed damage.

A spear forced him to solve distance with feet.

Mana Step gave him one way to alter those feet.

Arc Lance gave him another option entirely.

Not better.

Different.

He could threaten space without occupying it.

He could trigger movement.

Test a surface.

Strike a joint he could not safely reach.

Or waste a large amount of mana missing a monster by six feet.

All useful information.

Daniel called from the next access plate.

“Found another mark.”

They joined him.

On the underside of a broken stone lip, nearly hidden by dirt, three vertical lines had been carved beside a rectangular symbol.

The motif family.

Not translated.

Not proof that the conduit and Three-Line Waystation had identical builders.

But another relationship worth recording.

Nia stared at it.

“So this goes back to the road people?”

“Maybe,” Lucas said.

Marcus groaned.

Nia laughed. “I’m learning.”

Lucas opened Field Record.

WESTERN CONDUIT — NEW OBSERVATIONS

Conditioned Glassroot confirmed inside ancient Environmental Resource Routing infrastructure. Maintenance access plates extend northwest in repeated spacing. Three-line motif-family marking observed on damaged access structure. Relationship to Waystation / dungeon infrastructure remains unconfirmed.

Arc Lance acquired through Mana Spearman technique integration. Initial reliable combat-effective projection range: short. Mana cost: high relative to Kinetic Thrust. Cohesion declines quickly with distance. First combat use missed target but altered charge line. Do not confuse missed damage with failed utility.

He paused.

Then added:

A spear’s reach is not only where the point can touch.

By the time they returned to Lowbank, Priya had three new reports waiting.

Stonefield had observed the standard strong pulse.

The weaker east/down pulse followed.

Fragment One at the neutral rise brightened once.

And, according to a western relay watcher, the buried conduit valley produced a pale flash after both Stonefield signals had already been relayed.

The timing remained uselessly imprecise.

Lucas was almost relieved.

One clean answer would have been suspicious at this point.

Claire listened to their report without interrupting.

When Lucas reached the conditioned Glassroot identification, she leaned forward.

“So some of it is infrastructure.”

“Some of it was deliberately installed inside infrastructure.”

“Different sentence.”

“Important difference.”

She nodded.

When he described Arc Lance, Claire looked at Megan.

“Is he allowed to have that?”

Megan said, “Apparently the System doesn’t consult me.”

“It should,” Owen said.

“It really should.”

Marcus laid Priya’s map on the table.

The western maintenance line extended beyond their current survey.

Toward unknown ground.

Toward the direction the Ridgeback Stalker had fled.

Away from the known Sunken Road.

Maybe it curved back.

Maybe it connected to the Three-Line Waystation.

Maybe it went somewhere entirely different.

They did not know.

That was becoming a familiar shape.

Claire tapped the western route.

“No carts.”

“Agreed,” Daniel said.

“No excavation.”

“Agreed.”

“Observation team tomorrow?”

Lucas looked at Megan.

She sighed.

“Walking is fine. Controlled forward Mana Step practice remains fine. Combat use remains not fine.”

Marcus said, “What about the new blue spear thing?”

Megan looked at Lucas.

“How many times?”

Lucas considered lying.

Owen smiled.

“Seven deliberate attempts,” Lucas said. “One combat use.”

“Tomorrow you get controlled testing after I check the wrist.”

Lucas nodded.

That was fair.

Annoying.

Fair.

Outside, evening settled over Lowbank.

Fish smoked over low fires. Reeds moved in the river wind. Runners carried updated route notices east toward Stonefield and north toward Hearth.

The route network moved information again.

This time it carried a new fact.

Conditioned Glassroot existed inside ancient infrastructure.

And another fact followed it.

Lucas Mercer could now strike beyond the end of his spear.

Neither fact explained what lay under the ground.

Neither made the west safe.

At the edge of Lowbank, Lucas lifted the Channelwood Spear and sighted along its shaft toward the dark ridge.

Physical point.

Mana beyond it.

A path continuing after the obvious ending.

Somewhere beneath the valley, blue-white light moved through a conduit built before humanity arrived.

Lucas lowered the spear.

Tomorrow, they would follow it farther.
