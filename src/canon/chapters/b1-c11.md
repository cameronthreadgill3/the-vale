# Chapter 11
## The Wrong Prey

The Channelwood Spear was too easy to trust. Lucas discovered that before breakfast.

He stood beyond Hearth’s eastern barricade with the spear held loosely in both hands, his right palm wrapped, his left shoulder warm but functional, and three fist-sized pieces of bark arranged on a fallen log twenty feet away. The first thrust felt clean. The second felt cleaner. On the third, Kinetic Thrust fired exactly when he wanted it to, and the spearhead punched through the bark and buried itself two inches into the log.

Lucas stared at it. Yesterday that would have felt like success. Today it felt suspicious.

He pulled the spear free and tried again with the same movement, the same target, and less mana. The point struck, penetrated, and stopped. Iterative Insight sharpened the contrast. The difference was not only the Skill. The Channelwood shaft carried mana so smoothly that his bad timing no longer announced itself with a shudder, a twist, or the threatening crack of ashwood fibers. The weapon forgave him. That was useful. It was also dangerous.

“You’re frowning at the expensive stick.”

Owen approached from the settlement with a strip of cooked grazer meat in one hand.

“It isn’t expensive.”

“You almost died in a pocket dimension for it.”

Lucas considered. “Fair.”

Owen handed him half the meat. Lucas ate while looking at the spear.

“Problem?”

“It hides mistakes.”

“That sounds like the opposite of a problem.”

“It’s both.”

Owen chewed. “You know, sometimes I miss problems like rent.”

Lucas pulled up Status.

Human — Lv. 3

Progress: 4%

Mana Spearman — Lv. 3

Progress: 34%

Profession: Not Acquired

Strength: 11

Agility: 14

Vitality: 11

Endurance: 10

Perception: 13

Intelligence: 13

Wisdom: 13

Willpower: 10

Available Attribute Points: 4

He still had not spent them. The temptation was stronger now. Endurance would help sustained fighting. Vitality might make mistakes less expensive. Wisdom might improve control. Strength could help him manage the force Kinetic Thrust created. Every answer solved a different problem, so he still had no answer. He dismissed the interface.

Owen noticed. “Still hoarding points?”

“Collecting data.”

“You have become a dragon, but for spreadsheets.”

“I sold construction equipment. This is emotionally familiar.”

A horn sounded from Hearth. One long note. Not attack. Assembly. Grant had started using different signals the previous day.

Lucas and Owen returned through the east gap. About thirty hunters, scouts, builders, and anyone else Grant considered relevant gathered around the central board. A rough map had been scratched onto a broad plank with charcoal. Hearth sat near the bottom. The stream curved east. The meadow from yesterday’s hunt was marked farther north, and beyond it a circle represented the mana-rich spring.

Grant tapped the northern area. “We had another animal come south before dawn.”

Priya stood beside him. “Razor Hound. Level five.”

Lucas frowned. “Alive?”

“No.”

“Wounds?”

Priya nodded. “Crushed ribs. Deep puncture through the back. Not feeding damage.”

Grant looked around the group. “We now have a wounded Ridgeclaw, displaced grazers, and a dead Razor Hound inside a day and a half. All from the same direction.”

Marcus, still wrapped around the ribs, stood with his arms crossed. “So something bigger moved in.”

“Maybe,” Lucas said.

Marcus looked at him. “You have another explanation?”

“No. I have a dislike of turning likely into known.”

Grant nodded once. “Which is why we’re not sending a hunting party.”

Lucas relaxed slightly.

“We’re sending scouts.”

He stopped relaxing.

Owen raised a hand. “I assume this is the part where my day gets worse.”

“Yes.”

Grant pointed at him, Priya, and a lean man named Cole who had taken a Common Scout Class two days earlier. “Three-person observation team. Stay south of the spring. Confirm tracks, direction, and whether anything has established territory. No engagement.”

Good. Lucas agreed with all of it. Then Grant looked at him.

“You’re fourth.”

Sarah said, “No.” She had apparently learned teleportation. Lucas had not seen her arrive.

Grant exhaled. “He can sense mana signatures before the others can see them.”

“He also has an injured shoulder, a damaged hand, and the survival instincts of a curious raccoon.”

Owen coughed into his fist. Lucas looked at him.

“Nothing.”

Grant turned to Sarah. “They’re observing. Not hunting.”

Sarah looked at Lucas. “That distinction depends heavily on him remembering it.”

“I can observe.”

“You stabbed a stone dog yesterday after promising to rest.”

“It was part of the trial.”

“That sentence did not help.”

Lucas flexed his shoulder. No sharp pain. His palm was protected. More importantly, if something was forcing Level 5 predators south, knowing what it was mattered to everyone.

“I go,” Lucas said. “I don’t fight unless we need to disengage.”

Sarah’s expression made clear that contracts with Lucas had poor historical performance. Finally she pointed at his shoulder.

“If that gets worse, you turn around.”

“Yes.”

“And the hand.”

“Yes.”

“And if you see something Level ten and decide this is educational—”

“I leave.”

Owen leaned toward Priya. “He said that very convincingly.”

“I heard him.”

They left twenty minutes later. No formal Party. That was deliberate. They were not hunting, and Lucas did not want the existence of a Party interface quietly turning observation into an expectation of combat.

Cole led. His Scout Class had already given him a noticeable advantage at reading disturbed ground. Priya handled route memory and animal signs. Owen moved through the woods with the relaxed attention of someone who had spent years outdoors before the System decided to put numbers on it. Lucas watched mana. Four different ways of seeing the same forest. That interested him almost as much as the danger. Almost.

They found the dead Razor Hound forty minutes north of Hearth. The corpse lay beneath a fernlike plant, twisted onto one side. Lucas crouched without touching it. Identify offered nothing new beyond species, level, and rank. The puncture through its back was enormous. Not a claw mark. One hole.

Cole placed two fingers beside it. “Three inches across.”

“Could be horn,” Owen said.

Priya shook her head. “Angle’s wrong. Came down.”

Lucas looked at the crushed ribs. Something had hit the hound from above hard enough to fold its chest, then punctured it, or punctured it while crushing it. Neither possibility improved his morning.

They continued north. The forest changed gradually. Less noise, not silence. Silence would have been obvious. This was subtraction. Fewer Needle Rats rustling beneath roots. Fewer distant calls. Twice Lucas sensed small concentrations of mana retreating before the group approached. Animals avoiding them, or avoiding the direction they were traveling.

At the meadow they found yesterday’s Ridgeclaw kill stripped nearly to bone. Not eaten cleanly. Scattered. The carcass had been moved fifteen feet.

Owen crouched beside the dirt. “There.”

Tracks. Five toes. Broad. Deep. The same pattern from near the spring.

Cole measured one with his hand. “Bigger than yesterday’s?”

Priya shook her head. “Same, I think.”

Lucas studied the impressions. One track sank deeper on the right side. The next did too. Then the next.

“Weight shift,” he said.

Owen looked over. “Injured?”

“Maybe. Or carrying something.”

Iterative Insight offered no answer. Good. He compared the stride. Long, uneven only slightly. Whatever made the tracks was large enough that the ground noticed.

They followed them for less than two hundred yards. Then Cole stopped so abruptly Priya nearly walked into him. He pointed.

A tree ahead had been damaged. Not scratched. Broken. An ashwood trunk as thick as Lucas’s thigh leaned at an angle, bark peeled away six feet above the ground. Dark residue marked the exposed wood.

Lucas sensed mana before he saw anything else. Dense. Not the bright, quick gathering of a Razor Hound, and not the broad pulse of a grazer. This felt heavy, compressed, and close.

“Back,” Lucas whispered.

Nobody argued. They moved south. Ten steps. Twenty.

A branch cracked behind them. Not loud. Heavy. Lucas turned.

Something moved between the trees. Gray-brown hide. Low shoulders. Too large.

Identify.

Stonehide Ravager

Beast — Lv. 9

F-Rank

Lucas’s stomach dropped. Level nine.

The Ravager stepped into view. It resembled a boar only in the same way a landslide resembled falling dirt. Six feet at the shoulder. Thick plated hide layered across its neck and back. Two forward tusks curved from a blunt head, but the more dangerous feature was the short central horn above its nose, three inches thick at the base.

Lucas thought of the Razor Hound. Crushed ribs. Puncture from above. Likely had just become known.

The Ravager’s right foreleg was bloodied. Injured. That explained the uneven tracks. It did not make Lucas feel better.

Owen whispered, “We leave.”

“Yes.”

They backed away. The Ravager watched. One step. Two. Lucas felt mana gather beneath its hide, slowly, like pressure building under stone.

“Don’t run.”

Cole’s breathing went shallow. Priya kept moving. The Ravager lowered its head. Lucas’s entire body went cold.

“Now run.”

They turned. The forest detonated behind them.

The Ravager charged. Lucas heard trees snapping. Not branches. Trees. They split. Cole went left. Priya right. Owen vaulted a fallen trunk. Lucas followed. The Ravager did not. It went through it. The log exploded into rotten fragments, and Lucas felt the impact through the ground.

“Spread!” Owen shouted.

Good idea. The Ravager chose Cole. Bad luck. Cole glanced back and stumbled. Lucas saw the line immediately: too straight, too open. The Ravager would catch him.

Lucas changed direction. He had promised not to fight unless they needed to disengage. Apparently the universe enjoyed technical compliance.

He sprinted across the Ravager’s path. “Hey!”

No effect. He struck the side of a tree with the butt of his spear. “HEY!”

The Ravager’s head shifted. Lucas thrust at its face from fifteen feet away, pointless physically, but intent mattered. The beast changed targets.

“Bad plan,” Lucas told himself.

The Ravager turned. Mana surged. Lucas ran perpendicular. Agility fourteen mattered. It did not make him faster than a Level 9 monster. The Ravager clipped a tree while turning and lost half a step. Lucas used it. He ducked between two close trunks. The Ravager slammed into one. The tree shook and bark showered down. The beast recoiled.

Lucas saw a chance, not to kill, to create distance. He reinforced the Channelwood Spear. The mana flowed beautifully. Kinetic Thrust gathered. Lucas aimed behind the Ravager’s front leg where the plates looked thinner, stepped in, thrust, pulse.

The spearhead struck hide and stopped. Not completely. It penetrated perhaps an inch. Against the Trial Construct, an inch into a joint had mattered. Against the Ravager, it made the monster angry.

The beast twisted. Lucas tried to withdraw. Too slow. Its shoulder hit him. The world became sky, then dirt, then pain. Lucas rolled twice and stopped against a root. He could not breathe. His spear was gone. No. Three yards away.

The Ravager turned. Lucas’s left shoulder burned. His ribs hurt. His right palm felt wet beneath the bandage. The monster charged again. He moved, barely. A tusk tore across his jacket and opened his side. Heat became cold. Lucas hit the ground.

Owen’s arrow struck the Ravager’s face and bounced off the plated ridge above the eye.

“Lucas!”

The Ravager turned toward Owen. Lucas tried to stand. His left arm failed. Not broken. Maybe. He grabbed the spear with his right hand and pushed himself upright.

Priya threw something. A rock. It struck the Ravager’s injured foreleg and the beast stumbled. There. Injury. Real weakness. Not one Iterative Insight had gifted him. One they had observed from the tracks before the fight.

Lucas shouted, “Right leg! Keep it turning left!”

Owen understood first. He ran across the beast’s front from right to left. The Ravager pivoted. Its injured leg took weight and buckled slightly. Cole appeared behind a tree and drove a short spear into its flank. The weapon barely entered, but the Ravager turned again, the wrong direction for it. Pain changed its movement.

Lucas breathed shallowly. Think. Not beat it. Escape it. The meadow lay south. Open ground favored the Ravager. Bad. Dense trees east. Better. A narrow split between two boulders lay thirty yards uphill. Best.

“Owen! Rocks!”

Owen looked, saw them. “Move!”

They ran. Lucas tried. His side screamed. Blood soaked his shirt. The Ravager came after them. Cole reached the gap first, Priya second. Owen stopped on the far side.

“Lucas!”

The distance felt enormous. Twenty yards. Fifteen. The Ravager gained. Lucas could feel its mana building, a stronger charge. He was not going to make it. The realization arrived without drama. Simple arithmetic.

He had one option. Turn. Use Kinetic Thrust, not against the armor, against the injured leg.

Lucas planted his feet. His left shoulder protested so violently his vision blurred. He changed grip, mostly right hand. Terrible structure. Iterative Insight sharpened that fact. No solution. Just truth.

The Ravager charged. Lucas waited. Ten yards. Seven. Mana Reinforcement flowed into the spear. Five. Kinetic Thrust gathered. Three.

Lucas stepped sideways and thrust downward. The timing was ugly and the angle worse, but the point hit the already-bloodied joint above the Ravager’s right hoof. Kinetic Thrust fired. The Channelwood Spear flexed. The Ravager’s leg collapsed. Its body did not stop. A thousand pounds of monster became a sliding wall.

Lucas tried to jump clear. A tusk caught his thigh. Pain erased the forest. He hit stone beside the gap. The Ravager crashed into the boulders and one tusk wedged between them. For half a second the beast was stuck.

Owen grabbed Lucas under the arms. Lucas screamed.

“Sorry.”

“Move.”

Cole grabbed his other side. They dragged him through the gap. The Ravager ripped free behind them and stone cracked, but the gap narrowed after the first few feet. Too narrow. The Ravager rammed its head between the rocks and could not fit. It screamed. Lucas had never heard a boar scream like that. He hoped never to again.

“Keep moving,” Priya said.

Correct. The Ravager could go around. They did not stop for another five minutes. Lucas remembered perhaps half of them. Then Owen lowered him beside the stream, not the mana-rich spring, the smaller Ashwood tributary they had crossed on the way north.

Priya cut open Lucas’s pant leg. The tusk wound was ugly, a deep groove across the outer thigh, not through the muscle entirely, blood everywhere. His side was better: a long cut, shallow enough to be insulting compared with the leg.

Owen’s face had gone pale. “You’re fine.”

Lucas looked at him. “That sounded terrible.”

“I know.”

Cole pressed folded cloth into the thigh wound. Lucas nearly blacked out.

“Pressure.”

“I know what pressure is.”

“Then stop trying to kick me.”

“I am not intentionally doing that.”

Priya crouched near his shoulder. “Can you move the arm?”

Lucas tried. A few inches. Pain. “Yes.”

“That is a generous definition.”

The Ravager did not appear. Minutes passed, then ten. The forest slowly remembered sound.

Lucas opened Notifications because apparently near death had not cured him of curiosity. Several waited.

COMBAT CONTRIBUTION RECORDED

You survived direct engagement with an opponent significantly above your current Class level.

Relative challenge: Extreme.

Meaningful tactical adaptation recorded.

Mana Spearman progression increased.

He opened Progression.

Mana Spearman — Lv. 4

Progress: 8%

Lucas stared. A level. More than a level’s worth of progress from one encounter, not because he killed the Ravager. He had not. Because it had almost killed him. That distinction mattered.

Ding!

CLASS LEVEL INCREASED

Mana Spearman — Lv. 4

Automatic Attribute Growth:

Agility +1

Wisdom +1

Perception +1

Free Attribute Points Gained: 2

Available Attribute Points: 6

Human progression had jumped too.

Human — Lv. 3

Progress: 39%

Lucas closed the interface. Owen watched him.

“Please tell me you are not happy.”

“I’m not.”

“Your face is doing a thing.”

“I got a level.”

Owen stared at him, then laughed once. Not humor. Disbelief. “Of course you did.”

Lucas looked at the blood soaking Cole’s improvised bandage. The progression felt different than yesterday’s. Yesterday he had earned a weapon through controlled challenge. Today he had earned a level by making a sequence of decisions that had almost ended with his body under a monster. The System did not care whether that was wise. It measured danger, contribution, adaptation, accomplishment. That was important. Reward was not approval.

He said it aloud. “The System rewarding something doesn’t mean we should do it again.”

Priya looked at him. “No argument here.”

Owen pointed toward his leg. “I was already convinced.”

They built a rough litter from two poles and jackets. Lucas objected once. Nobody listened. Good.

The return to Hearth took almost three hours. Sarah met them at the gate. Lucas had seen her angry. This was different. She looked at the blood, then at his face, then at Owen.

“What?”

“Stonehide Ravager,” Owen said. “Level nine.”

Sarah’s expression emptied. “Medical. Now.”

The next twenty minutes were unpleasant. Megan used her healing Skill to slow bleeding and encourage the edges of the thigh wound to close, but the depth was beyond what she could repair cleanly. Sarah irrigated it. Lucas discovered new opinions about irrigation. His shoulder was badly strained again.

When it was over he lay under a blanket and looked at the Channelwood Spear. The shaft was scratched. The spearhead had a tiny chip near one edge. Not indestructible. Lucas liked it more for that.

Grant came later.

“We avoid the north,” Grant said. “Move hunting west and east. Nobody approaches the spring.”

“Good.”

“Marcus thinks we should organize a kill team once he’s healed.”

Lucas closed his eyes. “Marcus is an idiot.”

From outside the shelter came Marcus’s voice. “I heard that.”

“Good.”

Grant almost smiled. “We’re not doing it. Not yet.”

Lucas looked at him. “Because it’s Level nine?”

“Because four competent people went to observe it and came back carrying one.”

Better answer.

Grant left. Night settled over Hearth. Lucas opened Status one last time.

Strength: 11

Agility: 15

Vitality: 11

Endurance: 10

Perception: 14

Intelligence: 13

Wisdom: 14

Willpower: 10

Available Attribute Points: 6

He stared at Endurance ten, then Vitality eleven, then the six unspent points. He could spend them now, patch the obvious weakness, make himself tougher because today had hurt. But fear was not a build plan. He closed the interface.

Tomorrow, when he could think without pain and blood loss, he would decide. For now he looked at the Channelwood Spear. Yesterday it had felt like a threshold: a real weapon, a new Skill, Level three. Today a Level 9 beast had treated his best thrust as an irritation.

Lucas found that strangely reassuring. The Hollow Reach had not become easier because he had improved. It had become larger. His spear gave him more options. Kinetic Thrust gave him more force. Iterative Insight helped him learn from the difference. None of them made a bad fight good.

Outside, Owen was telling someone the story. Lucas heard the words “heroically distracted it,” followed by Priya correcting him with, “recklessly volunteered as prey.”

Lucas smiled despite himself.

Then a notification appeared. Not progression. Not a Skill. A discovery record.

REGIONAL THREAT OBSERVED

Stonehide Ravager — Lv. 9 F-Rank

Observation contribution recorded.

No reward followed. Lucas read it twice. Regional threat. Not boss. Not unique. Not event creature. Just something that lived here.

He dismissed the message. The Sunken Road waited somewhere beyond the routes they knew, ancient stone built before humanity arrived. Tomorrow, or the day after, once Sarah stopped threatening to tie him to the medical shelter, Lucas intended to find out why. Preferably without becoming anything else’s prey.
