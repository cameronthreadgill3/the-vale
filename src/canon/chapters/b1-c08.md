# Chapter 8
## Without a Teacher

Lucas discovered that scientific experimentation lost a lot of dignity when the lab was a patch of dirt and the expensive equipment was a stick. The stick was not even a particularly good stick. He held it in his right hand, frowned at the ashwood shaft, and activated Mana Reinforcement. Mana moved from him into the wood—too much of it, again. He released the Skill, waited for the faint pressure behind his eyes to fade, and tried again. This time he pictured only the last foot of the shaft. Mana entered through his hand, spread six inches, then blurred outward until almost half the spear carried a weak reinforcement. “Wrong.”

The morning sun had barely cleared the trees. Hearth was awake, but most of the noise sat in the center: cooking, water lines, watch changes, and the low argument of twelve hundred people learning that civilization required queues. Lucas had picked the eastern edge on purpose—no Daniel, no Marcus, no Megan, nobody telling him what mana was supposed to do. Owen had left before sunrise with Priya and the father from yesterday’s arriving family. Their scouting group was checking the route toward the northern spring. Lucas had almost gone. He wanted to. That was exactly why he stayed. The instinct behind movement, new terrain, unknown creatures, the chance of finding something nobody else had seen, remained. Instead he had a flaw in a Skill. And the flaw bothered him. Mana Reinforcement worked. That was not the problem. It worked too broadly. The Skill seemed eager to treat reinforcement as a general instruction: strengthen me, strengthen the spear, strengthen whatever Lucas was holding. Useful. Crude. Yesterday he had learned he could influence where the mana concentrated. Today he wanted to know why.

He set the spear across two short logs. A third log sat under the center as a crude fulcrum. Daniel would have built a better test rig in thirty seconds. That was why Lucas had not asked him. Daniel already understood load paths. If Lucas let him explain the answer, Lucas would learn Daniel’s answer. He wanted his own—not because independence was always better, but because he needed to know what happened when he reached the edge of what someone else could explain. Lucas placed a flat stone on one end of the spear. The shaft bent slightly. He added another. More bend. He activated Mana Reinforcement through the section above the center log. The bend changed—not much, enough. He released the Skill and took the stones off. Again. This time he reinforced near his hand instead of the pressure point. The shaft still bent almost as much as without mana. Distance mattered. Already known. But Lucas was not testing whether distance mattered. He was testing whether the Skill understood the target the way he did. He moved his grip. Reinforced the same physical section from a different contact point. The mana behaved differently. Lucas stopped. Repeated it. Same result. Interesting. The Skill was not simply filling wood with mana. It was following a path from him into the object. Contact mattered.

He sat back on his heels. His shoulder gave a mild ache—nothing sharp. Sarah had cleared controlled movement, but Lucas had promised himself no thrusts until she checked him again. This was better anyway—no combat, no urgency, no monster trying to remove a useful body part. Just a question. He opened Skills.

Mana Reinforcement

Channels mana through the body or a wielded object to improve structural integrity and effective output. Safe intensity depends upon control, attributes, mastery, and target compatibility.

The description had not changed. Lucas read it twice. Channels mana through. Not places mana in. Through. Language mattered. Maybe. Or maybe he was overthinking a tooltip written by an incomprehensible cosmic operating system. Both seemed possible. He closed the screen. Tried the spear again. This time he did not think about strengthening the wood. He thought about the path: hand, contact, grain, forward. Mana moved. It spread. Lucas resisted the instinct to push harder. That had been his mistake before. More mana was not more control. He reduced the flow. The reinforcement weakened. But the edges became easier to feel. Lucas froze. There. At low intensity, he could sense where the Skill stopped being deliberate and started becoming automatic. The boundary was fuzzy. He tried to hold it. The mana spread anyway. He released. Again. Same amount. Same section. This time he tried to narrow the boundary before activating. Failure. The mana entered broadly. Again. He activated normally, then narrowed. Better. Again. Worse. Again. Better. Again. Nothing. Lucas smiled. Inconsistent results were still results.

He found a scrap of charcoal near the cooking area and marked the shaft—one line six inches from his rear hand, another at twelve, another at eighteen. Owen would make fun of him for turning a spear into a ruler. Fortunately Owen was four hours north. Lucas reinforced to the first mark. Too far. Second attempt. Too short. Third. Close. Fourth. Close again. Fifth. The boundary settled almost exactly at the charcoal line. Lucas held it—one breath, two, three. Mana consumption felt lower than reinforcing the whole spear. Of course it did. Less target. Less mana. But that was not the interesting part. The reinforced section felt denser—not physically heavier, more present to his mana sense. The same amount of attention compressed into less space. He released. His pulse had accelerated. Ridiculous. He had not killed anything. Had not leveled. Had not found treasure. He had made magic stop at a charcoal line. And it felt like winning.

“Breakfast.” Sarah stood twenty feet away. Lucas jumped. She looked at the marked spear, then the stones, then him. “What are you doing?” “Nothing medically concerning.” “That answer is medically concerning.” Lucas stood. His left shoulder complained mildly. Sarah noticed. Of course she did. “Pain?” “Two.” “Out of ten?” “Out of however many gets me cleared.” “Lucas.” “Two.” She checked the shoulder. Range was better again. The deep ache remained, but the tissue no longer felt as angry after light movement. Sarah nodded. “Controlled thrusts today. No hard impact.” Lucas brightened. “Reinforcement?” “Not through the shoulder.” “Excellent.” “That was not enthusiasm-worthy.” “It was to me.” Sarah looked at the spear again. “What are the marks?” Lucas hesitated. He could explain. He almost did. Then stopped. “Testing something.” Sarah waited. When he offered nothing else, she shrugged. “Eat first.” “Bossy.” “Alive.” “Strong argument.”

Breakfast was smoked grazer, water, and something Emily insisted was edible root mash. The root mash tasted like wet cardboard had developed ambitions. Lucas ate it anyway. Hearth moved around him. Grant’s intake board already had twenty-seven names. Two new groups had arrived overnight. Ironbound trainees drilled near the western barricade. Daniel was directing construction of raised storage racks after Needle Rats had discovered the old ground-level version. People adapted. Lucas watched Daniel change the rack design after the first support leaned—cut, brace, test, adjust. No shame in the first version being wrong. That thought followed Lucas back to the eastern edge. He planted a dead branch upright in the dirt. Controlled thrusts. No impact. He stopped the spear point an inch from the bark. Again. Again. Spear Familiarity smoothed the motion—feet, hip, right hand driving, left hand guiding lightly. The injured shoulder stayed quiet. Lucas activated Mana Reinforcement only in the forward third of the spear. Thrust. Stop. The mana spread backward during movement. He frowned. Static control was easier. Motion disrupted it. Again. Same. The act of accelerating the spear changed his intent. His mind stopped thinking about a section of wood and started thinking about the attack. The Skill followed the broader intent. Lucas slowed down. Thrust. The reinforcement held longer. Slower. Held. Faster. Spread. He had found another variable—not speed itself, attention.

Lucas leaned the spear against a tree. He walked a circle. People talked about Skills as abilities—press button, get effect. Spear Familiarity did feel partly like that. It gave him a foundation he had not personally earned through years of training. Mana Reinforcement also activated on command. But once active, it responded to him—intent, attention, contact. Maybe Skills were not instructions. Maybe they were structures, a prepared way for mana to behave. Lucas picked up the spear. He activated reinforcement at the forward mark. Then thrust slowly while refusing to think about the thrust. Instead he watched the mana. The spear movement became awkward. Spear Familiarity tried to help. His body wanted the practiced line. Lucas let it. Mana spread slightly. Better than before. Again. He separated the tasks—body handled movement, attention handled mana. That worked better—not perfectly, better. He laughed. A nearby woman carrying water looked at him strangely. Lucas did not care. He tried again. By midday, his mana reservoir had emptied twice—not fully. He stopped before the headache. Rested. Drank. Waited for regeneration. Wisdom mattered. He could feel it now—not as a number, as recovery, as control, as the difference between mana obeying immediately and requiring correction. Three unspent points waited. Lucas opened Status.

Strength: 10

Agility: 13

Vitality: 10

Endurance: 10

Perception: 12

Intelligence: 12

Wisdom: 12

Willpower: 10

Available Attribute Points: 3

He stared at Wisdom. Yesterday he had almost spent one. Today the argument looked stronger. But spending the point now would contaminate the experiment. If control improved afterward, was it technique or attribute? Lucas closed Status. “No.” He would earn the answer first. Then decide what the answer needed.

The afternoon test was harder. He found three pieces of wood—one ashwood branch, one softer pale branch Daniel’s crews discarded, one short piece of Redhorn bone. Different materials. Same Skill. The pale wood accepted mana easily but felt unstable. Reinforcement spread quickly and unevenly. Ashwood resisted slightly more but held a cleaner path. Bone felt completely different. Mana entered reluctantly, then seemed to settle into the material with surprising density. Lucas repeated each test. No Identify explanation. No teacher. No Skill prompt. He recorded the results by scratching marks into the dirt—A for ashwood, P for pale, B for bone. He tested contact point, flow, boundary, duration. He had no stopwatch. He counted breaths. It was terrible methodology. It was also the best methodology available in an alien forest. The results became clearer. Target compatibility mattered exactly as the Skill description said. But compatibility was not a yes-or-no property. Different materials changed how reinforcement traveled. Lucas held the bone in his right hand. He pushed mana into the center. It resisted. He increased force. The mana scattered. Wrong. He reduced pressure. Nothing. He changed the mental instruction—not enter, follow. The bone had grain too, though not like wood. Structure. Growth lines. He imagined mana taking the easiest route rather than the route Lucas demanded. The response changed—subtle, but real. Lucas’s breathing stopped. He tried again. The mana flowed into the bone with less resistance—not because he used more, but because he stopped fighting the material. His entire morning snapped into a new shape. He had been treating Mana Reinforcement as force—push mana here, hold it there, strengthen this section. But the Skill was channeling—through him, through contact, through a target with its own structure. Control was not only imposing intent. It was adjusting intent to what the mana and material were already doing.

Lucas grabbed the ashwood spear. He reinforced the forward foot. Instead of forcing a hard boundary at the charcoal mark, he let the mana spread naturally. Then he shaped the spread—not wall, gradient. Dense at the tip. Less behind it. Less again toward the middle. The mana settled. Lucas’s eyes widened. The transition felt smoother. No sudden weak point. No hard edge where reinforcement stopped. He had been solving the wrong problem. He did not need perfect localization. He needed deliberate distribution. The difference was enormous. Lucas thrust—slow. The pattern held. Again. Faster. It distorted. He corrected during the movement. The correction came late. Again. He anticipated the distortion. Better. Again. The spear point stopped a fraction from the dead branch. The reinforcement remained concentrated forward, tapered behind, and almost absent at his rear hand. Lucas held still. His mana sense traced the pattern. It was not stronger than anything he had done before. It was better—less mana, less waste, smoother load path, more deliberate, his own. The System chimed. Lucas froze.

HIDDEN ACHIEVEMENT EARNED

Student Without a Teacher

Through deliberate experimentation, repeated failure, independent observation, and genuine technical improvement, you have advanced a System-granted technique beyond its provided foundational use without instruction, Skill assistance, or external guidance.

Reward: General Skill acquisition available.

Lucas stared at the notification. His heart hammered harder than after the grazer charge. Achievement. Hidden. Not for killing something. Not for reaching a level. For learning. He opened Achievements.

Student Without a Teacher — Hidden

Status: Earned

Then Skills. A new notification waited.

GENERAL SKILL ACQUIRED

Iterative Insight — Rare

Deliberate experimentation provides enhanced intuitive feedback regarding efficiency, failure, and underlying principles.

This Skill does not provide unknown knowledge. It does not replace practice. It does not automatically identify weaknesses. It improves the quality of feedback generated by your own deliberate attempts to understand and improve.

Lucas read the description once. Twice. Then a third time. Rare. His Class was Uncommon. The new General Skill was Rare. He expected something dramatic. Nothing happened. No rush of power. No expanded mana pool. No sudden knowledge. The spear remained a marked piece of ashwood. Lucas almost laughed. “Okay.” He activated Mana Reinforcement again. The tapered pattern formed. Iterative Insight did nothing obvious. He thrust. The pattern distorted near the center. And Lucas felt the failure differently—not an answer, a sharper impression. The correction had begun too late because his attention followed the movement instead of anticipating the mana’s tendency to spread under changing intent. Lucas blinked. Had he known that already? Partly. That was the strange thing. The Skill had not inserted information. It had made his own feedback cleaner—like wiping fog from glass. He tried again. This time he deliberately changed only one variable—earlier anticipation. Thrust. Better. Again. Earlier still. Worse. The failure carried a distinct impression: he had overcorrected and starved the transition section. Lucas smiled slowly. “Oh, this is dangerous.” Not dangerous because it made him powerful. Dangerous because he could lose days to this. Maybe weeks. A thousand questions suddenly looked more interesting. How did mana behave in water? Why did Razor Hounds avoid the stream? Why did bone hold reinforcement differently? Could he shape mana in his body the same way? Could movement itself become part of the distribution rather than a disruption? Could a thrust carry reinforcement forward as the weapon accelerated? Could— Lucas stopped. One question at a time. That was probably part of learning too. He checked Progression.

Human — Lv. 2

Progress: 67%

Mana Spearman — Lv. 2

Progress: 43%

Profession: Not Acquired

Meaningful advancement. No level. The achievement and technique work had moved both tracks, especially Class. Lucas approved. Then he checked Titles. None. Good. Not every accomplishment needed a badge pinned to his chest. Footsteps approached. Lucas looked up. Owen emerged between the trees at the eastern approach, sweaty, muddy, and grinning. Priya followed. The father from yesterday walked behind them. All three were alive. Owen saw Lucas sitting in a dirt laboratory surrounded by sticks, bone, charcoal marks, and stones. He stopped. “What happened to you?” Lucas looked around. “Research.” “You look homeless.” “We are homeless.” “More than usual.” Lucas stood. “How was the spring?” “Real.” That mattered. “Good water?” “Identify says potable. Strong mana concentration too.” Lucas’s attention sharpened. Owen noticed. “No.” “What?” “You are not walking four hours today because I said mana.” “I wasn’t.” “You absolutely were.” Lucas picked up the spear. “What about the creature?” Owen’s grin faded. “Tracks.” “Big?” “Very.” “Identify?” “Nothing to Identify. Just prints.” Priya said, “Five toes. Claws. Wider than my hand.” Lucas filed it away. Tomorrow’s problem. Owen pointed at the charcoal marks on the spear. “So what did research accomplish?” Lucas considered the question. He could tell Owen about the achievement. Probably would. But first he wanted to answer honestly. “I learned I was using the Skill wrong.” Owen stared. “System gave you a broken Skill?” “No.” Lucas looked at the spear. “I was treating the basic version like the finished version.” That felt important enough to keep. Owen waited. Lucas finally smiled. “And I got a Rare General Skill.” Owen’s mouth opened. Then closed. Then opened again. “You buried that part.” “It was funnier.” “What Skill?” “Iterative Insight.” “What does it do?” “Helps me learn from deliberate experimentation.” Owen stared at the dirt laboratory, the marked spear, the scraps of bone and wood. “You spent the whole day failing on purpose.” “Yes.” “And the System rewarded that.” “Apparently.” Owen shook his head. “You are going to be insufferable.” “I already was.” They walked back toward Hearth together. Lucas carried the spear carefully. The charcoal marks remained. So did the method. Try. Observe. Change. Try again. The System had given him Mana Reinforcement. What came next did not have to belong to the System. The rest of the Hollow Reach waited—ancient roads, unknown regions, creatures stronger than anything Lucas had fought. He was still Human Level 2. Mana Spearman Level 2. His best weapon was a sharpened branch with charcoal marks on it. He had an injured shoulder, two unspent Attribute Points, and almost no idea how the Multiverse worked. But for the first time since the Accession, Lucas possessed something that felt entirely his—not power, a method. The System had given him Mana Reinforcement. What came next did not have to belong to the System.
