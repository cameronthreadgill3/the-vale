# Chapter 23
## What the Roots Keep

Stonefield celebrated the Broodmother's death for eleven minutes.

Lucas knew because that was how long it took Megan to make Marcus stop retelling the part where he'd hit Lucas's spear with a maul.

"You left out the part where you ignored the retreat order," she said.

Marcus sat on an overturned stone block near the quarry, shirt lifted while she wrapped his ribs. "Tactical initiative."

"You got thrown into a staircase."

"Still initiative."

"Gravity showed more initiative."

Owen, sitting nearby with his back against the quarry wall, raised one hand. "For the record, I support this medical terminology."

Megan pointed at him without looking. "You're next."

His hand dropped.

Lucas watched from a flat section of stone while Thomas Reed and two Stonefield workers carried Rachel's pack toward the settlement.

Nobody cheered when they saw it.

That was when the celebration ended.

The Broodmother had been a problem.

Rachel was a person.

The difference settled over the quarry without anyone announcing it.

Thomas stopped beside Lucas.

"You found it where she left it?"

"Near the doorway."

"And nothing else?"

Lucas knew what he meant.

"No."

Thomas nodded once.

He did not ask Lucas to soften it.

That was one thing Lucas liked about him.

"Notebook inside?"

"Yes."

Thomas looked toward the pack.

"Her brother will want to see it."

Lucas had not known Rachel had a brother in Stonefield.

Another reminder that people continued existing outside the parts of their lives he happened to witness.

"Of course."

Thomas hesitated.

"Anything in it about the dungeon?"

"I didn't read it below."

"Good."

The word carried no accusation.

Only relief.

Rachel's notebook might contain useful information. It also belonged to Rachel before it belonged to anyone's investigation.

Thomas walked away.

Megan finished Marcus's ribs and came directly toward Lucas.

He considered pretending not to notice.

She stopped in front of him.

"Walk."

"That feels like a trap."

"Good instincts. Walk."

Lucas stood.

His right hip objected immediately.

Not enough to stop him.

Enough to make the first step shorter.

Megan saw it.

Of course she did.

He walked twenty feet along the quarry shelf, turned, and came back.

"No Mana Step for two days," she said.

Lucas stared at her.

"Two?"

"Minimum."

"That seems aggressive."

"Your gait changed."

"It's barely changed."

"Your body is compensating before you're consciously deciding to compensate."

That was annoyingly specific.

Lucas looked down at his leg.

The old Ravager scar along his thigh had held.

This pain sat higher and deeper, wrapped around the outside of the hip and into the upper leg. Not sharp unless he pivoted.

Yesterday's diagonal Mana Step had been the obvious cause.

The Skill had moved him.

His body had paid for the bad landing.

"Can I practice the mana portion without stepping?"

Megan narrowed her eyes.

"Define practice."

"Standing still."

"That is not a definition."

"Moving mana through the pattern without releasing the impulse."

"Will it hurt your hip?"

"No."

"Do you know that?"

Lucas opened his mouth.

Closed it.

"Probably not."

"Then test it slowly. If it hurts, stop."

"That's almost permission."

"It's a conditional threat."

"Still counts."

She looked at the spear.

"And no lunging."

"I didn't ask about lunging."

"You were going to."

Lucas decided not to reward her.

By late afternoon, the Party had separated.

Emily disappeared into a work shelter with three Glassroot fragments and the expression of someone who had forgotten food existed.

He complained that this lacked a measurable limit.

Lucas sat beneath a stretched hide awning with Rachel's notebook on the stone table in front of him.

Thomas sat opposite.

Same dark hair Rachel had worn tied back underground.

Same eyes.

Not identical.

Related enough.

"Lucas."

"I know."

His hands stayed on his knees.

Caleb had agreed.

Finally Caleb pushed the notebook across the table.

"If there's anything personal, skip it."

"Okay."

"If there's anything about what happened down there..."

His jaw tightened.

"Tell me."

He opened the cover.

Rachel's handwriting was compact.

Not neat.

Functional.

Quarry measurements.

Clay locations.

A list of people who owed her favors, followed by increasingly insulting descriptions of what those favors were worth.

Lucas glanced at Caleb.

"She kept accounting records."

Caleb's mouth moved.

Almost a smile.

"She kept leverage."

That sounded more accurate.

Lucas continued.

The dungeon notes began several pages in.

BREACH AIRFLOW: steady outward. colder than surface.

GLASSROOT: responds to mana? Emily thinks yes. Lucas definitely thinks yes but says "maybe" every time.

DO NOT LET EMILY LICK IT.

Lucas stopped.

Thomas leaned forward.

"What?"

Lucas turned the notebook so they could see.

Caleb laughed once.

It broke halfway through.

Lucas waited.

Caleb wiped at his face with the heel of his hand.

"Keep going."

Three routes.

Pulse intervals.

Skitterer movement.

Not truth.

Observation.

Unconfirmed.

Further down:

BROODGUARD favors high route. Doesn't chase past narrow split. Maybe territory. Maybe too big. If forced back from basin, lower shelf might be safer than upper.

Lucas stared at that.

The lower shelf had become their approach after the ledge collapse.

Rachel had seen the possibility before they used it.

He kept reading.

The entries from the second descent became shorter.

More hurried.

One page contained only route marks and numbers.

Then:

PULSE CHANGED.

NOT FASTER. DOUBLE.

Lucas sat straighter.

Thomas noticed.

"Something?"

"Maybe."

"Before the collapse?"

"Looks like it."

Chapter twenty-one's re-entry had shown altered rhythm.

The Broodmother had used pulses.

The network also pulsed independently.

Lucas had assumed the changes represented rising dungeon activity.

That might still be true.

But Rachel had noticed a directional second pulse before the Party knew enough to care.

East and down.

The three-line doorway sat beyond the basin.

Lucas's curiosity woke up.

He immediately told it to sit down.

Not today.

He turned the page.

The last written entry was only six words.

ROOTS MOVED BEFORE GUARD TURNED.

Then:

Not all one system?

Caleb watched him.

"Important?"

"It means Rachel noticed the same problem we did later."

The contacts.

The signaling delays.

The independent tendrils.

"She asked the right question."

"That's different."

"She'd like that answer."

"Copy what you need."

"You sure?"

"Yeah."

"Just don't turn her into a map."

Lucas understood.

Rachel had become route notes in Lucas's Field Record before she became someone with a brother.

"No."

Lucas remained under the awning.
