# Chapter 17
## Beneath Shiverstone

Sarah's idea of a quiet day involved more supervision than Lucas considered medically necessary. He spent the morning inside Hearth. No hunting. No scouting. No running. No testing Kinetic Thrust. No walking to the southern route board, because apparently "checking a map" counted as exercise if the map sat far enough away.

Lucas accepted all of it. Mostly because his thigh hurt less by noon. That was the irritating thing about Sarah. Her rules kept working.

He sat beside Daniel near the northern work area while Daniel repaired a split tool handle with hide wrap and resin.

"You're fidgeting," Daniel said.

"I am sitting."

"Violently."

Lucas looked at his hands. He was turning a short piece of ashwood between his fingers. Fine. Maybe.

The provisional hunting agreement from yesterday had already produced three new arguments, two revisions to Priya's route board, and one passive-aggressive note somebody had written beneath the maintenance section.

USE THE BRIDGE, FIX THE BRIDGE.

Daniel approved of it. Grant did not approve of the handwriting. Lucas approved of both reactions.

"How long before people start charging tolls?" he asked.

Daniel tightened the wrap. "Longer if you stop giving them ideas."

"Good point."

A voice called from the southern gate. Not alarm. Urgent. Different.

Lucas looked over. A woman from Stonefield was being escorted inside. Rachel Moore. Dust covered her boots and pants to the knee. No blood. Good.

She found Grant first. Then Marcus. Then her eyes moved across the work area and landed on Lucas. That was less good.

Rachel walked over. "We found something."

Owen appeared from nowhere. Of course he did.

Lucas looked at him. "Were you hiding behind a building?"

"I heard the sentence."

Rachel ignored them. "Thomas wants people from Hearth to see it before anyone goes inside."

Daniel stopped working. "Inside what?"

Rachel took a breath. "Stonefield opened a hole."

Lucas frowned. "That is not specific enough."

"It was a quarry shelf yesterday."

"And today?"

"A cave."

Owen smiled. "Better."

Rachel shook her head. "No. Not better."

That changed the mood. Grant joined them. "What happened?"

Rachel explained. Stonefield had been widening one of their shallow extraction cuts along the exposed rock shelf east of the settlement. The stone there fractured in clean plates. Good building material. Yesterday afternoon, workers broke through a thinner wall than expected. Behind it was empty space. Not a pocket. A cavern. They stopped.

This morning Thomas sent two scouts to inspect the opening without entering more than a few yards. Both came back. One had found something glassy growing through the rock. The other had used Identify.

Rachel looked at Lucas. "It called it Glassroot."

Emily arrived halfway through the word. "Called what Glassroot?"

Rachel almost laughed. "A crystal-looking root."

Emily's expression became predatory. Grant pointed at her. "No."

"I didn't say anything."

"You were going to."

"I was going to ask a scientific question."

"You were going to volunteer."

Emily considered. "Both can be true."

Rachel pulled a wrapped object from her pack and opened the cloth. A fragment rested inside. Six inches long. Translucent. Not clear. Milky gray with pale blue threads running through it. It looked organic in shape and mineral in substance.

Lucas leaned closer. Identify.

Glassroot Fragment

F-Rank Material

Mana-responsive mineral-organic structure.

Harvest state: Detached / Damaged

Additional properties unavailable.

Emily whispered, "Oh."

Grant looked at her. "That sound is why you're not going alone."

She ignored him.

Lucas reached toward the fragment. "Can I?"

Rachel nodded. He touched it. Mana sense responded immediately. Not strongly. Cleanly. The blue threads inside the fragment held mana differently from stone. Closer to Channelwood. But rigid. Structured.

Iterative Insight sharpened the contrast. He could tell the material had pathways. He could not tell what they did. Good.

"It conducts," Lucas said.

Emily looked at him. "Like your spear?"

"Different. Maybe."

Daniel leaned closer. "Natural?"

Lucas shrugged. "Identify says mineral-organic."

Owen nodded. "So rock plant."

Emily glared at him. "That is offensively bad."

"Still accurate."

Rachel rewrapped it. "Thomas also found marks at the opening."

"What kind?" Priya asked from behind them. Apparently everyone had now arrived.

"System marks?"

"No. Carved stone."

Lucas's interest doubled. Ancient? Maybe.

Grant saw his face. "No."

"I didn't say anything."

"Your face did."

Sarah appeared at the edge of the group. Lucas immediately became less enthusiastic. She looked at Rachel. "How far?"

"Stonefield."

Then at Lucas. "No."

Rachel blinked. Lucas sighed. "I had a quiet morning."

"It is noon."

"That's almost a day."

"It is literally not."

Grant stepped in before Lucas could make things worse. "We don't need him today."

Lucas looked at him. Traitor.

Grant continued. "We need information first."

That was correct. Annoying.

Rachel nodded. "Thomas wants a group tomorrow."

Sarah looked at Lucas's leg. "Tomorrow is possible."

Lucas smiled. "See?"

"Possible is not permission."

Less satisfying.

The rest of the day became preparation. Not expedition. Preparation. Lucas hated how useful it was.

Priya updated the Stonefield route. Daniel listed equipment. Rope. Torches. Spare cord. Wood wedges. Water. Cloth. Food. Simple climbing gear made from salvaged belts and braided fiber. Emily prepared sample wraps and containers.

Grant argued for twelve people. Lucas argued against it. "Underground, twelve people means twelve ways to block a passage."

Marcus crossed his arms. "Eight?"

"Party limit."

Grant frowned. "That doesn't mean the System knows best."

"No. It means eight gives us formal contribution tracking and basic status."

Daniel nodded. "Also manageable."

They chose eight. Lucas. Owen. Marcus. Tessa. Emily. Rachel. Daniel. Megan.

The last name created debate. Megan was still a Healer Initiate. Useful. Limited. Her early Skill helped clotting and minor tissue repair. It could not regrow organs or erase trauma. Sarah made that point three times. Megan made it a fourth.

"I'm medical support," she said. "Not immortality."

Owen nodded solemnly. "Disappointing."

Sarah looked at him. "Would you like a demonstration of ordinary medicine?"

"No."

Good decision.

The expedition would leave at first light. Stonefield would provide a staging area. Nobody would enter beyond the first chamber until the site was identified and escape routes were confirmed. Grant wanted a fixed turn-back time. Lucas agreed.

Grant noticed. "You're becoming reasonable."

"Don't spread that around."

By evening, Lucas's thigh had settled completely. Sarah checked it. No fresh swelling. No heat. No reopening. The scar line looked ugly. Functional.

"Tomorrow," she said.

Lucas waited.

"You can go."

"That sounded painful."

"It was."

"No running unless necessary?"

Sarah stared. Lucas nodded. "Understood."

She pointed at the Channelwood Spear. "And you are not using the dungeon as an excuse to prove your leg works."

"That seems oddly specific."

"You have patterns."

Lucas had no defense.

They left before sunrise. The route to Stonefield was familiar now. That mattered. Field Record held the path. Wet channels. Low ridge. Southern marks. Lowbank turnoff. Bog route. Ravine crossing. Stonefield trail.

But this time they used the newer northwest connection. The Ironbound timber route shaved distance. Neutral markers had already replaced two faction slashes at major junctions. The Ironbound marks remained at the actual timber stand. Small change. Meaningful.

Marcus noticed Lucas noticing. "Happy?"

"Moderately."

"Try not to injure yourself."

"That almost sounded caring."

"It wasn't."

Their relationship was improving. Terrible development.

They reached Stonefield before midday. Thomas Reed waited at the quarry with six people. The quarry was more impressive than Lucas remembered. Stonefield had cut shallow terraces into the exposed gray shelf. Not with advanced equipment. Levers. Hammers. Fire. Wedges. Labor. Humanity remained annoyingly effective when deprived of power tools.

The new opening sat at the rear of the cut. Ten feet wide. Seven high. Broken stone lay around it. Cold air moved from within.

Emily immediately stared at the walls. Thin pale veins ran through the rock. Glassroot. Not the thick fragment Rachel had brought. Hair-thin here. Growing denser around the opening.

Lucas used Identify.

Glassroot Vein

F-Rank Material Formation

Mana-responsive mineral-organic growth.

Depth: Identification unavailable.

Thomas pointed at the carved stone Rachel had mentioned. It sat partly exposed at the left edge of the breach. Not part of Stonefield's quarry face. Worked. Dark.

Lucas's stomach tightened. Daniel brushed dust away. A vertical band of symbols appeared. Not the Three-Line Waystation language. At least Lucas did not think so. These marks were simpler. Geometric. Repeated circles with descending lines.

Identify.

Sealed Boundary Marker

F-Rank Construct

Condition: Breached / Inactive

Purpose: Threshold designation

Language: Unrecognized

Owen looked at the hole. "Sealed."

"It says boundary," Lucas replied.

"That is not better."

Thomas folded his arms. "We didn't know it was there."

"No blame," Grant would have said. Lucas meant it. The marker had been inside rock. Stonefield had broken through from the side.

Daniel examined the fracture. "Wasn't a door."

"No," Thomas said. "We punched through a wall."

Lucas looked at the cavern. That mattered. They had not found an intended entrance. They had made one.

The group formed a Party. Eight names appeared.

Lucas Mercer.

Owen Brooks.

Marcus Reed.

Tessa Ward.

Emily Carter.

Rachel Moore.

Daniel Cross.

Megan.

Basic status indicators. No health bars. No glowing tactical interface. Just enough to know if someone dropped from functional to injured. Lucas liked limited information. It forced people to pay attention.

They entered. Ten feet. Twenty. The outside light weakened. Torches came up.

The passage was not natural. Not entirely. The cavern walls curved irregularly, but stone had been cut in places. Old support arches appeared every thirty yards. Some broken. Some intact. Glassroot spread through everything. At first thin veins. Then finger-thick strands. Pale blue. Milky white. Some crossed the walls like frozen roots. Others hung from the ceiling.

Mana moved through them. Slow. Not current. Pulse.

Lucas stopped. "Do you feel that?" Emily whispered.

"Yes."

"What?" Owen asked.

"Mana."

"Helpful."

Lucas closed his eyes. The Glassroot around them responded in different rhythms. Not synchronized. Not random either. Iterative Insight sharpened his own comparison. He could feel where mana entered a strand and dispersed. Not why. Not source. Just pattern.

Field Record captured the physical route. Broken arch. Left bend. Narrow section. Glassroot cluster. Observed facts layered cleanly. Explorer and Iterative Insight. Different tools. Working together.

Thirty yards deeper, the tunnel opened. Everyone stopped.

The chamber beyond was enormous. Their torchlight did not reach the far wall. Glassroot did. It spread across the cavern in thick luminous structures. Some as wide as Lucas's arm. Some thicker than trees. They descended from the ceiling and climbed from the floor, crossing through stone shelves and disappearing into darkness. Blue-white light pulsed faintly inside them. The chamber glittered without being bright.

Beautiful. That made Lucas suspicious.

System notification appeared.

REGIONAL DUNGEON ENVIRONMENT DISCOVERED

GLASSROOT DEPTHS

Rank: F

Status: Active

Entry route: Nonstandard Breach

Recommended preparation: Unavailable

Dungeon depth: Unavailable

Lucas read it twice. Dungeon.

Owen said, "I got it."

Marcus nodded. Everyone with the System had. Emily smiled. Daniel did not.

"Nonstandard breach."

"Meaning?" Rachel asked.

"We came through the wall," Daniel said. "Very professionally."

Megan looked at Lucas. "Active?"

"Yes."

No one moved forward. Good group. The System had named the place. That did not make it safe.

A second notification appeared.

DUNGEON PARTICIPATION CONDITIONS AVAILABLE

Formal dungeon participation begins upon meaningful entry beyond the threshold chamber.

Withdrawal remains possible where physically achievable.

No resurrection protections detected.

Owen's smile disappeared. "That last sentence felt unnecessary."

"No," Megan said. "It didn't."

Permanent death. Still permanent. Dungeon did not mean game.

Lucas looked across the chamber. The threshold area sloped downward in three visible routes. Left followed a shelf beside a glassroot trunk. Center descended through broken stone. Right passed beneath an arch coated in pale growth. None were marked safe. None were marked dangerous. Perfect.

Emily crouched near a small Glassroot protrusion. "Don't touch," Rachel said.

"I'm looking."

"Your looking uses hands."

Emily sighed. "Oppressive expedition."

Lucas used Identify on a bulbous growth near the floor.

Glassroot Node

F-Rank Dungeon Resource

Harvest difficulty: Moderate

Disturbance response: Unknown

Emily stood. "Fine."

Very fine. Resource. Dungeon resource. Stonefield had broken into something potentially valuable. The reason to enter had just become obvious. Materials. Progression. Maybe ancient knowledge. Maybe danger. Definitely danger.

Daniel pointed at the old arches. "These aren't Stonefield's."

"No."

"Same builders as the road?"

Lucas studied the symbols cut into one support. Not enough. "Unknown."

Field Record kept it unknown. Good.

Then they heard clicking. Soft. Far away.

Everyone froze. Tessa raised her bow. Marcus shifted his weight. Lucas lowered the spear point toward the center route.

The clicking came again. Multiple. Not close. Something moved beyond a Glassroot trunk. Low. Fast. Too far for Identify.

Owen whispered, "We leave."

Nobody argued. That pleased Lucas almost as much as the dungeon discovery.

They withdrew in order. Tessa and Owen first back toward the passage. Emily and Megan middle. Daniel and Rachel. Marcus and Lucas last. No creature followed.

At the narrow section Lucas glanced back. For half a second, something crossed the distant blue light. Many-legged. Dog-sized. Gone. No Identify. No assumption.

They exited into sunlight. Thomas looked at their faces. "Well?"

Lucas took a breath. "Dungeon."

Stonefield went quiet. Thomas stared at the breach. "System dungeon?"

"Glassroot Depths. F-Rank. Active. We entered through a nonstandard breach."

Thomas rubbed his beard. "Good?"

"No idea."

Emily held up one finger. "Resources."

Megan held up another. "Things moving inside."

Emily lowered her hand slightly. "Resources with complications."

They spent the next hour setting a controlled perimeter around the breach. Not claiming the dungeon. Lucas made that point immediately. Stonefield had discovered it through quarry labor. That mattered. The entrance physically sat inside Stonefield's resource site. That also mattered. But a dungeon could affect everyone. Creatures could emerge. Resources could reshape trade. Progression could attract people. The route agreement from yesterday suddenly looked small.

Thomas surprised Lucas. "We don't keep this secret."

Rachel looked at him. Thomas shrugged. "If monsters start coming out, secret doesn't help."

Good answer.

Marcus nodded. "Hearth sends fighters."

"Stonefield controls the quarry."

Marcus looked ready to argue. Lucas raised a hand. "Later."

Marcus looked at him. "You're saying that?"

"Growth."

Owen wiped fake tears. Lucas ignored him.

The immediate problem was expedition design. Not politics.

They returned to Hearth before dark. No one descended again. No one harvested Glassroot. No one died. A successful first dungeon encounter by Lucas's current standards.

Grant called the meeting before dinner. Thomas had sent Rachel with them as Stonefield's representative. Priya updated the map. GLASSROOT DEPTHS appeared east of Stonefield. A red circle marked the nonstandard breach. Daniel drew the threshold chamber. Three routes. Unknown.

Emily listed confirmed materials. Glassroot Fragment. Glassroot Vein. Glassroot Node. No properties beyond mana-responsive structure and dungeon-resource status.

Megan listed medical concerns. No known toxins. No known diseases. No known creature attacks. Which meant no assumptions.

Marcus listed combat needs. Frontline. Ranged. Scout. Healer. Rope. Fallback point.

Lucas listened. Then Grant asked the obvious question. "Who goes in?"

Silence. Not because nobody wanted to. Because too many did. The System had changed people quickly. A dungeon meant risk. It also meant advancement. Everyone understood that now.

Grant proposed a temporary expedition. Not Ironbound. Not Freewalker. Not Stonefield. Mixed. That mattered.

The initial eight-person scouting Party would remain the core because they had seen the threshold and already worked together. Lucas. Owen. Marcus. Tessa. Emily. Rachel. Daniel. Megan.

A second support team would remain outside the breach. Stonefield personnel. Two Hearth fighters. Medical supplies. Rope. Food. Nobody else entered unless the expedition failed to return on schedule or called for help. No random people following for experience. No looting race. No faction-exclusive claim.

For once, nobody argued much. The Dungeon itself was persuasive.

Sarah stood near the back. Lucas avoided eye contact. She walked forward anyway. "Leg."

"Fine."

"That was not a question."

He sat. She checked it. Mild swelling. No reopening. No deep heat. The quiet day had mattered. She looked unhappy. But not prohibitive.

"You can go tomorrow."

Lucas blinked. "Who are you?"

Sarah pointed at him. "If you run on that leg because you see a shiny root, I will personally drag you out."

Emily said, "That threat seems aimed at both of us."

"It is."

Fair.

Later, after the meeting, Lucas opened his interface.

Human — Lv. 3

Progress: 98%

Mana Spearman — Lv. 4

Progress: 8%

Explorer — Lv. 2

Progress: 94%

Almost. The dungeon discovery had moved both Race and Profession hard. No Class. Correct. No combat.

He stared at Human ninety-eight. Then Explorer ninety-four. Two thresholds waiting. Not earned yet. Good.

Six Attribute Points remained. He considered spending. Endurance for the dungeon. Perception. Wisdom. Vitality. Everything had an argument. He did nothing. Fear before entering unknown terrain was not evidence. Tomorrow would provide evidence. That was either discipline or a terrible personality trait. Probably both.

Field Record held the route to Stonefield. The quarry. The breach. The entrance tunnel. The threshold chamber. Three unexplored paths. The Skill ended there. Black beyond observation.

Lucas liked that black. Not because he wanted danger. Because unknown territory remained honest.

Owen sat beside him near the fire. "First dungeon."

"Apparently."

"You excited?"

Lucas thought about the clicking. The moving shape. The active Glassroot. The old arches. The System's reminder that no resurrection protection existed.

"Yes."

Owen smiled. "Same."

"Also concerned."

"Same."

"Those should probably coexist."

"Probably why Grant isn't letting Marcus lead alone."

Marcus, six feet away, looked over. "I heard that."

Owen nodded. "Good."

Marcus threw a piece of bark at him. Lucas smiled.

The temporary expedition existed. Tomorrow they would cross the threshold deliberately. Not a quarry accident. Not a scouting peek. An expedition. The Glassroot Depths waited beneath Shiverstone. And for the first time since the Accession, Lucas was about to enter a place the System itself called a dungeon.

No respawns. No guarantees. No glowing arrow pointing toward the correct path. Good. He had never trusted easy directions anyway.

The expedition plan took another hour to become something Lucas would trust with his life. That was not hyperbole anymore.

Grant wanted check-in times. Daniel wanted load limits. Megan wanted everyone to understand exactly what "medical support" meant before adrenaline translated it into "miracle worker." Rachel wanted Stonefield's people to control the breach itself because their quarry crews understood the rock around it. Marcus wanted a clear combat order. Owen wanted two retreat routes. Tessa wanted firing lanes. Emily wanted samples.

Everyone looked at Emily. She raised both hands. "Samples acquired under controlled conditions."

"Better," Megan said.

Priya drew the threshold chamber again. Three routes. Left shelf. Center slope. Right arch. No one had explored beyond the point where the clicking began.

Lucas marked the last confirmed visual contact. "Something crossed here."

Marcus leaned over. "Size?"

"Dog-sized."

"Level?"

"Unknown."

"Number?"

"Unknown."

"Species?"

Lucas looked at him. Marcus sighed. "Right."

Owen tapped the map. "Clicking came from more than one source."

"Probably," Lucas said.

"Probably?"

"I heard multiple intervals. Could still have been one animal moving."

Owen nodded.

The room went quiet for a moment. This was what Lucas liked about the group. Nobody turned incomplete information into certainty just because certainty was more comfortable.

Daniel pointed at the center slope. "Loose rock?"

"Some."

"Then center isn't our first choice."

Marcus frowned. "Why?"

"Because eight people retreating uphill over loose stone becomes eight people falling on each other."

"Left?"

"Better visibility."

Tessa agreed. The left shelf became the tentative first route. Tentative. Lucas liked that word almost as much as probably.

Priya drew a thick line along it and stopped where their knowledge stopped. "No imaginary continuation," she said.

Lucas smiled. "You understand Field Record spiritually."

"I understand you people keep trying to make maps lie."

Fair.

Grant set three expedition rules. Nobody separated beyond voice range. Nobody harvested an unknown resource unless the group had enough time and safety to observe the response. Nobody continued deeper after the agreed turn-back point regardless of how interesting the next chamber looked.

Emily objected only to the tone of the second rule. Owen objected to nothing, which made Lucas suspicious.

"What are you planning?"

"Nothing."

"That's worse."

Owen grinned.

Marcus leaned against the wall. "What if we find a boss?"

Lucas looked at him. "Then we leave."

Marcus smiled. "You don't know what it is."

"Exactly."

"What if it's Level Five?"

"Then we decide after we know that."

"What if it's Level Three?"

"Then we decide after we know that."

Marcus shook his head. "You've made caution annoying."

"Skill issue."

Owen laughed hard enough to choke. Even Grant smiled.

The humor helped. Not because anyone had forgotten the danger. Because everyone remembered it. The Hollow Reach had taught them that fear worked best when it did not get to own the room.

After the meeting, Daniel showed Lucas the equipment pile. Two coils of rope. Wood wedges. Three torches per person, though most would carry one active at a time. A small hammer. Cloth wraps. Water. Dried food. Two spare spear shafts for the group, not System weapons, just straight ashwood poles with hardened tips. A crude stretcher bundle.

Lucas stared at the stretcher. Daniel noticed. "Better to carry it in and not need it."

"Agreed."

That sentence felt heavier than it should have. Dungeon casualties belonged to Chapter Nineteen in the outline Lucas did not consciously know existed. The world did not care about outlines. The stretcher was real.

Megan checked the medical kit. Clean cloth. Boiled cord. A small amount of alcohol somebody had arrived with during the Accession, now treated as precious antiseptic rather than beverage. Poultices Sarah trusted enough not to insult. No potions. No instant healing. No convenient reset.

Lucas looked at Megan. "You nervous?"

"Yes."

"Good."

She raised an eyebrow. "That's a strange response."

"I'd worry more if you weren't."

Megan considered. "Then yes. Very competent terror."

"Excellent."

Outside, Stonefield's messenger fire had been lit near the southern board. A runner would leave before sunrise to confirm the expedition start. Another runner from Stonefield would meet them halfway if anything changed overnight. Human communication. No global chat. No magical party invitation across miles. Fire. Feet. Planning. Lucas found that grounding.

He returned to Priya's map one final time. The Glassroot Depths sat beneath a black circle east of Stonefield. The symbol was new. Priya had drawn branching lines inside it.

"Too artistic," Lucas said.

"It needs to be recognizable."

"It looks like a haunted turnip."

Priya stared at him. "Draw your own."

Lucas considered the charcoal. "No."

"Then it stays."

Haunted turnip it was.

Field Record held a much less insulting version in his memory. The route from Hearth to Stonefield. The quarry terraces. The exact breach. The old threshold marker. The tunnel sequence. The chamber. The three visible descents. The point where the unidentified shape crossed the Glassroot light.

He tried once more to imagine what waited beyond. Nothing answered. No Skill. No intuition masquerading as prophecy. Just possibility. That mattered.

Iterative Insight could help him compare techniques after he had something to compare. Field Record could organize routes after he had traveled them. Mana Spearman could help him survive a fight after a fight began. None of them replaced the first step into the unknown.

Lucas sat by the fire and looked at the Channelwood Spear across his knees. The small chip in the spearhead caught orange light. The weapon had survived the Ravager. It had not defeated the Ravager. That distinction remained useful.

He ran mana through the shaft once. Low intensity. Clean flow. No Kinetic Thrust. No shoulder pain. No thigh pain. Sarah would approve. Probably.

He dismissed the mana. Tomorrow would not be about proving he was stronger than the dungeon. That would be stupid. Tomorrow was about learning what kind of place the Glassroot Depths were before the Depths taught them the expensive way.

For once, Lucas hoped the first lesson was boring. He knew better than to expect that. Still, boring had acquired a reputation Lucas thought was unfair. Boring meant everyone came back. Boring meant Megan kept her supplies. Boring meant Daniel's stretcher remained an awkward bundle of poles instead of becoming necessary. Boring meant the first dungeon map contained notes instead of names crossed out.

That was not cowardice. It was a successful expedition.

Lucas looked across Hearth. Fires burned low. Watch rotations changed. Someone argued quietly over dried meat portions. Ordinary problems continued under an impossible sky.

Tomorrow, eight people would walk beneath Stonefield into an active dungeon. Tonight, Lucas allowed ordinary to matter.

Then he stood, tested the leg once, and went to sleep before curiosity could invent another task.
