# Chapter 3
## The Shape of Mana

Lucas woke with his left shoulder keeping time with his heartbeat. For a few seconds he stayed still under the pale morning light, staring at the silver-edged leaves and trying to remember why the ground had become his mattress. Then something boomed in the distance, and the memory arrived all at once: the Accession, the Hollow Reach, Bark Hounds, Needle Rats, and the ridiculous little number parked in the back of his mind.

Human — Lv. 1
Progress: 90%

Lucas groaned and sat up. His shoulder filed a complaint. “So much for magical healing.” “You don’t have magical healing.” Sarah, from his right. Lucas turned. She sat beside one of the injured men from the first day, changing a bandage around his calf. The wound looked angry, cleaner than he expected, though. “Technicality,” Lucas said. “It’s an important one.” “How’s he doing?” “Better than yesterday.” The injured man gave Lucas a tired thumbs-up. Sarah tied off the cloth, stood, and walked over. “Move the arm.” Lucas raised it. Pain started around shoulder height. Sarah stopped him. “Don’t force it.” “Can I use a spear?” “You can do whatever you want. Whether you should is a different question.” “That sounds suspiciously like doctor language.” “Nurse language. Doctors get paid more to say the same thing.” Lucas smiled. Sarah’s expression softened for half a second, then she pointed at him. “You get hit there again, you’re going to make this worse.” “I’ll add it to my strategic plan.” “You have one?” “Try not to get bitten.” “Groundbreaking.”

Lucas stood. The camp had changed overnight—not dramatically, no walls, no proper shelters, no civilization popping out of the grass like a miracle—but there were systems now. Water containers clustered near the fire pit, a rough medical area, a pile of usable branches that had become something like a communal weapons rack, and someone had marked four watch directions with fabric strips tied to stakes. Daniel was already organizing a group around a crude barricade. Grant stood nearby, talking with three people Lucas did not recognize. Farther off, Emily knelt over the red-leaf plants from the stream. The chaos of the first arrival had started turning into structure. Not safety. Structure.

Lucas retrieved his ashwood spear. The point had dulled on the Bark Hound. He sat on a fallen log and used his utility knife to sharpen it again. Owen wandered over carrying two cups made from cut plastic bottles. “Breakfast.” Lucas looked inside. Something gray floated in boiled water. “What is it?” “Food.” “That was not the question.” “Someone had oatmeal.” Lucas drank. It tasted like wet cardboard with ambition. “Luxury.” “Enjoy it. The restaurant closes when the packet does.” They ate in silence for a while. Then Owen nodded toward the tree line. “Grant wants another water run.” “Of course he does.” “We’re burning through it faster than expected.” “Hundreds of people.” “Exactly.”

Lucas checked the camp. More people had arrived during the night—small groups, some from other clearings. One group of fourteen had come in just before dawn after following smoke. That changed something. The arrival clearings were connected. People could move between them, which meant information would eventually move too, and conflict, probably conflict first. Grant called everyone near the center after sunrise. Lucas and Owen joined. “We have three priorities,” Grant said. “Water. Food. Perimeter.” Daniel added, “And shelter before weather decides to get creative.” Grant nodded reluctantly. “Four priorities.” A few people laughed. Grant ignored it. “We’ve got reports from two nearby groups. One clearing is maybe three miles north. Another farther southeast. Both saw monsters overnight. One lost five people.” The clearing went quiet. Lucas watched faces change. Five deaths turned rumor into arithmetic. Grant continued. “They also reported something else. People are gaining levels.” That brought the noise back—questions, shouts. Someone asked what level. Someone else asked how. Eric got dragged into the discussion because he was still one of the few confirmed Level 2 humans in their group. Lucas stepped back. He did not need another argument about whether hunting monsters was smart. The answer depended entirely on which monster. A Level 2 Needle Rat near the perimeter? Maybe. A Level 7 Mossback Ape eating plants beside a stream? Absolutely not. Except someone in camp would eventually decide Level 7 meant better rewards. The System had built a ladder. Humans were going to climb it, even if some of the rungs had teeth.

Lucas checked Progression again. Ninety percent. Close enough to feel intentional. Too close to ignore. He hated that. Owen noticed. “Still ninety?” “Yeah.” “You going looking for ten?” “No.” “That answer sounded painful.” “I’m trying to grow as a person.” “Race progress joke?” Lucas looked at him. “Accidental.” “Shame. It was almost good.” They were assigned to the second water team. This time Grant sent ten people: Lucas, Owen, Daniel, Emily, Eric, Janet with the hatchet, two scouts, and two additional volunteers carrying improvised clubs. No one objected. More people meant less water carried per person, but more eyes and weapons. The Bark Hound corpse from yesterday had already been stripped by scavengers—almost nothing left but bones, blood, and bits of hide. Needle Rat tracks were everywhere. Lucas identified them automatically now, not because the System had changed, but because he knew what to look for. That distinction mattered.

They entered the forest. The morning air was cool under the ashwood canopy. Lucas kept his spear point low. His shoulder made every movement slightly awkward, so he compensated by putting more weight on his right arm. Not ideal. The forest did not care. They reached the stream without incident again. Lucas distrusted repeated good luck. Water collection started. Emily moved toward the red-leaf patch she had sampled the day before. “Same plants?” Lucas asked. “Yes.” “Learn anything?” “Not much. Identify calls it Crimson Veinleaf.” Lucas tried.

“What are you doing with it?” “Testing.” “That word gets dangerous around here.” Emily gave him a dry look. “I’m not eating it.” “Strong start.” “I crushed one leaf yesterday. It smells metallic. Slight color change in hot water. No obvious reaction with ashwood bark.” Lucas blinked. “You’re doing chemistry with leaves and sticks.” “I’m doing what I can.” He respected that. The System had taken laboratories, equipment, books, every tool modern specialists depended on. It had not taken curiosity.

Lucas looked toward the ridge. The Mossback Ape was not there. Something else was—movement behind the trees, smaller, too small for the ape. “Hold.” Everyone stopped. Lucas raised a fist without thinking. Grant would have approved. That irritated him. The movement came again, low and fast. Identify.

Lucas’s stomach tightened. Not a Bark Hound. This one was leaner, longer, gray hide instead of brown, and thin ridges of bone or keratin running along its forelegs. One hound. Then a second appeared, Level 5. A third, Level 4. Owen whispered, “We backing up?” “Yes.” No debate. They had water. They had no reason to fight. The group tightened formation and moved away from the stream. The Razor Hounds followed—not charging, pacing. Lucas hated intelligent predators. They made fear feel personal. The hounds spread: one left, one right, one behind. “They’re herding us,” Daniel said. Lucas saw it. They were being pushed off the route back to camp. “Don’t let them choose direction.” “How?” Lucas looked ahead—thick brush to the south, open ground northeast. The hounds wanted them northeast. Why? He searched: trees, rock, brush, nothing obvious. Then he heard it, a faint growl ahead. More hounds. “Stop.” Everyone froze. Lucas pointed. “More in front.” Owen listened. His face changed. “Yeah.” Ambush. The pack was larger than the three they could see. Lucas’s heart picked up. Grant’s rule echoed in his head: see something above your ability, come back. Wonderful advice. They were trying. The animals had other plans.

Daniel looked at Lucas. “Options?” Lucas almost laughed. Why was he asking him? Because Lucas had noticed the trap. That did not mean he knew how to leave it. He studied the terrain: stream behind, dense brush south, hounds north and east, west led uphill toward the ridge where the Level 7 ape had been. The ape. Lucas looked at the red-leaf plants. No. Different direction. Wait. Yesterday the Mossback Ape had ignored them completely while eating. Territorial? Maybe. The Razor Hounds had stayed away from the stream yesterday. Had they? They had only attacked halfway back. Lucas looked at the stream—open stones, water, no cover. Predators liked cover. “Back to the water.” Daniel frowned. “That’s away from camp.” “Better than into the pack.” Nobody had a better idea. They retreated toward the stream. The hounds closed. One darted forward. Eric swung the shovel. The animal stopped outside range, testing. Another moved toward Emily. Lucas shifted between them. The hound stared at him with pale yellow eyes, different from Bark Hounds, sharper. Lucas felt something strange—not fear, under the fear, pressure, a faint humming sensation behind his sternum. He almost dismissed it as adrenaline. Then the hound stepped closer and the pressure strengthened. Lucas inhaled. The sensation moved—no, not moved; he noticed it moving. Something warm flowed from the center of his chest into his right arm, faint, almost imaginary. Lucas blinked. The feeling vanished. The hound lunged. Lucas braced. The spear point dipped too late. Bone ridges on the creature’s foreleg struck the shaft. The wood jerked sideways. The hound slipped past the point. Lucas twisted. Teeth caught his shirt. Fabric tore. He slammed the butt of the spear into the animal’s ribs. Owen struck from the other side. The hound retreated. “Lucas?” “I’m fine.” He was not thinking about the shirt. The warmth had returned—chest, arm, hand. The spear? No. It stopped at his palm. He could feel it, not with skin, not exactly, a pressure beneath sensation, like knowing where his hand was with his eyes closed, except this thing had not existed yesterday, or he had not noticed it.

Another hound charged Daniel. Lucas watched. The sensation flared again, this time not in Lucas but around Daniel—a faint shimmer? No visual light, something deeper, a distortion he could not see and somehow perceived anyway. Daniel swung the hammer. The hound dodged. Lucas stared. “What?” Owen snapped. “Nothing.” “Great time for nothing.” Lucas backed toward the stream. They reached the stones. The pack stopped at the tree line. Five hounds visible now, maybe more. They did not enter the open bank. Interesting. Emily whispered, “They don’t like the water?” “Or they don’t like something here.” Lucas looked around: red-leaf plants, stream, open ground, rocks. No ape. The pressure in his chest pulsed again. Lucas focused on it. Nothing happened. He closed his eyes for half a second—bad idea—opened them. The sensation remained. Think. What changed? Danger. Combat. Proximity to monsters. No. He had fought before. The feeling had not shown up. Level progress? Ninety percent. Maybe nearing a threshold changed perception. Maybe the environment near the stream had higher mana. He focused on the nearest hound. Identify returned the same information: Razor Hound — Lv. 5. But beneath the words, that pressure existed, a faint density around the creature. Lucas concentrated. The sensation sharpened. For one second the hound became more than shape and movement. He felt something inside it—a current, fast, restless, circling through limbs and chest. Then pain stabbed behind Lucas’s eyes. He lost it. “Whoa.” Owen caught his arm. “You okay?” Lucas pressed fingers to his temple. “Yeah.” “That did not look like yeah.” “I think I felt something.” “What?” Lucas looked at the hounds. “Mana.” Nobody spoke. Daniel’s expression changed first. “You’re serious?” “I don’t know.” “Excellent.” “I know what it felt like.” “What did it feel like?” Lucas searched for language. “Pressure. Heat. Movement. Not physical.” Emily stared at him. “Can you do it again?” “Hopefully when fewer things are trying to eat us.”

One of the Razor Hounds stepped onto the stones. The others stayed back. The lead animal lowered its head. Lucas felt the current inside it again, faint but there. It gathered toward the legs. Lucas’s eyes widened. “Move!” The hound exploded forward, faster than before, not ordinary speed. The animal crossed the stones in a blur. Lucas shoved Emily aside. The hound hit him. Pain tore through his bruised shoulder. He fell hard. Something snapped inside his vision.

Lucas gasped. For an instant every sensation intensified: the ground under his fingers, the air in his lungs, the pulse of blood through his shoulder, and the mana. It was suddenly obvious—not visible, present, everywhere. Thin in the air, denser in living bodies, moving through the Razor Hound above him like liquid electricity. The creature opened its jaws. Lucas saw the flow gather through its neck and forelegs. He reacted before the muscles moved, rolled. The hound’s teeth snapped where his throat had been. Lucas drove the spear upward. The point struck the creature’s underside, not deep enough. The hound recoiled. Daniel hit it. Eric’s shovel connected with its rear leg. The animal screamed. The other Razor Hounds surged forward. Bad. Very bad. “Back across the stream!” Owen shouted. They splashed into the water. Lucas stumbled after them. Cold water hit his boots. The pack followed to the edge and stopped. The injured hound tried to continue. Its paw touched the stream and recoiled violently—not fear, pain. Lucas felt the mana around the water, different, dense, flowing, not just downstream but through the stones and the roots along the bank. The stream itself carried something the hounds hated. “Water,” Lucas breathed. Daniel looked at him. “Yes, Lucas. Excellent observation.” “No. Mana in the water.” That shut him up. Lucas crouched and touched the stream with his fingers—cold, wet, and underneath that, movement, a current inside the current, smooth, cool, stable, nothing like the jagged restless flow inside the hounds. A notification appeared.

Conscious mana perception achieved.
System-recognized magical development recorded.

Lucas stared. Another line followed.

CLASS INITIATION REQUIREMENTS MET.
Class selection available.

His heart stopped for half a beat. Not now. He dismissed the notification. Daniel saw his expression. “What?” “Class.” Every head turned. “What about it?” Eric asked. “I unlocked selection.” Silence. Even the hounds across the stream seemed temporarily less important. Owen grinned. “You got a Class?” “Selection.” “What choices?” “I haven’t looked.” “Why not?” Lucas pointed at the five predators staring across the stream. “Scheduling conflict.” Daniel laughed once. “Fair.”

They moved upstream. The hounds followed along the opposite bank, unable or unwilling to cross. That gave the group a corridor. They stayed in the water despite soaked boots. Lucas experimented while walking. He focused on the stream. Mana. The word fit now, not because the System had labeled it, but because nothing else did. He could sense it as a layer under physical sensation, faint enough that distraction erased it, strong enough that concentration brought it back. Air mana felt diffuse. Water mana felt structured. Living mana pulsed. His own—Lucas turned attention inward and almost tripped. Owen grabbed him. “Walking first. Enlightenment second.” “Noted.” Lucas kept moving, but he had felt it: mana inside himself, a small reservoir or network centered somewhere below his sternum, threads spread through his body. Most were dim, unused. When he tightened his grip on the spear, mana moved slightly toward his hands—not because he commanded it, but because intent influenced it. Lucas nearly stopped again. Intent. The System descriptions for Wisdom mentioned control and efficiency. Intelligence mentioned capacity and magical complexity. Those had sounded like game mechanics yesterday. Now they described something real inside him.

The hounds eventually stopped following. The group waited another hundred yards before leaving the stream. Nobody celebrated, not yet. They still had to get home. The return route took longer. Everyone was exhausted. Lucas’s shoulder hurt badly now. Sarah was going to be thrilled. They reached camp near midday. People rushed toward the water containers. Grant intercepted them. “What happened?” “Razor Hounds,” Daniel said. “Five minimum. Water repelled them.” “Repelled?” “Ask Lucas.” Grant looked at him. Lucas hated becoming the person people looked at. “I think the stream carries mana they don’t tolerate.” Grant’s eyes narrowed. “You think?” “I felt it.” “You can sense mana?” “Yes.” “How?” Lucas considered the question. “I almost got eaten.” Grant stared. “That’s not useful.” “It’s accurate.” Owen stepped in. “He leveled too.” Grant’s attention sharpened. Lucas opened Status.

Racial growth had applied evenly. Five free points. He closed the screen before the discussion could turn into a crowd lecture. Sarah checked his shoulder and told him he was lucky it hadn’t dislocated. Lucas spent the afternoon resting the arm while practicing with the spear under Owen’s watchful eye, testing how mana moved with different grips and stances. When he imagined something he had no idea how to do, nothing happened. When he reproduced the brace that had killed the Bark Hound—rear foot set, hips aligned, point lowered—the mana gathered faintly through his legs, core, shoulder, and hands. Not much, but the pattern was repeatable. Changing his front hand position made the physical movement feel worse and the mana flow messier. Changing it back cleaned it up. Different grip, worse. Back again, better. He stopped. No notification appeared. That almost pleased him more than receiving one would have. The System had not handed him an answer. It was letting him observe. Learn. Maybe that was enough for now.

At sunset Lucas stood at the edge of camp with the spear in his right hand. His shoulder still hurt. His boots were still damp. The forest still looked dangerous. But the world had changed again. Yesterday, monsters had levels. Today, they had mana. Tomorrow, Lucas would choose what kind of fighter he intended to become. He opened Progression one last time.

Seven percent already—from the Razor Hound fight, from sensing mana, from surviving. He closed the screen. For the first time since the Accession, Lucas did not feel like the System was simply happening to him. He had reached a choice. That was different. And choices, he had learned long before the world ended, were where people revealed what they actually valued. Tomorrow, he would reveal his.
