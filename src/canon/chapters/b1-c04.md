# Chapter 4
## The First Choice

Lucas did not choose a Class when he woke up. That fact irritated almost everyone who knew he had the option. Grant asked before breakfast. Owen asked during breakfast. Eric asked after breakfast. A woman Lucas barely knew asked while he was sharpening his spear. By the fourth time, Lucas considered selecting Warrior out of spite. He did not.

The Class interface hovered at the edge of his awareness with four choices: Warrior, Spearman, Mana Initiate, and Mana Spearman. Three Common. One Uncommon. The obvious answer sat there glowing with all the subtlety of a billboard. Lucas distrusted obvious answers. He also distrusted people who distrusted obvious answers merely because they were obvious. Sometimes the obvious answer was obvious because it was correct. The problem was figuring out which kind of obvious he was dealing with.

“Still thinking?” Owen asked. Lucas looked up from the spear point. “Still asking?” “Yes.” “Then yes.” Owen sat on the log beside him. The camp had grown louder since dawn. More people had arrived from neighboring clearings. News traveled with them. So did injuries. One group had lost two people to something they called a Hookbeak, a birdlike monster large enough to carry a child. Another group had found edible berries. A third had discovered that one of their people had unlocked a Class after killing several Needle Rats. Lucas had been waiting for exactly that kind of information.

“What’d the guy get?” he asked. Owen had already tracked down the rumor. “Hunter. Common. Two others got Warrior. One woman got Scout.” “Any unusual choices?” “Apparently the Hunter also had Ranger.” “Common?” “Both Common.” Lucas nodded. Behavior shaped options. No surprise. Still useful confirmation. “What about Skills?” Lucas asked. Owen grinned. “Knew that was next.” “Well?” “Warrior guy got something called Weapon Familiarity and another called Physical Reinforcement.” Lucas stopped sharpening. “Physical Reinforcement?” “His words.” “What does it do?” “Makes him stronger for a short time.” “Cost?” “He said stamina.” “Not mana?” “Apparently not.” Interesting. Lucas stored that away. “What did the Hunter get?” “Tracking Sense.” “Only one Skill?” “Only one he admitted to.” Reasonable. Information was already becoming currency. Some people would share. Some would hide. Some would lie.

Lucas opened his Class screen again.

Mana Spearman
Uncommon Class
A hybrid combat path integrating spear technique with mana development.
Qualification recognized through combined martial adaptation and conscious mana perception.

He focused on the rarity label. Uncommon. Additional information appeared. Class rarity reflects structural complexity, developmental potential, and acquisition difficulty. Rarity does not guarantee superiority in all circumstances. Lucas smiled. There it was. Not better. More complex. More potential. Harder to acquire. The System had apparently anticipated humanity’s instinct to treat rarity like a scoreboard. Owen leaned closer. “What?” “Rarity doesn’t mean automatically stronger.” “I could have told you that.” “You told me to pick it because it was Uncommon.” “I contain multitudes.” Lucas snorted.

He focused on Warrior.

Common Class
A foundational combat path emphasizing physical conflict, durability, and martial development.
Estimated developmental compatibility: Moderate.

That line had not appeared yesterday. Lucas frowned. He opened Spearman. Estimated developmental compatibility: High. Mana Initiate. Estimated developmental compatibility: High. Mana Spearman. Estimated developmental compatibility: Very High. That was more useful. The System was not hiding the answer. It was telling him how well each path fit what he had already done.

“Okay,” Lucas said. Owen straightened. “Okay what?” “I’m done waiting.” “You’re picking it?” “Yes.” “Mana Spearman?” “Yes.” “Finally.” “Your patience has been inspiring.” Lucas stood. His shoulder still hurt, less than yesterday, enough that Sarah had allowed him to use the arm carefully, which was not the same as approving his decisions. He moved away from the center of camp, not for privacy exactly—the interface was private—but he wanted room in case selecting a Class did something dramatic. Given the last three days, expecting restraint from reality seemed optimistic.

Daniel noticed him walking toward the edge of the clearing. “Moment of truth?” “Apparently.” Daniel followed. So did Owen. Emily looked up from her plant samples and joined them. Lucas raised an eyebrow. Emily shrugged. “This is data.” “Comforting to know my life choices are peer-reviewed.” Grant arrived last. Of course he did. Lucas ignored the audience. He opened the Class interface.

Lucas read every line. Then he stopped on the last two. Class growth. Actual numbers. Five points per level. That was more than Race. The Class mattered. A lot. “Still alive?” Owen asked. “Reading.” “Rude.” Lucas ignored him. He focused on Mana Reinforcement.

Mana Reinforcement
Channel mana through the body or a wielded object to temporarily improve structural integrity and effective output.
Efficiency, duration, and safe intensity depend on user control, attributes, mastery, and target compatibility.

That sounded powerful. Also vague enough to kill him. He focused on Spear Familiarity.

Spear Familiarity
System-assisted foundational understanding of spear handling, positioning, balance, and common movement patterns.
Does not replace practice, judgment, or physical capability.

Good. No instant mastery. Lucas appreciated that more than he expected. He returned to the confirmation. Select Mana Spearman? YES / NO. He chose yes.

The world did not explode. No beam of light descended from the sky. No voice announced his destiny. For one second, Lucas felt disappointed. Then the mana inside him moved. Every thread he had sensed the day before lit at once—not visually, internally. A warm rush moved from his chest through his shoulders, arms, spine, legs, and hands. His knees almost buckled. Owen caught his right arm. “Lucas?” “I’m good.” He was not sure that was true. The sensation intensified. Mana flowed through pathways he had only barely perceived before. Where it passed, his body felt sharper, more connected. Then came pressure—not pain, growth. Something changed behind his eyes. His breath caught. A notification appeared.

Class acquired.

Initial Class growth applied.

Lucas opened Status.

He stared at the numbers. Three automatic points: Agility, Perception, Intelligence—exactly the Class tendencies suggested. Two free points. His choice. That felt more significant than the Class itself. “What happened?” Grant asked. Lucas told them. Daniel whistled softly. “Five stats from one Class level.” “Three assigned. Two free.” Grant immediately said, “Strength.” Lucas looked at him. “Both?” “You’re using a spear.” Owen shook his head. “Agility.” Emily said, “Wisdom.” Everyone looked at her. “What?” she said. “He nearly gave himself a headache moving mana.” Lucas smiled. “That was where I was leaning.” Grant frowned. “You need physical power.” “I’ll get physical growth through the Class.” “You don’t know that.” “The System literally listed Strength as a secondary tendency.” Grant’s expression said he disliked being corrected by menus.

Lucas considered the options. Two points. Small numbers now. Potentially enormous consequences later. Strength improved physical force. Agility improved movement. Perception improved awareness. Intelligence improved mana capacity and complexity. Wisdom improved control, regeneration, and efficiency. Willpower supported mental resistance and intent. Mana Spearman had already nudged Agility, Perception, and Intelligence. Lucas’s weakness was obvious: control. He allocated one point to Wisdom.

WISDOM: 10 → 11

One point remained. He almost put it into Wisdom again, then stopped. Too early to specialize blindly. Mana Reinforcement used the body and weapon. Endurance might matter. Strength might matter. Willpower might matter. He could wait. The System did not require immediate allocation. Lucas closed the screen with one point unspent. Grant looked offended on principle. “You didn’t spend both.” “No.” “Why?” “Because I don’t know what I need yet.” “That’s going to become your answer to everything, isn’t it?” “Until I know things.” Daniel laughed. Grant walked away. Owen watched him go. “You enjoy that too much.” “A little.”

Then Lucas picked up the spear. The difference was immediate—not in strength, understanding. Spear Familiarity sat somewhere inside his awareness. It did not move his body. It did not whisper instructions. But positions that had felt uncertain yesterday now felt categorized: lead hand, rear hand, range, line, center. The spear stopped feeling like a sharpened branch and started feeling like a tool with rules. Lucas shifted his stance. His feet adjusted almost naturally—not automatically. He could feel when the position was wrong. That was different. He thrust. The movement was smoother. He tried again. Better. Owen’s eyebrows rose. “That looks annoyingly improved.” “It feels annoyingly improved.” “Can you fight now?” “No.” “But less badly?” “Probably.”

Lucas reversed the spear. The System-assisted understanding helped him recognize errors. It did not remove them. His injured shoulder shortened the motion. His rear foot drifted. His grip tightened too much. The Skill made flaws easier to notice. It did not fix them. That was exactly the kind of ability Lucas liked. He spent twenty minutes testing: forward thrust, short thrust, brace, recovery, side step. His shoulder eventually forced him to stop. Sarah appeared as if summoned by bad decisions. “You got the Class.” “Yes.” “You’re using the shoulder.” “Carefully.” “That’s the word people use before I tell them to stop.” Lucas lowered the spear. She looked at his Status numbers when he read them aloud. “Any Vitality?” “Still ten.” “Then your injury did not magically disappear.” “Was worth checking.” “No.” She walked away. Owen waited until Sarah was out of earshot. “She scares you.” “Correct.” “Good.”

Lucas sat. Mana Reinforcement remained unopened in his Skills screen like a button labeled PRESS HERE FOR CONSEQUENCES. Naturally, he pressed. He focused on the Skill. The mana inside him responded—not all of it. A thin stream moved toward his right arm. Warmth spread through muscle and bone. The System provided no visual effect, no glowing skin, no dramatic hum. His arm simply felt denser, more solid. Lucas raised his hand. “Interesting.” Owen backed up one step. “What?” “I activated it.” “Mana Reinforcement?” “Yeah.” “Do something.” “That sentence has killed people.” Daniel returned carrying a length of scrap wood. “Try this.” He held it horizontally. Lucas stared. “You want me to punch it?” “No. Press.” Better. Lucas wrapped his reinforced hand around the wood and squeezed. Nothing happened. He squeezed harder. The wood creaked faintly. Owen leaned closer. “Again.” Lucas increased the Skill. The mana flow intensified. Heat rose through his forearm. The wood creaked louder. Then pain flashed through Lucas’s wrist. He released immediately. Mana Reinforcement collapsed. Lucas shook his hand. “What happened?” Emily asked. “Too much.” Daniel examined the wood. A shallow indentation marked where Lucas’s fingers had pressed. “Not bad.” Lucas disagreed. The effect had been real. So had the pain. He looked at the Skill description again. Safe intensity depended on control, attributes, mastery, and target compatibility. He had just discovered what “safe intensity” meant.

“Again,” Lucas said. Sarah’s voice came from across the clearing. “No.” Everyone laughed. Lucas waited ten minutes, then tried again when Sarah was busy. This time he used less mana, much less. The warmth spread slowly. No pain. He held it for five seconds, ten, fifteen. The sensation weakened. Mana drain. He could feel the internal reservoir lowering—not dramatically, enough to notice. He released. A mild fatigue settled through him. “Duration cost,” Lucas said. Emily crouched nearby. “How much?” “No idea.” “Can you see mana numerically?” Lucas checked Status. No mana value. Interesting. “No.” “So you’re estimating.” “Yeah.” “Useful.” “Eventually.”

Lucas activated the Skill again, this time on the spear. The mana resisted—not refused, resisted. Flowing into his own arm felt natural. Moving it beyond his body into wood felt different. He concentrated. A thin current crossed his palm. The spear warmed, very slightly. Lucas’s eyes widened. He pushed more. The current destabilized. Mana slipped away—not vanished, wasted. The Skill deactivated. His head throbbed. “Okay.” “What?” Owen asked. “Body is easier than weapon.” “Why?” “No idea.” “Excellent.” Lucas rested, then tried again. The Class had not given him mastery. It had given him access. That distinction grew clearer with every attempt. Mana Reinforcement created a pathway. Lucas still had to learn how to use it. He tried the spear three more times. The first failed. The second held for two seconds. The third lasted nearly five. When he released, Lucas noticed something. The spear shaft felt different while reinforced—not stronger in some abstract sense, more resistant to flex. He tested without mana, pressed it against the ground. The wood bent slightly. Activated Reinforcement, pressed again. Less bend. “Structural integrity,” he said. Daniel nodded. “Useful.” “Very.” A spear breaking during a fight was bad. A reinforced spear might survive impacts it otherwise could not. But Lucas saw another possibility. If the weapon flexed less, force transferred differently—more directly. Maybe penetration improved. Maybe recoil worsened. Maybe both. He wanted to test. His shoulder did not. Lucas hated responsible decisions. He stopped.

By midday, three more people in camp had acquired Classes: one Warrior, one Scout, one Healer Initiate. The healer changed everything. Her name was Megan. She had spent most of the first two days helping Sarah. Apparently repeated medical care plus some kind of mana perception had unlocked the Class. Her first healing Skill could accelerate clotting and reduce minor tissue damage. It did not close deep wounds. It did not restore lost blood. It did not repair broken bones. Sarah looked almost relieved. “Good,” she said after testing it. Megan blinked. “Good?” “It has limits.” “Why is that good?” “Because if you could erase serious injuries at Level 1, people would start acting even stupider.” Lucas liked Sarah more every day.

Grant began pushing for more people to unlock combat Classes. That argument became the camp’s new background noise. Some agreed. Others pointed out that Class options depended on behavior, and throwing everyone at monsters would shape humanity into an army of Warriors whether they wanted that or not. Lucas listened. That concern bothered him. Classes were not just power. They were direction. What you did influenced what you could become. The System rewarded behavior with future possibilities. Which meant desperate early decisions could shape decades of development. Maybe longer. People did not understand that yet. Neither did Lucas. But he understood enough to be cautious.

Late afternoon brought the first serious test. A pair of Needle Rats crossed into the clearing. People moved to kill them—too many people. Eight humans rushed two Level 2 creatures. Lucas stayed back. So did Owen. The rats panicked. One darted toward the medical area. Lucas moved. Spear Familiarity changed the fight before Mana Reinforcement did. The Skill helped him judge distance. He did not chase the rat. He stepped into the line it needed to cross. Spear low. Point hidden until the last moment. The rat committed. Lucas thrust—not hard, accurate. The point entered beneath the jaw. The rat died almost instantly. A notification appeared.

Class progression gained.
Mana Spearman — Lv. 1
Progress: 23%

Race progression gained.
Human — Lv. 2
Progress: 12%

Lucas stared. Class XP. Finally. Not Race alone. The Class had its own progression. The second rat died to three other people. Lucas received nothing from that kill. Good. Contribution remained real. Owen walked over. “Twenty-three percent?” Lucas looked at him. “How’d you know?” “Your face.” “My face says percentages?” “Your face says you just got something you wanted.” Lucas checked Status again.

He considered Strength—more force. Agility—more speed. Intelligence—more mana. Wisdom—better control. Then his shoulder ached. Endurance? Vitality? Every choice mattered. Lucas closed the screen. Still unspent. He had survived three days without understanding the rules. Now the rules were becoming visible. That did not make decisions easier. It made ignorance harder to excuse. Lucas rested one hand on the spear. Mana Spearman. Uncommon. Not legendary. Not chosen by a god. Not a destiny. A path he had earned because of what he had actually done. He liked that. More importantly, he understood something Grant did not. The Class was not the answer. It was another question. What could spear technique become when mana stopped being something separate from the weapon?

Lucas activated Mana Reinforcement at the lowest intensity he could manage. A thin warmth moved through his hand into the shaft. The spear stiffened, barely. He held the flow one second, two, three, then intentionally reduced it without fully releasing the Skill. The mana flickered, almost failed. He corrected. It stabilized. Lucas smiled—small, controlled, repeatable. That felt better than squeezing wood until his wrist hurt. The System remained silent. No achievement. No new Skill. No reward. Good. Lucas kept practicing.
