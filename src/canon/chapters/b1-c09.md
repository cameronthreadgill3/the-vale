# Chapter 9
## The Longer Road

Lucas discovered that leaving Hearth to hunt felt different from leaving Hearth to scout. Scouting had an excuse built into it. You were looking. Learning. Mapping. If the forest offered nothing, you came home with information and called the day useful. Hunting meant walking into the trees with the specific intention of finding something dangerous enough to kill. That made the spear in his hand feel less like a tool and more like a declaration.

“You’re doing the face again,” Owen said. Lucas glanced over. “What face?” “The one where you stare at nothing and mentally turn it into a spreadsheet.” “I don’t have a spreadsheet.” “That’s why it’s tragic.” They stood at Hearth’s northern barricade just after sunrise. The settlement behind them was already awake. Smoke climbed from cook fires. Daniel’s crews were moving poles toward the new storage racks. Someone was arguing about water allocation near Grant’s board. Sarah had inspected Lucas’s shoulder twice, then made him demonstrate six controlled thrusts before clearing him to leave. Light duty, she had called it. Lucas suspected Sarah and the System had different definitions of light. He rolled the shoulder once. Mild tightness. No sharp pain. Good enough.

Their hunting group was smaller than the grazer expedition: Lucas, Owen, Marcus, Priya, and a broad-shouldered woman named Tessa Ward who had chosen the Common Archer Class two days earlier. Five people. Enough to watch each other without turning movement through the forest into a parade. Owen had finally made his own choice—Ranger, Common. He had announced it the previous evening with considerably less ceremony than Lucas expected. Apparently the decision had come down to a simple question: which Class best rewarded the things Owen was already doing? Tracking. Moving quietly. Reading terrain. Fighting when necessary. Ranger. Lucas approved of the logic, which Owen claimed was the worst possible endorsement. Now Owen carried a short ashwood bow built by one of Hearth’s more optimistic amateur craftsmen, a knife, and three light javelins. The bow looked functional. The arrows looked like an ongoing negotiation with physics. Marcus had a heavier spear and a stone-headed club at his belt. His Warrior Class showed in the way he moved. Nothing magical to Lucas’s eye, but his posture had become more grounded over the last few days—stronger, more certain. Tessa’s bow was better than Owen’s. She had made it herself. Priya had no Class yet. She carried a sharpened staff and had been invited because she knew the northern route better than anyone except Owen.

Grant stepped through the barricade opening. “Same rules,” he said. “You see something well above your ability, you come back.” Marcus nodded. Grant looked at Lucas. Lucas raised an eyebrow. “Why are you looking at me?” “Because you’re the one who studies dangerous things.” “I study them from responsible distances.” Owen coughed. Lucas ignored him. Grant’s mouth twitched. “Be back before dark.” That was all. No speech. No command structure. Lucas appreciated that about Grant. The man liked organization, sometimes more than Lucas did, but he had learned quickly that capable groups worked better when he gave them a goal and stopped trying to control every footstep.

They left Hearth behind. The northern trail was becoming recognizable—not a road, not yet, but repeated traffic had bent grass, broken low branches, and left pale scars on ashwood bark where Daniel’s crews had marked turns. Four hours to the spring when carrying supplies. Less if they moved lightly. They were not going all the way. Owen had found grazer sign east of the route yesterday, along with smaller tracks he could not identify. The plan was to hunt away from Hearth’s increasingly disturbed surroundings and see whether the System treated the same creatures differently when the circumstances changed. That last part was Lucas’s plan. Marcus called it “making lunch unnecessarily academic.” Lucas called that jealousy. For the first hour, nothing tried to kill them. Lucas practiced while walking—not with the spear. Sarah would materialize from another dimension purely to yell at him if he started thrusting through the forest. He practiced mana distribution through his right hand. Mana Reinforcement activated softly. The Skill wanted to spread through the hand and wrist. Lucas let it, then shaped the distribution—dense across the palm, lighter through the fingers, almost nothing above the wrist. Iterative Insight did not speak. It never spoke. That was becoming one of Lucas’s favorite things about it. Instead, each attempt left a cleaner residue of understanding—a sense of where effort had been wasted, where his intent had become vague, where the mana had followed the structure of his body rather than the pattern he imagined. He adjusted. Again. Better.

“You’re doing magic, aren’t you?” Priya asked. Lucas released the Skill. “Technically.” “You look constipated.” Owen pointed at her. “Thank you.” “I hate this group.” “No you don’t,” Owen said. “You selected us.” “That was before I understood the culture.” Marcus chuckled. They reached the split in the trail near midmorning. Owen crouched. The forest floor here was damp enough to hold prints. Redhorn Grazer tracks crossed from west to east. Several animals. Fresh. Owen touched one. “Maybe two hours.” Lucas studied the ground. Identify did nothing to tracks—not directly. He had tried. The System apparently did not consider every footprint an object deserving a convenient label. Which meant learning mattered. Owen pointed to the deeper edge of one print. “Moving east. Not running.” Priya nodded. “Toward the low meadow.” Lucas knew the place from Owen’s description—a long depression between two ridges where pale grass grew waist-high. Water collected there after rain. Good grazing. Also terrible visibility. Marcus looked at Lucas. “Your experiment.” “Our hunt.” “Your experiment disguised as a hunt.” “Multitasking.” They moved east. Owen took point. Lucas watched him work. Ranger had not turned Owen into a supernatural tracker overnight. He still stopped, checked broken stems, lost the trail once, backtracked. But Lucas noticed differences. Owen moved with fewer unnecessary steps. His attention seemed to settle naturally on useful details. When he crouched beside a patch of disturbed soil, his expression changed before he consciously explained what he had found. Class-assisted familiarity. Similar to Spear Familiarity. The System could provide foundations. It could not provide judgment. That distinction kept appearing.

Twenty minutes later, Owen raised a fist. Everyone stopped. He pointed ahead. Lucas heard chewing. A Redhorn Grazer stood beyond a screen of brush. Identify.

Lucas waited. Another shape moved behind it.

Redhorn Grazer

Beast — Lv. 4

Then a smaller one. Lv. 2. Three animals. Marcus whispered, “We can take them.” Lucas looked at the terrain—no prepared stakes, no barricades, no camp nearby, no Sarah waiting five minutes away if somebody’s ribs became an abstract concept. The same species. Different problem. “Maybe,” Lucas said. Marcus frowned. “We handled two.” “With a funnel we spent half a day building.” “I know.” “Just checking.” Marcus studied the meadow again. Lucas liked that he did not bristle. Chapter-four Marcus might have. They had all learned something. Owen crawled back from the brush. “More tracks north side. Herd might be bigger.” “How much bigger?” Tessa asked. “No idea.” Lucas considered. A Level 3 grazer near Hearth had become manageable once they understood its charge. Here, even one could be dangerous because retreat routes were worse. Multiple animals could turn the grass into a blind stampede. Relative difficulty. The System used that phrase indirectly through everything it rewarded. A creature’s level mattered. Circumstance mattered too. “Let’s circle,” Lucas said. Marcus nodded. They spent twenty-five minutes doing nothing heroic. It was productive. The meadow narrowed at its eastern end where exposed stone formed a shallow channel—not enough to trap a grazer, enough to limit how many could charge side by side. The northern ridge had scattered trees. The southern side was open. Owen found eleven distinct sets of tracks. “Not three,” he whispered. “Definitely not three,” Lucas said. Marcus sighed. “Fine.” Tessa smiled. “Was that painful?” “Deeply.” They abandoned the herd. No progression notification appeared. Lucas was glad. He did not want the System rewarding every sensible decision with confetti. Sometimes survival could simply be survival.

They continued east along the ridge. Near noon, they found blood. Owen saw it first—dark drops on pale grass, then a smear across a rock. Lucas crouched. Fresh enough to shine. Priya pointed downhill. Something had crashed through the brush. The trail was obvious. Marcus tightened his grip on his spear. “Predator?” “Or wounded prey,” Lucas said. Owen studied the ground. “Both.” He pointed—grazer tracks, and another print over them. Four toes. Long claws. Lucas’s attention sharpened. Not the enormous five-toed tracks from the spring. Different creature. They followed slowly. The smell reached them first—blood, then a wet tearing sound. Tessa climbed three steps up the ridge and looked through the trees. She lowered herself immediately. “Cat.” “How big?” Priya whispered. Tessa considered. “Bad size.” Owen nodded. “Technical.” They edged forward until Lucas could see it. The creature crouched over a dead Redhorn Grazer beneath a leaning ashwood—long-bodied, gray-brown fur, heavy forequarters, short thick tail, two dark ridges running from above its eyes down the sides of its neck. Identify.

Ridgeclaw

Beast — Lv. 5

F-Rank

Lucas’s stomach tightened. Level 5. Higher than anything they had intentionally hunted. The Ridgeclaw lifted its head. Blood covered its muzzle. One ear was torn. Its right hind leg dragged slightly when it shifted. Wounded. Marcus saw it too. “That changes things.” “Yes,” Lucas said. “How much?” Lucas almost answered. Then stopped. Iterative Insight had made him increasingly suspicious of fast conclusions. He watched. The Ridgeclaw returned to feeding. Its breathing was heavy. The grazer beneath it was Level 3 according to Identify, though dead creatures sometimes gave less useful information after enough time passed. What had injured the Ridgeclaw? The grazer? Another Ridgeclaw? Something worse? Lucas examined the clearing. Blood on the ground led from west. The grazer had run. Claw marks crossed its flank. One horn was broken. The Ridgeclaw’s limp might have come from the horn. Wounded did not mean weak. Wounded predators were often more dangerous because they had fewer options. Owen leaned close. “We leave?” Lucas looked at him—not fear, question. This was why they had come. Not specifically for a Level 5 cat with knives attached to its feet, but to hunt beyond Hearth. To understand what difficulty actually meant when prepared terrain disappeared. Lucas checked his shoulder. Fine. Mana. Good. Spear. Still ashwood. Still charcoal-marked. Still not a proper System weapon. “We don’t fight it over the carcass,” Lucas said. Marcus nodded immediately. “Agreed.” “We can pull it?” Tessa asked, “With what?” Lucas looked at the dead grazer. “No. We make it decide.” Owen smiled slightly. “That sounds ominous.” “It means we annoy it from somewhere safer.” “Less ominous.”

They withdrew. The ridge behind them rose into broken stone—not cliffs, large slabs of dark rock jutting from the soil. Lucas walked among them. One narrow gap. Too tight for the Ridgeclaw? Probably not. Another. Better. A wedge-shaped passage between two slabs opened into a small bowl of stone. Human-sized exits on both sides. The Ridgeclaw could enter, but turning would be difficult. Lucas traced the route. “We don’t trap it.” Marcus nodded. “We make it commit.” “Tessa hits it from the rocks. Owen too if his arrows agree to participate.” “My arrows are artists.” “They’re sticks with feathers.” “Artists.” Lucas continued. “If it charges, Marcus and I take the gap. Priya stays high and calls movement.” Priya frowned. “I can fight.” “I know. You also don’t have a Class.” “That doesn’t make me useless.” “No. It makes your risk-to-reward terrible against Level 5.” She stared at him. Then nodded. “Fair.” That was easier than expected. They prepared for ten minutes—no elaborate construction, no stakes, just position. Lucas tested the forward-weighted reinforcement pattern on his spear—tip dense, shaft tapered, no hard boundary. Iterative Insight sharpened the response. Stable while stationary. Less stable during rapid acceleration. He adjusted the sequence: grip, intent, flow, movement, correction. The same pattern from last night. His shoulder remained quiet. Marcus noticed the charcoal lines. “Owen said you got a Skill.” “Rare General.” Marcus whistled softly. “What does it do?” “Makes my mistakes more educational.” Marcus waited. “That’s it?” “That is a horrifyingly valuable thing.” Marcus laughed. “Sure.” Lucas smiled. He understood the reaction. People wanted Skills that exploded. Lucas wanted fewer stupid repetitions.

Tessa climbed onto the slab. Owen took the opposite side. Priya positioned farther back with clear sight to the feeding site. Lucas and Marcus waited in the stone gap. Then Tessa fired. The arrow flew surprisingly straight. It struck the ground beside the Ridgeclaw. The predator’s head snapped up. Second arrow. Hit. Shallow, high on the shoulder. The Ridgeclaw roared. Sound punched through the trees. Lucas felt mana move inside it—dense, hot, fast. The creature bounded toward Tessa. “Here!” Owen shouted. His arrow hit a tree. Artist. The Ridgeclaw changed direction anyway. Owen threw a stone. It clacked off rock. The predator saw movement in the gap—Lucas, Marcus, two targets at ground level. Easier. Its body lowered. Mana gathered through its forequarters. Lucas’s Perception caught the shift. “Commit!” The Ridgeclaw launched—fast, faster than a grazer over the first few strides. Lucas activated Mana Reinforcement—forward third, taper. The predator entered the gap. Marcus stepped first. Physical Reinforcement flashed through his posture, visible to Lucas only as the sudden change in how force moved through his body. Marcus stabbed. The Ridgeclaw twisted. Spear point cut its shoulder. It hit Marcus anyway—not cleanly, a glancing collision. Marcus slammed into stone. Lucas thrust. The Ridgeclaw turned toward him—too fast. Lucas’s spear point struck ribs instead of throat. Mana held. The shaft bent. Lucas released pressure before the load traveled fully into his shoulder. The spear slid free. Good. The Ridgeclaw swiped. Lucas stepped back. Claws passed inches from his stomach. Too close. Marcus recovered and drove his club into the animal’s hindquarter. The injured leg buckled. The Ridgeclaw screamed. Then mana surged. Lucas felt it gather around the predator’s shoulders and spine. “Back!” The creature spun with impossible violence. Its forepaw struck Marcus’s spear and snapped the upper third—not cracked, snapped. Marcus swore. Lucas moved. The Ridgeclaw lunged at Marcus. Lucas stabbed into its flank. The spear penetrated—not deep. The predator twisted. Lucas let go. That decision saved his shoulder. Maybe his arm. The spear ripped from his hand as the Ridgeclaw turned. Lucas drew the utility knife. Immediately hated the universe. Marcus had his club. The Ridgeclaw stood between them, Lucas’s spear hanging from its side. Blood ran down gray fur. Its hind leg shook. It was wounded. Still Level 5. Still extremely capable of killing them. Tessa’s arrow struck its back. The Ridgeclaw whirled. Owen’s javelin came next. It hit—poor angle, but enough to make the animal flinch. Marcus charged. Lucas almost yelled at him. Then saw why. The Ridgeclaw’s attention was up. Marcus came low. Club into injured rear leg. Crack. The creature collapsed. Lucas moved without thinking—no spear, knife, terrible reach. He grabbed the broken half of Marcus’s spear from the ground instead—three feet of shaft, jagged end, better than a knife. Mana Reinforcement. The Skill spread. Lucas shaped it—not the weapon he had practiced with, different wood, different balance. Iterative Insight sharpened the mismatch immediately. Too much mana near his hand. Wrong. He shifted forward. The Ridgeclaw surged upright. Lucas stabbed. The broken shaft entered beneath the jaw. Mana pattern collapsed on impact. Force traveled backward. Lucas released. Marcus drove the club down—once, twice. The Ridgeclaw clawed at stone. Tessa fired from above. The final arrow entered behind the foreleg. The predator convulsed. Then stopped.

For several seconds, nobody moved. Lucas listened to his breathing—too fast. Shoulder? Aching. Not sharp. Hands? Shaking. Marcus sat down hard. “Everybody alive?” Priya called from above. Owen answered, “Emotionally unclear.” Tessa laughed once, breathless. Lucas looked at the dead Ridgeclaw. A notification appeared.

Meaningful combat contribution recorded.

Relative challenge exceeded current Class level.

Mana Spearman progression increased.

Human progression increased.

Lucas blinked. There. Not a percentage explanation. No formula. But explicit confirmation. He opened Progression.

Human — Lv. 2

Progress: 82%

Mana Spearman — Lv. 2

Progress: 79%

Profession: Not Acquired

Lucas stared. Before the fight: Human sixty-seven. Class forty-three. One fight had moved Human fifteen percentage points. Class thirty-six. The Level 2 Needle Rat after he acquired Mana Spearman had given twenty-three Class progress when he was Level 1. A Level 5 Ridgeclaw, fought at Class Level 2, had given substantially more despite contribution being split across the group. Relative difficulty was real. They butchered what they could, kept the hide, claws, and useful bones, and started the long walk back. By the time they reached Hearth, the sun was low. The camp accepted the meat and materials without ceremony. Another successful hunt. Lucas sat near the fire with the cracked spear across his knees. Owen dropped beside him. “You’re doing the face again.” “You’re thinking about the next hunt.” “No.” “The spring?” “No.” “The giant tracks?” Lucas looked at him. Owen grinned. “Absolutely not.” “Good.” Lucas held up the cracked spear. “Tomorrow I’m getting a weapon that doesn’t try to become firewood every time something important happens.” As if the System had been waiting for the sentence, a quiet chime sounded.

MANA SPEARMAN MILESTONE APPROACHING

Class Lv.3 threshold near.

Additional Class development may unlock new Skill pathways and equipment opportunities.

Lucas read the notification twice. Equipment opportunities. He looked at the broken spear. Then at Owen. Owen saw his expression. “What?” Lucas smiled. “Tomorrow might be interesting.”
