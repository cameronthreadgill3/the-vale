# Chapter 5
## Too Much

Lucas learned three things about Mana Reinforcement before breakfast. First, it was a lot easier to turn on if he stood still. Second, it fell apart the second he tried to do anything useful with it. Third, the Skill did not care which of those facts he found inconvenient.

He stood near the eastern edge of camp with the ashwood spear in both hands, feet planted in the stance Spear Familiarity insisted was merely acceptable—not good, acceptable. Apparently the System had standards. Lucas activated Mana Reinforcement. Warmth gathered under his sternum. He pushed a thin stream through his shoulders and down both arms. The mana crossed into the spear. The shaft stiffened—better than yesterday, still uneven. He could feel the weak spots, places where mana leaked instead of soaking into the wood. Lucas adjusted his grip. The pattern shifted. Interesting. He took one slow step. The reinforcement flickered. Second step. It wobbled. Third. Gone. Lucas sighed. “Again,” he muttered.

“Talking to yourself now?” Owen walked up with two pieces of flatbread someone had made from scavenged grain and optimism. Lucas took one. “Scientific process.” “It looked like walking.” “Advanced walking.” Owen bit into his bread. “How advanced?” “I can almost do three steps before failing.” “Terrifying.” Lucas activated the Skill again, less mana this time. The effect was weaker. The spear barely stiffened. But the flow held. One step. Two. Three. Four. He smiled. Then he tried to thrust. The Skill collapsed. Owen nodded gravely. “Clearly you have mastered magic.” “Go away.” “No.”

Lucas reset. The problem was not just moving. It was changing too much at once. Walking asked the flow to adapt through his body. Thrusting moved shoulders, hands, hips, grip, weight. Mana that behaved itself while he stood still turned into soup the moment he used it. Which meant Mana Reinforcement was not a switch. It was another movement pattern—maybe dozens of them. Lucas liked that. He did not like the amount of work hiding behind it. But he liked the rule.

Sarah passed them carrying boiled water. She looked at Lucas, then at the spear. “Shoulder?” “Fine.” “That answer has never meant fine.” “It’s improving.” “That’s better.” She kept walking. Lucas waited until she was farther away. Owen dropped his voice. “You’re afraid of her.” “Correct.”

The camp was starting to look like someone had meant to build it. Daniel’s barricades had grown—not walls, layers of sharpened branches set where animals would have to slow down. Watch groups had actual rotations. Water teams had assigned containers. People were carving wooden bowls, scraping hides, sorting plants that might not kill them, and throwing together crude sleeping shelters. Progress did not make the place civilized. It just made the danger look organized. Grant had started doing the same thing with people. Combat-capable Classes were being catalogued: Warriors, Scouts, Hunters, one Archer Initiate, Megan the Healer Initiate. Lucas hated the word catalogued. He understood why Grant did it anyway. Information mattered.

Marcus crossed the clearing toward them, pry bar over one shoulder. “Grant wants a field team.” Lucas stopped practicing. “For what?” “Food.” “Finally.” They had enough water now. Food was the next emergency. Snacks were almost gone. Some plants had been tagged edible, just not in quantities that would keep hundreds of people alive. Several groups had seen herd animals in the open grassland west of the forest. Grant wanted a closer look. “Who?” “You, me, Owen, two Scouts, Eric, Daniel, and a Hunter named Claire.” Eight. A formal Party could support eight. Lucas smiled. “Does Grant know Parties exist?” Marcus shook his head. “No clue.” “Want to find out?”

That got everyone’s attention. Lucas had not found Party in any useful place yet. But the System menu had answered intent before. He focused. Party. A new prompt appeared. Create Party? Maximum members at current System access: 8. Lucas grinned. “Found it.” Owen leaned closer. “Found what?” “Party creation.” Marcus’s expression sharpened. “That matters.” Lucas selected create. A new interface appeared.

Party Name: Optional
Invite Member.
Shared communication features: Restricted.
Experience distribution: Contribution-weighted.
Shared party allocation: Enabled.

Lucas paused. “Contribution-weighted.” Marcus nodded. “Good.” “No leeching.” “Also good.” Lucas sent Owen an invite. Owen blinked. “Oh.” “You get it?” “Yeah.” He accepted. The name OWEN BROOKS appeared in Lucas’s Party interface. One by one they added the others: Marcus, Daniel, Eric, Claire, two Scouts named Aaron and Priya. Eight members. Party full. A faint awareness settled into place—not telepathy, not location tracking, more like the System admitting they were connected. Lucas could see names and basic status indicators. No health bars. No exact stamina. Just whether someone was active, injured, or incapacitated. “Useful,” Daniel said after checking his own. “Very.”

Grant listened to the explanation before they left. “Can we create multiple Parties?” “Looks like it.” “Any raid option?” Lucas tried. Raid formation unavailable. Requirements not met. “Not yet.” Grant nodded. “Bring back information before meat.” Lucas raised an eyebrow. “That almost sounded like something I’d say.” Grant looked unimpressed. “If you see something dangerous, return.” Marcus smiled. “He says that every time.” “Because you people keep finding dangerous things.” Fair.

They left camp just after sunrise. The western route was different from the stream path—less dense forest, more sunlight. The trees thinned until ashwood gave way to waist-high grass. Red-brown blades rolled across the terrain. Lucas stopped at the edge. The Redgrass Expanse. Nobody had named it officially. Someone from another clearing had called it that, and the name stuck. Identify returned:

Redgrass
F-Rank Flora
Fibrous grass adapted to mana-rich open environments.
Additional identification unavailable.

Claire crouched near the edge—early thirties, compact, dark-haired, crude bow made from bent ashwood. Hunter — Lv. 2. Highest Class level in the group. “What do you see?” Lucas asked. She pointed. “Tracks.” Lucas stared. Nothing. Claire smiled. “Hunter Skill.” “What kind?” “Trail Sense.” She tapped the grass. “Large herd moved through here recently.” “Direction?” “Southwest.” Lucas looked. Still nothing. Good. Different Classes saw different pieces of the world. This place was already too big for one person to understand alone.

They followed the tracks. Lucas practiced Mana Reinforcement while walking—very lightly, a whisper of mana through his body, not the spear. Body first. Low-level reinforcement held through his legs easier than his arms. Walking already moved mana with the muscles. Maybe the Skill liked active structures better than passive ones. Or maybe Lucas had been walking his entire life and holding a spear for three days. Experience mattered. He fed it a little more mana. His steps got firmer—not faster exactly, more efficient. Then his right calf cramped. Lucas dumped the Skill immediately. Marcus noticed. “Too much?” “Maybe.” “Physical Reinforcement does that if I hold it too long.” “Cramp?” “Burn first. Then weakness.” “Mine feels like the muscles stop agreeing with the mana.” Marcus nodded. “Sounds healthy.” “Extremely.”

They kept going. Half an hour later Claire stopped. “Animals.” Everyone dropped. Beyond a low ridge, a herd grazed—at least twenty, four-legged, broad-backed, deer-sized but heavier, dark red fur along the spine, curved black horns. Identify.

Redhorn Grazer
Beast — Lv. 3
F-Rank

Another Level 4. Another Level 2. Mixed levels. Eric whispered, “Food.” Daniel said, “Potentially.” Claire studied them. “Pack behavior?” “Herd,” Owen corrected. “You know what I mean.” Lucas watched. The grazers were calm. No predators in sight. Younger animals clustered near the center. The larger ones faced outward. Not helpless. Lucas focused on one Level 4 adult—broad horns, heavy shoulders. “Those things charge,” he said. Marcus nodded. “Definitely.” Claire looked around. “We don’t need the biggest.” “Pick one?” She scanned. A Level 2 grazer stood farther from the herd, smaller, maybe injured, one rear leg favored slightly. Daniel frowned. “That feels cruel.” “We need food,” Claire said. “I know.” Nobody liked it. That did not change the math. Lucas looked at the herd. They could leave, come back hungry, send another group. Eventually someone would kill something. Better to learn carefully. “How do we do it?” Marcus asked. Claire pointed. “Drive the injured one away from the herd. Don’t enter the herd. If the adults turn, we leave.” Simple. Dangerous.

They positioned. Lucas and Marcus left flank. Owen and Eric right. Claire and the Scouts behind. Daniel held center. The plan worked for about fifteen seconds. They moved forward. The injured grazer noticed. It limped away. The herd tightened. Good. Then Eric got too close. One adult lowered its head. Lucas felt mana shift inside it—dense, different from the Razor Hounds, gathering through chest and legs. “Back!” Eric hesitated. The grazer charged—fast, much faster than something that size had any right to move. Eric dove aside. The grazer changed direction toward Owen. Lucas shoved Mana Reinforcement through his legs and spear. Too much. The flow surged. He moved. For one stupid, perfect second, everything worked. His reinforced leg drove off the ground harder than expected. He closed the distance. Spear came down. Mana flooded the shaft. Lucas planted the butt against the earth and braced—exactly like the Bark Hound. Except the Bark Hound had weighed maybe eighty pounds. The Redhorn Grazer weighed several hundred.

The horned beast hit the spear. The reinforced shaft held. Lucas did not. The impact drove him backward. His injured shoulder screamed. Mana surged chaotically. He panicked and pushed more. The Skill answered too well. Power slammed into his arms. The spear stopped bending. His body tried to eat what the weapon no longer would. Pain tore from his palms through his wrists and up both arms. Something in his left shoulder felt like it was ripping apart. Lucas screamed. The grazer twisted away. The spear tore free. Marcus hit the beast from the side with the reinforced pry bar. Daniel struck the other flank. Owen dragged Lucas backward. “Drop it!” Lucas could not. His hands had locked around the spear. Mana Reinforcement was still on, too strong. His fingers felt numb. “Lucas!” He tried to release. Nothing. The mana kept flowing because he was still thinking hold, brace, do not let go. Intent. Of course. Lucas forced himself to think release. Nothing. He changed the idea—not stop, open, relax the hand, let the spear fall. The mana flickered. His right hand opened. Then left. The spear dropped. Mana Reinforcement collapsed. Lucas hit his knees. Every muscle in his arms trembled. His left shoulder burned badly enough he thought he had dislocated it.

Behind him the fight kept going. He made himself look. The charging adult had not retreated. Marcus and Daniel were holding its attention. The original injured grazer was running. Claire fired an arrow. It struck high in the flank. The herd was getting agitated. Bad. Very bad. “Retreat!” Lucas shouted. Marcus backed off. Daniel followed. Claire hesitated. “The injured one—” “Leave it!” The larger grazer charged again. This time nobody tried to stop it. They moved fast. The group fell back toward the trees. The herd followed fifty yards and stopped. Nobody spoke until the redgrass disappeared behind ashwood trunks. Lucas leaned against a tree. His arms shook. Marcus looked at him. “You okay?” “No.” “That’s honest.” “My shoulder.” Daniel inspected without touching. “Can you move it?” Lucas tried. Barely. Pain stopped him. “Not much.” Owen looked pale. “You nearly got launched.” “I noticed.” Eric stood a few feet away, guilt all over his face. “That was my fault.” Lucas looked at him. “No.” “I got too close.” “You made a mistake.” “So did I.” Eric shook his head. “You jumped in.” “And then I tried to stop a truck with a stick.” Daniel nodded. “Accurate summary.” Lucas laughed despite the pain. Bad idea.

They returned to camp without food. Grant saw them and knew immediately. “What happened?” “Learning,” Owen said. Sarah arrived before Lucas could answer. She took one look at his arm. “What did you do?” Lucas considered lying. “No.” He smiled weakly. “Good choice.” Sarah tested the shoulder carefully. Lucas nearly bit through his tongue. “Not dislocated,” she said. “Wonderful.” “Maybe worsened strain. Possibly small tear. Hard to know.” Megan joined her. “Can healing help?” “Minor tissue damage,” Sarah said. “Try low intensity.” Megan placed a hand near Lucas’s shoulder. A faint warmth spread through the joint—different from Mana Reinforcement, softer. The pain dropped a little, not gone. After thirty seconds Megan stopped, breathing harder. “That’s it.” Lucas tested the arm. Still painful. Better. Sarah pointed at him. “No spear today.” “That seems extreme.” “No spear tomorrow morning either.” “Cruel.” “You almost tore your shoulder apart using magic.” “Technically the grazer helped.” Sarah stared. Lucas looked away. “Right. No spear.”

Grant waited until Sarah finished. “What happened?” Lucas explained—not defensively, precisely. Low reinforcement. Charge. Spear braced. Impact. More mana. Weapon held. Body did not. Grant frowned. “So the Skill worked.” Lucas looked at him. “That is one interpretation.” “The spear didn’t break.” “My shoulder almost did.” Marcus stepped in. “That’s the important part.” Grant nodded. “I know.” Lucas studied him. Grant was not waving the injury off. He was taking the event apart—same as Lucas, different priorities. “What did you learn?” Grant asked. Lucas looked down at his trembling hands. “That stronger is not the same as better.” Grant waited. Lucas continued. “Reinforcement shifts failure points.” Daniel nodded immediately. “Yes.” “If I strengthen the spear enough, the force goes somewhere else.” “Into you,” Marcus said. “Exactly.” Lucas flexed his fingers. Pins and needles. “If I reinforce my arms but not shoulders, maybe it shifts there. If I reinforce everything, maybe the force shifts into joints. Or spine. Or whatever fails next.” Emily had joined them. “That means the Skill is not adding durability uniformly.” “Not unless I can make it.” “And even then,” Daniel said, “physics still exists.” Lucas smiled. “Apparently.”

Grant looked west. “So hunting grazers is possible.” Sarah snapped, “That is what you took from this?” Grant did not flinch. “With a better plan.” Lucas understood both of them. That was getting exhausting. He opened Progression.

Mana Spearman — Lv. 1
Progress: 81%

Human — Lv. 2
Progress: 34%

He blinked. Eighty-one. The encounter had paid him well for a fight that produced no kill. Danger. Contribution. Technique. Failure. Meaningful combat. The System cared about the attempt. Or the adaptation. Maybe both. Lucas focused on the Class progress. No explanation. Of course. He almost smiled. His shoulder ruined the satisfaction.

Later that afternoon, Marcus came back. “You resting?” “I’m sitting.” “That wasn’t the question.” “Then no.” Marcus handed him a piece of cooked root. “Sarah says if I let you practice, she’ll blame me.” “She should.” Marcus sat beside him. For a while they watched the camp. A group repaired barricades. Megan treated someone’s cut hand. Claire argued with Grant about the next hunting attempt. Daniel was designing something with sharpened stakes. The Trial kept moving even when Lucas could not. Marcus finally said, “You know what you did wrong?” Lucas glanced over. “Several things.” “The big one.” “Overcommitted.” Marcus nodded. “You treated the Skill like strength.” “Yeah.” “Physical Reinforcement does the same thing to me.” “How?” “First day I got it, I tried to lift a log I couldn’t lift before.” “And?” “Got it up.” Lucas waited. “Then I dropped it because my grip failed.” Lucas laughed. Marcus grinned. “Grant didn’t put that in the brochure.” “Of course not.” “My back hurt all night.” Lucas looked at him. “Same rule.” “Probably.” “Reinforcement improves capacity somewhere. Doesn’t guarantee the rest of the system can support it.” Marcus nodded. “Exactly.”

Lucas thought about the grazer. The spear had held. That had felt impressive at first. Now it just looked stupid. He had solved the wrong problem. The spear breaking might have been safer. That realization bothered him. Failure was not always failure. Sometimes failure protected the weaker parts. If everything got stronger unevenly, the weak point just moved. Lucas looked at his unspent Attribute Point. Vitality suddenly looked more attractive. So did Endurance. Wisdom still mattered. Control had failed. But the injury was physical. He could spend the point now. Patch a weakness. Or keep waiting. Lucas closed the screen. Not yet. He wanted to know more.

Daniel found Lucas an hour later—not because Lucas had asked, because people had started noticing that if Lucas was quiet he was usually experimenting. “How’s the arm?” “Complaining.” “Good. Means it’s still attached.” Lucas snorted. Daniel sat. “I keep thinking about the force transfer.” “So do I.” “If the spear is rigid, you become the shock absorber.” “Yes.” “So maybe the answer is not pure reinforcement.” Lucas looked at him. “Partial?” “Or timed.” “Or layered.” Daniel nodded. “Body first. Weapon second. Or the reverse depending on the impact.” Lucas considered that. “Or intentionally leave weak points.” Daniel’s eyebrows rose. “That sounds insane.” “Maybe. If the weapon is supposed to flex under certain loads, forcing it not to could be wrong.” “Like building a structure that needs to give.” “Exactly.” They sat with that for a while. The idea felt important and incomplete at the same time.

Lucas spent the rest of the afternoon testing low-intensity reinforcement while sitting still. Fingers only. Failed. Again. Palm. Almost. No notifications. No achievement. No reward. Just information. The sun lowered. Lucas’s Class progress remained at eighty-one percent. Race at thirty-four. His shoulder hurt. His hands still tingled. The day had produced no food. By most reasonable standards, the hunt had failed. Lucas was not sure the System agreed. He looked at his right hand. Activated Mana Reinforcement. A faint warmth appeared. This time he stopped before the urge to add more arrived. That might have been the most useful thing he learned all day. His unspent Attribute Point waited in Status. Yesterday he had thought of it as power held in reserve. Now it looked more like a decision about which weakness he was willing to live with. Strength would not have saved his shoulder. Wisdom might have improved control. Vitality might have made the tissue more durable. Endurance might have helped him tolerate the strain. Every answer depended on what failed next. Lucas closed the screen without spending the point—not because he was afraid to choose, but because for the first time he understood enough to know how much he still did not know.
