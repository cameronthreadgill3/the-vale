# Chapter 22
## The Narrow Moment

The first rule of the second Broodmother expedition was that nobody was allowed to call it a hunt.

Marcus objected immediately.

“If we’re going underground with weapons to kill something, that feels like a hunt.”

“We’re going underground to test whether killing it is possible without getting trapped,” Daniel said.

Marcus considered that. “A cautious hunt.”

“A survey with stabbing,” Owen offered.

Megan tightened the strap on her medical bag. “If either of you gets hurt because you were busy naming the activity, I’m writing ‘idiot’ under cause of injury.”

Lucas leaned against the Stonefield quarry wall and rolled his right hip carefully. Stiff. Not damaged, as far as Megan could tell. Yesterday’s repeated Mana Step use had jarred the joint and left the muscles tight enough that his body objected when he changed direction too quickly.

That mattered. Everything mattered now.

The Broodmother was Level 10.

Lucas was Human Level 5, Mana Spearman Level 6, Explorer Level 5.

Numbers were not destiny. The last three weeks had taught him that repeatedly. They were still information. A Level 10 dungeon-bound organism connected to a network of moving Glassroot, backed by Skitterers, inside terrain it could partially reshape, was not something seven people should fight because the number beside its name looked tempting.

So they had spent most of the morning arguing about how not to fight it.

Priya had redrawn the basin from seven separate descriptions. Daniel had marked every place the Glassroot had moved. Emily had listed the pulse sequences they remembered. Tessa had reconstructed Skitterer positions. Owen had compared the chamber to animal territory. Megan had vetoed two retreat routes because injured people could not realistically climb them.

Lucas had mostly stared at the timing. Pulse. Movement. Response.

Yesterday, Daniel had cut one Glassroot strand. The strand had flashed. The Broodmother had reacted. Then the chamber had reacted. Not simultaneously. Close. But not simultaneously.

Iterative Insight did not give Lucas an answer. It did something more annoying. It made him unable to stop noticing the question.

He opened Field Record.

GLASSBACK BROODMOTHER — OBSERVED RESPONSE CHAIN

1. Broodmother physical contact with Glassroot.

2. Blue-white pulse enters local root structure.

3. Nearby root branches reposition.

4. Skitterer behavior changes.

5. Route geometry changes.

Observed alternate sequence:

1. External Glassroot strand cut.

2. Local strand flashes.

3. Broodmother reacts.

4. Wider root response follows.

Confidence: Moderate.

Unknown: direction of signaling.

Unknown: whether Broodmother commands network, receives information from network, or both.

Unknown: whether response requires continuous contact.

Lucas closed the record.

Owen watched him. “You’ve got the face.”

“What face?”

“The one where you’re about to suggest something that sounds reasonable until we’re doing it.”

“That’s unfair.”

“Name three times it’s been unfair.”

Lucas thought about it. “No.”

“Excellent.”

Daniel came over carrying a coil of cord, three sharpened stakes, and a stone hammer. “We’re set.”

Lucas nodded.

The plan had four phases. Phase one: enter by the quarry breach, reach the lower gallery, and confirm the basin had not changed enough to invalidate everything. Phase two: provoke a controlled response from maximum retreatable distance. Phase three: test the timing between Broodmother, root network, and Skitterers. Phase four existed only if the first three produced something useful.

That was important. They were not committed to killing the Broodmother. They were committed to learning whether they could.

Grant had disliked even that. Stonefield’s people disliked it more. But the Glassroot Depths were spreading beneath the region, or had always been there and were becoming active. Four surface connections were now known. A dungeon organism that could influence the root network was not merely an underground problem.

Ignoring it was also a decision.

Lucas checked his spear.

Channelwood Spear

F-Rank — Uncommon

Condition: Worn

Minor structural damage detected.

The chip in the spearhead had not worsened. Good enough.

He flexed his fingers around the shaft. Mana flowed more easily than it had before yesterday’s Class level. Not dramatically. Cleaner. His body had adjusted around the new Agility, Intelligence, and Wisdom growth overnight in the strange way System changes always seemed to settle without making him wake up feeling like someone else.

Thirteen free Attribute Points waited. He had not spent them. Marcus had called that insane. Lucas had called Marcus impatient. Both statements probably contained some truth.

“Party,” Tessa said.

Lucas accepted the invitation. Seven names appeared at the edge of awareness.

Lucas.

Owen.

Marcus.

Tessa.

Emily.

Daniel.

Megan.

Seven. The missing eighth space did not exist in the interface. Lucas felt it anyway. Rachel had been part of the first descent. Her pack was still below.

“Turn-back rules?” Megan asked.

Daniel answered. “If primary retreat closes, we move immediately to fallback. If both are compromised, no one pushes deeper for a better angle.”

“If I call medical withdrawal?”

“We leave,” Marcus said.

Megan looked at Lucas.

“We leave.”

She nodded.

Owen adjusted his bow. “Change?”

“Anyone calls it,” Lucas said. “Everyone stops assuming the old plan still exists.”

Tessa looked toward the quarry breach. “And if the Broodguard comes back?”

Nobody answered immediately.

Glassroot Broodguard. Level 9.

Absent yesterday. Not dead. Not solved.

Lucas said, “Then the plan changes.”

“That is impressively unhelpful.”

“It’s also accurate.”

They entered.

The quarry breach swallowed daylight within thirty yards. Stonefield had installed rope guides along the first descent and reflective scraps at key turns. Human improvements over ancient stone and living crystal.

The Glassroot began deeper in. Blue-white veins glimmered through translucent structures embedded in walls and ceiling. Some were finger-thin. Others were thicker than Lucas’s torso.

He watched them. Hazard Recognition remained quiet. That did not mean safe. It meant nothing he currently understood as a danger indicator had changed enough to matter. Important distinction.

The lower gallery looked almost identical to yesterday. Almost.

“Change,” Emily said.

Everyone stopped. She pointed overhead. Three thin root strands had occupied the ceiling yesterday. Four crossed it now.

Daniel stared. “You sure?”

“I counted them because one had that fork.”

She pointed. The fork remained. A new strand curved beside it.

Lucas focused. Hazard Recognition sharpened the mismatch once Emily identified it. Not danger. Difference. Field Record accepted the observation.

They continued.

At the final turn before the defended basin, they established the first retreat marker. Daniel drove a stake into a crack in the stone and tied cord around it. The line ran back through the gallery, not because they needed a rope to navigate, but because Glassroot had already demonstrated that it could alter visible routes. A physical reference mattered.

Fifty feet farther back, they had a second route through the lower ledge. Primary. Fallback. No heroic improvisation required.

They reached the observation shelf.

The Broodmother remained below.

Lucas felt his stomach tighten despite expecting it. The creature looked less like an animal the second time. Its front half possessed enough recognizable anatomy to invite the word: six heavy limbs, a low plated thorax, a wedge-shaped head protected by overlapping translucent armor.

The abdomen ruined the illusion. It spread across the basin floor in a huge segmented mass, partly raised, partly sunk among Glassroot trunks. Blue-white lines traveled beneath its plates and continued into the roots touching it.

Skitterers moved around the basin. Nine visible. Yesterday there had been at least twelve.

Rachel’s pack remained near the three-line doorway. No body. No Evan. No Broodguard.

Lucas used Identify.

Glassback Broodmother

Beast — Lv.10

F-Rank

Dungeon-Bound Brood Organism

Additional identification unavailable.

No new generosity from the System.

Owen crouched beside him. “Still ugly.”

“Maybe it thinks you’re ugly.”

“It has six legs and a glowing ass.”

“Different beauty standards.”

Marcus whispered from behind them, “Can we save the flirting with the monster until after?”

The Broodmother shifted. Everyone froze. One front limb scraped stone. A faint pulse moved through the roots touching its abdomen.

Lucas counted silently. One. Two. At two seconds, three Skitterers near the doorway changed direction. At nearly three, a root branch along the basin’s left wall bent inward.

Lucas looked at Emily. She had seen it too. She held up two fingers, then three.

Good. Not instant. Phase one complete. The basin was changed, but not enough to invalidate the plan.

Tessa moved into position with her bow. Daniel and Marcus stayed near the shelf’s descent. Megan remained behind the first bend, close enough to reach them but not exposed to the basin. Emily carried no weapon beyond a short knife and one of Stonefield’s bone-tipped poles. Her job was observation. Owen moved right.

Lucas descended six steps.

The Broodmother’s head turned. Hazard Recognition gave him nothing. No warning. No understood indicator. Yesterday had been the same. The creature simply noticed him.

“Contact,” Lucas said.

Tessa fired. The arrow struck a root two feet from the Broodmother. Not the creature. The root. The shaft bounced away.

The Broodmother jerked. A pulse flashed from its abdomen into the surrounding network.

Lucas counted. One. A Skitterer turned toward the shelf. Two. Two roots began to bend.

“Again,” Lucas said.

Tessa’s second arrow struck another root farther away. The Broodmother reacted again, but differently. Its head moved. No immediate pulse.

Interesting.

“Closer matters,” Emily whispered.

“Or connected strand,” Daniel said.

Lucas pointed to a narrow root descending from the Broodmother’s left side. “Test that cluster.”

Tessa shifted. Her third arrow hit.

The Broodmother slammed one forelimb against the stone. Pulse. Immediate from body to root. Skitterers moved a heartbeat later.

Lucas felt the sequence through mana perception now that he knew where to look. The network was not one continuous flash. The pulse traveled. Fast. But traveled. That meant distance existed inside the system. Distance meant timing.

A Skitterer charged the steps.

Marcus met it. His stone-headed maul crashed against its glassy back and knocked it sideways. Owen’s arrow punched into the softer joint behind a forelimb.

Lucas thrust. Mana Reinforcement tightened through the spearhead and first third of the shaft. The point entered the joint. The Skitterer folded.

Progression flickered at the edge of his awareness. He ignored it.

The fallen creature twitched near the roots. Thin tendrils began moving toward it.

“Corpse response,” Emily said.

“Time it.”

They did. The tendrils reached the Skitterer in six seconds. The Broodmother did not visibly react.

Lucas stared. Different pathway.

“Maybe the roots have their own behaviors,” Owen said.

“Looks like it.”

Which meant killing the Broodmother might not shut down the dungeon. Good to know. Terrible to know.

Another pulse. This one came without an arrow strike. The Broodmother had initiated it. Four Skitterers repositioned. A thick root near Lucas’s left side began lowering across the stairs.

“Change.”

Lucas backed up. The route was narrowing. Not closed yet.

Daniel pointed toward the root. “Same one as yesterday?”

“No,” Emily said. “Adjacent.”

Lucas watched the Broodmother. Its abdomen remained connected at five visible contact points. Maybe more underneath. Each contact glowed faintly. The pulse had originated from one point, not all five.

Iterative Insight stirred around the observation. Not an answer. A pattern. When Lucas used Mana Reinforcement badly, he had once tried to strengthen everything at once. Wasteful. Unstable. Better control came from choosing a path.

The Broodmother might be doing the same thing. Distributed system. Concentrated action.

“Emily. Are the contact points equal?”

She squinted. “No.”

“Brightness?”

“Three dim. Two active.”

“During the last pulse?”

“The rear-left brightened first.”

Lucas’s pulse accelerated. There. Maybe.

“Next pulse, call the first contact.”

“Got it.”

The Broodmother advanced. Not much. Its enormous abdomen dragged against the root bed. The motion exposed a section beneath it. For half a second Lucas saw something pale and narrow between plates. Then roots folded over the gap.

Tessa inhaled sharply. “You see that?”

“Yes.”

“Soft tissue?”

“Maybe.”

Marcus glanced at Lucas. “Phase four?”

“Not yet.”

A second Skitterer came up the stairs. Then two more. The Party gave ground. Owen and Tessa fired from alternating angles. Marcus controlled the center. Daniel used his pole to jam a Skitterer’s limb against the wall rather than trying to break the armor.

Lucas waited. That felt wrong. Every instinct wanted him helping. But this was the test.

The Broodmother pulsed.

“Rear-right!” Emily shouted.

Lucas saw it. One contact point brightened sharply. The light condensed there before spreading into the network. For less than a second, the other connections dimmed.

Singular. The distributed defense had a narrow moment.

Lucas smiled without humor. “There you are.”

Owen heard him. “I hate when you say things like that.”

“Next pulse, we hit the active contact.”

“With what?”

“Everything accurate.”

Marcus crushed a Skitterer’s leg. “That your technical term?”

“Yes.”

They retreated to the shelf. Two Skitterers remained between them and the Broodmother. The creature did not pursue quickly. It did not need to. The chamber pursued for it. Roots shifted. Paths narrowed. Pressure accumulated.

Lucas understood the shape now. The Broodmother was not a fortress because every piece was equally strong. It was a fortress because attacking any piece caused the rest of the system to respond. So they needed to strike while the system was busy responding. Not harder. Narrower.

“Daniel,” Lucas said. “Can you brace the left root without cutting it?”

“Move it?”

“Stop it moving for a second.”

“Maybe.”

“Do not cut.”

“I remember.”

“Tessa, Owen, active contact when Emily calls it. Marcus keeps the stairs. I go down.”

Megan’s voice came from behind them. “How far?”

“Bottom of the steps.”

“No farther.”

Lucas looked back. She stared at him. Medical judgment final.

“Bottom.”

“Good.”

They reset.

Lucas drew mana into his legs. Mana Step waited as potential, not a button. He hated thinking of Skills as buttons. Buttons encouraged people to press them. Skills were movements with consequences.

He descended.

The Broodmother turned. Its front limbs spread. A Skitterer leaped. Marcus intercepted it.

“Go!”

Lucas reached the bottom. The Broodmother was thirty feet away. Too far for the spear. Close enough to feel the mana moving through it. The network around him hummed.

Hazard Recognition sharpened. Something had changed. What?

He scanned. Footing. Roots. Skitterers. Retreat. The cord.

The physical retreat cord behind him had gone slack.

“Change!”

Daniel looked back. A root had risen under the line farther up the gallery, lifting it free from the floor. Not severed. Displaced. The route still existed. The reference had changed.

“Primary still open!” Daniel called.

“Confirmed,” Owen said.

They did not assume. Good.

The Broodmother struck. One hooked limb swept toward Lucas. He stepped back without Mana Step. The hook missed. Stone chipped.

He needed the Skill for the right moment. Not every moment.

The Broodmother struck again. Lucas angled right. His hip protested. He let it.

A blue-white glow gathered beneath the creature.

“Contact!” Emily shouted. “Front-left!”

Everything happened at once. Owen fired. Tessa fired. Daniel jammed his pole beneath the root that had begun lowering toward the steps. Marcus kicked a wounded Skitterer off the ledge.

Lucas moved. Mana Step. Forward.

The impulse hit through his legs. This time he did not try to make it stronger. He made it clean. Foot. Hip. Center. Landing.

The basin jumped toward him. Thirty feet became twenty. Then twelve.

The Broodmother’s front-left contact flared. Two arrows struck within a handspan. One shattered against root armor. Tessa’s lodged at the seam.

The Broodmother recoiled. The pulse hesitated. Not stopped. Hesitated.

Lucas thrust from outside normal reach. Kinetic Thrust. Mana compressed along the spear. He did not aim at the Broodmother. He aimed at the lodged arrow.

The kinetic burst struck the shaft. The arrow drove deeper into the seam. Blue-white light snapped. The contact separated.

For one impossible second, the entire basin went dark. Not black. Dim. Every Glassroot vein dropped to a faint ember. The Skitterers stopped.

The Broodmother screamed.

Lucas had heard animals scream. This was not that. The sound vibrated through stone. Its abdomen lifted. The remaining four contact points blazed.

Emily shouted, “They’re concentrating!”

Exactly. The network had lost one route and compensated through the others.

The Broodmother’s armor changed. The plates over its abdomen pulled inward toward the remaining active contacts. And the pale seam beneath the central thorax opened.

“Now!” Lucas shouted.

Tessa fired. Her arrow disappeared into the exposed seam. Owen’s followed.

The Broodmother convulsed. A forelimb came down.

Lucas saw it too late. Hazard Recognition did not save him. Perception did. Barely.

Diagonal Mana Step. He pushed. Wrong angle. His rear foot slipped on glass dust. Mana fired anyway.

Lucas shot sideways, landed badly, and rolled. Pain ripped through his hip. The hooked limb struck where he had been and cracked the floor.

“Lucas!”

“I’m up!”

He was lying. Then he made it true.

The Broodmother twisted toward Tessa. Marcus left the stairs.

“Marcus, no!”

Too late. He charged down and slammed the maul into the wounded seam. The blow did almost nothing to the armor. It did something to the arrow already buried there. The shaft snapped. The point drove inward.

The Broodmother shrieked again. A pulse erupted through every remaining contact. The roots moved. All of them.

“Retreat!” Daniel shouted.

Correct. They had the opening. They also had the chamber trying to close.

Lucas limped toward the steps. A root swept low. Kinetic Thrust. He struck the root sideways with the mana burst, not to break it, only to alter its path. It shifted inches. Enough.

Owen passed. Tessa passed. Marcus backed up last, hammer raised.

The Broodmother lunged. Its abdomen tore free of one root. Then another.

Lucas stopped. Not because he wanted to. Because he understood. The creature was disconnecting itself to pursue. And every disconnected contact forced the remaining network load narrower.

“Wait.”

Owen grabbed his shoulder. “No.”

“Look.”

The Broodmother pulled forward. Third contact tore loose. Only two remained. Both burned bright enough to hurt Lucas’s mana perception. The plates around them tightened. The rest of the creature’s underside opened wider.

Distributed defense becoming singular. Not because they overpowered it. Because they made it choose between the network and the chase.

Lucas looked at the steps. Looked at the Broodmother. Looked at Rachel’s pack. Still beyond it. Not relevant. Not yet.

“Emily! If it disconnects fully?”

“I don’t know!”

Good answer.

The Broodmother advanced again. One remaining root stretched. The other pulsed. Skitterers around the basin jerked back into motion. One. Two. The delay had grown.

“Last active contact!” Emily shouted.

Tessa had one clear arrow. Owen had two. Lucas had a chipped spear and a hip that hated him. Marcus had already ignored one order. Daniel was holding a root branch off their retreat route with a pole and several bad words. Megan was somewhere behind them, waiting to patch whatever stupidity survived.

Lucas smiled. “Controlled survey.”

Owen swore at him.

The Broodmother pulsed.

Tessa fired. Her arrow struck the last bright contact. Owen’s first arrow missed. His second hit Tessa’s shaft. Not intentionally. It did not matter. The first arrow punched deeper.

Lucas planted his rear foot. Kinetic Thrust. The burst crossed the remaining distance. The arrow vanished into blue-white light. The contact ruptured.

The Broodmother tore free.

Every Glassroot in the basin went dim. The Skitterers froze. The Broodmother did not. It came at them alone.

That was the point. Still Level 10. Still enormous. Still dangerous. But singular.

Marcus met the first limb with the maul and was thrown backward hard enough to hit the stairs. Lucas stepped inside the second. No Mana Step. No trick. Spear Familiarity, practice, timing.

He slid the Channelwood point into the exposed underside seam. Mana Reinforcement concentrated along the shortest useful path. Not maximum. Enough.

The Broodmother twisted. The spear bent. Lucas released one hand before the torque reached his shoulder. The shaft survived.

Tessa’s arrow struck beside his point. Owen’s hit farther back. The creature snapped toward them.

Marcus rose with blood on his lip. “Again!”

They did. Not a duel. Not Lucas against a boss. Seven people had built this moment. Emily had found the active contacts. Daniel had preserved the retreat. Tessa and Owen had broken the connection. Marcus had controlled the stairs and forced openings. Megan had made retreat possible by defining what injury they could and could not carry. Lucas had connected observations.

Now they attacked the thing that remained.

The Broodmother’s armor was still brutally hard. So they stopped attacking armor. Leg joints. Underside. Existing wounds. Every strike was placed. Every miss cost energy.

The creature caught Marcus across the chest with a forelimb and threw him again.

Megan shouted from the steps. “Marcus!”

“I’m breathing!”

“Not the standard!”

Lucas almost laughed. Then the Broodmother charged him.

Hazard Recognition flared. This time there was evidence. Its front plates shifted. Weight moved. The right forelimbs loaded. Lucas knew the pattern because it had already tried it twice.

Mana Step. Forward-left. Not away. Inside.

The movement was ugly. His hip screamed. But the landing held. The Broodmother swept past.

Lucas drove the spear upward into the pale seam beneath its thorax. Kinetic Thrust. The spearhead punched deeper. The chipped metal edge caught.

For a heartbeat, Lucas was connected to the creature. He felt mana. Not the vast network. Just the Broodmother. Dense. Frantic. Concentrated into a body that had spent the entire fight avoiding exactly this condition.

Iterative Insight sharpened everything he had already learned. The angle. The resistance. The line of force. The mistake would be pushing harder.

Lucas changed direction. He twisted the spear a few degrees, following the seam instead of fighting the plate. The point slid.

The Broodmother collapsed onto one side. Marcus roared and brought the maul down on the exposed spear shaft near the head. The impact drove the point through.

The creature convulsed. Tessa fired once more. Owen followed.

Then the Broodmother stopped.

Silence filled the basin. Lucas remained crouched beside it, both hands on the spear. Nobody moved. The Glassroot stayed dim. One Skitterer twitched. Marcus raised the maul. The creature remained still.

System text appeared.

GLASSBACK BROODMOTHER DEFEATED.

Contribution assessment complete.

Dungeon-bound organism neutralized.

Additional dungeon state change detected.

Lucas exhaled.

Then another notification struck.

MANA SPEARMAN LEVEL INCREASED.

Mana Spearman — Lv.7

Automatic Class Growth Applied.

Free Attribute Points Awarded: 2.

His knees almost gave out. Not from the level. From his hip.

Megan reached him. “Sit.”

“I’m fine.”

“Sit.”

He sat. She checked his hip while everyone else maintained the perimeter. The System could announce victory. Humans still had to make sure nothing else was trying to kill them.

“Strain,” Megan said. “Maybe worse if you keep doing that sideways launch.”

“Diagonal.”

“I do not care what the Skill calls it.”

“Fair.”

Explorer progress ticked upward but did not level. Human progress climbed. Lucas checked only after Megan finished.

Human — Lv.5

Progress: 63%

Mana Spearman — Lv.7

Progress: 11%

Explorer — Lv.5

Progress: 38%

Available Attribute Points: 15

He stared at the number. Fifteen. Still unspent.

Marcus sat two steps above him, holding his ribs. “You are officially ridiculous.”

“You ran down the stairs.”

“I was helping.”

“You ignored the plan.”

“The plan worked.”

“That is not how plans work.”

Owen laughed, breathless. “Please keep arguing. It means nobody’s dead.”

The words ended the humor. Nobody’s dead. This time.

Rachel’s pack waited near the doorway. Lucas looked at it. Megan followed his gaze.

“Can you walk?”

“Yes.”

“Without Mana Step?”

“Yes.”

“Then walk.”

They approached carefully. The Glassroot remained dim but not dead. Faint light still existed deep inside the larger trunks.

Emily crouched near one without touching it. “Network’s still active.”

“So the Broodmother wasn’t the dungeon,” Daniel said.

“No.”

“Just using part of it.”

“Or integrated with part of it.”

They did not know. That remained acceptable.

Rachel’s pack lay on its side beside the doorway. One strap was torn. Stonefield’s mark had been scratched into the flap.

Marcus stopped several feet away. No one rushed. Owen checked the ground. Tessa watched the dark approaches. Lucas used Hazard Recognition. Nothing new. That was not permission.

He moved closer. The pack contained rope, two empty water containers, a broken knife, three wrapped strips of dried meat, chalk, and Rachel’s small field notebook.

No body. No explanation. No Evan.

Lucas closed the pack. “We take it.”

Marcus nodded.

Behind them, the Broodmother corpse remained physical. The Glassroot tendrils had not yet approached it. Interesting. Field Record captured that too.

Then Daniel looked at the doorway. Three lines were carved above it. Same motif family as the Sunken Road. Same family as the Three-Line Waystation. The door itself stood partly open. Darkness beyond.

Owen looked at Lucas. Lucas looked at the doorway. Every curious part of him wanted to know. Every lesson of the last three weeks answered first.

“No.”

Owen smiled. “Growth.”

“We’re injured. We just changed the dungeon state. We don’t know what the roots are going to do next, the Broodguard is still missing, and that door has waited longer than we have.”

“Very mature.”

“I hate it.”

“That helps.”

They withdrew.

At the observation shelf, Lucas looked back once. The basin was quieter. Not safe. Quieter. A faint blue-white pulse moved through a root along the far wall. Slow. Independent of the dead Broodmother.

The dungeon was still breathing.

They climbed toward Stonefield carrying Rachel’s pack. No body. No closure. But something recovered. And, more importantly, a method confirmed.

The Broodmother had looked strongest when its defense was spread everywhere. It had become vulnerable only when pressure forced that strength into fewer and fewer paths.

Lucas understood the lesson because he had made the same mistake with mana. Trying to reinforce everything. Trying to solve every weakness. Power spread everywhere became control nowhere.

Concentration was not merely more force in one place. It was choosing the place that mattered.

A notification appeared as daylight finally touched the quarry passage.

SYSTEM-RECOGNIZED TECHNICAL DEVELOPMENT RECORDED.

Lucas stopped. Owen nearly walked into him.

“What?”

Lucas read the next line.

Mana Reinforcement development threshold advanced.

Technique consolidation in progress.

No Skill evolution. Not yet.

Lucas smiled. “Nothing finished.”

Owen stared at him. “That is a deeply annoying answer.”

“It’s accurate.”

They stepped into daylight. Stonefield’s people were waiting.

Lucas carried one end of Rachel’s pack. Daniel carried the other.

Behind them, beneath stone and living glass, the three-line doorway remained unopened. The Broodguard remained missing. The dungeon remained active.

And fifteen Attribute Points waited for Lucas to decide what kind of problem he intended to become.
