# Chapter 4
## West on the Old Road

WEST ON THE OLD ROAD

The Sunken Road had earned its name honestly.

In places only a shallow depression remained beneath the grass. In others, rain had exposed fitted black stones under two feet of soil, their edges still too straight to belong to geology. The road did not run across the Hollow Reach so much as hide under it, a dead thing occasionally showing bone.

Lucas preferred roads that admitted what they were doing.

He stood at the western marker while Priya knelt beside it with charcoal, hide paper, and the expression of someone personally offended by inaccurate distances.

“Again,” she said.

Lucas looked down at the three pale lines glowing across the knee-high stone. Roadclaimant produced the same faint pressure behind his eyes it had the first time.

[Transit Reference]

[F-Rank Infrastructure]

[Condition: Degraded]

[Route Recognition: Partial]

[Authority: Insufficient]

“No change.”

Priya wrote it down. “Good.”

“Usually people prefer change.”

“Usually people aren’t trying to establish whether a magical rock changes its mind between breakfast and noon.”

Daniel shifted the pack on his shoulders. “I’m still stuck on magical rock.”

“Ancient transit reference,” Priya corrected.

“Glowing magical rock.”

Tessa smiled without looking up from the leather wrap she was tightening around her wrist. Owen, twenty yards ahead, was studying the ground where the buried road bent between two low ridges.

Megan checked the straps on the medical pack she had inherited from Sarah for the expedition. “Can we call it whatever makes us leave sooner? I’d like to be back before Daniel’s lunch becomes a biological event.”

Daniel looked offended. “Dried meat lasts.”

“Your dried meat has opinions.”

Lucas tapped the butt of his borrowed spear against the ground. The weapon was another ashwood replacement, longer than the hunting spear he had nearly destroyed against the Gravelmaw. Daniel had selected it personally, reinforced the socket, and then made Lucas promise in front of witnesses not to use Kinetic Thrust unless someone’s life depended on it.

Lucas had objected to the phrasing. Daniel had added Impulse Shift to the promise. Lucas had stopped negotiating.

The Channelwood core remained in Hearth with Emily and the crafters. That bothered him more than he wanted to admit—not because the borrowed spear was bad. It was straight, balanced, and serviceable. It simply wasn’t his.

“Bearing?” Lucas asked.

Priya stood and checked the simple sighting frame she had built from two sticks, cord, and a hanging stone. “West-northwest. Same as the Stonefield report. The visible road matches for the first half mile.”

“After that?”

“Unknown.”

Owen raised a hand. Everyone stopped talking.

He crouched beside the depression in the earth, then waved them forward. Lucas joined him first.

Tracks crossed the old road. Not human. The impressions were broad and deep, each one nearly the length of Lucas’s forearm. Four heavy toes. The soil had been crushed rather than merely displaced.

Owen pointed toward the north slope. “Three days. Maybe four. Big.”

Lucas did not need Identify. The old memory arrived anyway. Stonehide Ravager. A wall of muscle under plated hide. A charge that had turned trees into scenery. Pain in Lucas’s leg. The hard certainty, back then, that he had picked a fight with something operating on a different scale.

Tessa saw his face. “Same one?”

“Could be.”

Daniel studied the track. “You sound thrilled.”

“I’m not.”

That was true. Mostly.

The Stonehide Ravager remained one of the largest unresolved threats near the known settlements. Book-one Lucas might have treated fresh evidence of it as an invitation. Current Lucas looked west along the road. They had a route to inspect, a weapon problem to solve, and six people standing within charging distance of an animal that had nearly killed him when he was weaker and stupider.

The second condition had improved. Probably.

“We detour if the trail crosses again,” Lucas said.

Owen glanced at him.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“That was a look.”

“It was approval.”

“Your approval looks suspicious.”

Owen grinned. “Growth.”

Lucas regretted allowing that word into circulation.

They started west.

* * *

The route-scouting protocol had sounded excessive when written around a table. On the ground, Lucas decided it was barely enough.

Every quarter mile, Priya recorded bearing, terrain, visible infrastructure, water access, animal sign, and a rough travel-time estimate. Owen marked predator evidence separately. Daniel assessed whether a cart, stretcher, or construction crew could actually use the terrain. Megan noted evacuation hazards. Tessa watched the edges and occasionally reminded everyone that maps were less useful if the people making them were eaten.

Lucas used Field Record. The skill captured what he observed, not what he wished were true. That distinction mattered.

[Field Record Updated: Western Sunken Route]

The buried road appeared intermittently for the first mile. Where stone vanished, subtle signs remained: unnaturally even drainage, lines of compacted soil, occasional fragments of pale route material buried beneath roots.

Roadclaimant made those fragments easier for Lucas to notice once he was close. It did not point toward them. It did not draw a line across his vision. It did not whisper turn left, idiot. Lucas appreciated the restraint, although after the third patch of thorn brush he admitted a small directional arrow would have been convenient.

The first checkpoint appeared shortly before midday. Owen spotted the top of it protruding from a bank. They cleared vines and loose soil until a waist-high pillar emerged beside a rectangular stone foundation. A second pillar lay broken ten feet away.

Three parallel lines ran down the standing face.

Lucas approached. Roadclaimant reacted immediately.

[Transit Checkpoint]

[F-Rank Infrastructure]

[Condition: Severe Degradation]

[Route Verification Function: Partial]

[Transit Authority Required]

Then another line appeared.

[Recognized: Roadclaimant]

[Authority Request: Denied]

Lucas stared at it.

Daniel leaned over his shoulder. “It insult you?”

“Professionally.”

Priya was already writing. “Exact wording.”

He repeated it.

Tessa walked around the foundation. “What were you trying to request?”

“Nothing.”

“Comforting.”

Lucas touched the pillar again. The pale lines brightened slightly. A shallow groove opened across the stone face, revealing three inset circles and a narrow slot filled with dirt.

No further notification appeared.

Priya crouched beside him. “Recognition opened the interface.”

“Looks like it.”

“But not authority.”

“Looks like it.”

Daniel pointed west. “Does it tell us where the road goes?”

“No.”

“Excellent infrastructure.”

Lucas cleaned the slot with the tip of his knife. Nothing happened. He tried Identify on each circle.

[Route State Indicator — Inactive]

[Load State Indicator — Unavailable]

[Verification State Indicator — Degraded]

That was all. No translation of the carved symbols beneath them. No explanation of who had built the checkpoint or what counted as authority. Evidence, not answers.

Lucas stepped back. “Record it. We don’t force anything.”

Priya looked up. “You don’t want to experiment?”

“I want to experiment when we understand what failure does.”

Daniel put a hand over his heart.

Lucas pointed at him. “Say growth and I leave you here.”

Daniel wisely said nothing.

They ate beside the checkpoint.

Halfway through the meal, Owen stopped chewing. Lucas saw it and set his food down. Owen held up two fingers, then pointed north.

A branch snapped somewhere beyond the ridge. Not close. Not small.

The group moved without discussion. Packs tightened. Megan stepped toward the center. Tessa took the southern edge. Lucas stood with the borrowed spear angled low.

Another crack. Then silence.

Hazard Recognition did nothing. No immediate evidence of an attack. Lucas hated how sensible the skill was.

They waited. A minute passed. Two.

Owen climbed halfway up the bank, staying below the crest. He returned five minutes later. “Fresh scrape on a cedar. High.” He raised his hand above his head. “And bark dust on the ground.”

“Ravager?” Megan asked.

“Something large.”

Lucas looked at the route ahead. The road bent northwest. The sign lay north. Close enough to matter.

“South detour,” he said.

Priya frowned at the map. “We lose the road.”

“We lose sight of it.” Lucas pointed toward the next low rise. “If the bearing stays consistent, we can parallel it and reconnect beyond the ridge.”

“And if it turns?”

“Then we backtrack.”

Daniel stood. “That’s going to add at least an hour.”

“Yes.”

No one argued. Lucas noticed that too.

They moved south.

* * *

The detour cost ninety-three minutes. Priya made sure everyone knew the exact number.

It also taught them something the direct route would not have. The Sunken Road was not merely a line of stone.

A hundred yards south of the visible bed, Lucas began noticing narrow pale fragments in the soil. Not paving. More like buried ribs extending away from the main route.

He stopped at the third. “Daniel.”

Daniel crouched beside him. The fragment was the width of two fingers and disappeared beneath the earth in both directions.

“Drainage?” Daniel asked.

“Maybe.”

Priya joined them. “Field Record?”

Lucas focused.

[Observed Feature Added: Lateral Route Structure]

Nothing else. Identify returned only:

[Conditioned Structural Material]

[F-Rank]

[Function: Indeterminate]

Emily would have loved it. Which meant she would have asked them not to rip it out of the ground just because they were curious.

Lucas left it alone.

The detour rejoined the old road on the far side of the ridge exactly where Priya’s projected line suggested. She looked unbearably pleased.

Lucas pointed at her map. “You’re going to make that face all afternoon, aren’t you?”

“Yes.”

“Fair.”

The Ravager sign faded behind them. They never saw the creature. Lucas counted that as a successful encounter. That thought would have horrified him a month ago.

* * *

By late afternoon, the land changed. The rolling grass broke into shelves of exposed stone. Sparse pines clung to cracks. The old road became easier to follow because erosion had stripped away the soil above it.

Then the road ended. Not faded. Ended.

Lucas stopped so abruptly Daniel nearly walked into him.

Ahead lay open air. A fault split the landscape from north to south, perhaps eighty feet across at its narrowest visible point and deep enough that the bottom disappeared into shadow. The far wall showed bands of dark stone, pale mineral, and roots hanging from broken earth.

And spanning the gap was a bridge. Or the remains of one.

Two black-stone arches reached from either side toward the center. Between them, a narrow deck crossed the fault, formed from fitted slabs reinforced by pale ribs that glimmered faintly beneath the surface.

A gate stood at the near end.

Lucas walked closer. The gate had no door. Instead, two pillars flanked the road, connected overhead by a stone beam. Three circular recesses were set into the left pillar. The right bore the familiar parallel-line motif.

Priya breathed out slowly. “Well.”

Daniel peered over the edge of the fault and immediately stepped back. “I dislike this road now.”

Tessa tossed a pebble. They waited. And waited. No impact reached them.

Megan looked at her. “Helpful.”

“I learned it’s deep.”

“We knew that.”

“Now we know it’s dramatically deep.”

Lucas approached the gate. Roadclaimant tightened.

[Transit Load Gate]

[F-Rank Infrastructure]

[Route Segment: Western Span]

[Condition: Degraded / Functional]

[Passage State: Sealed]

A second cluster appeared.

[Recognized: Roadclaimant]

[Local Authority: None]

[Transit Authority: Insufficient]

Lucas exhaled. “At least it’s consistent.”

“What?” Priya asked.

“It recognizes the title. Still refuses authority.”

Daniel examined the bridge deck. “Can we cross anyway?”

Lucas stepped toward the threshold. A pale line ignited across the road.

[Passage State: Sealed]

He stopped. The line faded.

“Apparently not.”

Owen moved along the edge, searching north. Tessa went south. Both returned after several minutes.

“No alternate crossing nearby,” Owen said.

Tessa shook her head. “Cliffs get worse south.”

Priya stared across the gap. On the far side, the road continued west between two stone shoulders. And beside it stood another marker. Even at this distance, Lucas could see a faint pale glow.

The route was active on both sides. The bridge simply refused them.

Daniel crouched at the base of the left pillar. “There’s more here.”

Soil and gravel had piled against the gate over years. They cleared it carefully, revealing a flat stone apron divided into rectangular sections. Each section held a symbol. Not letters Lucas could read. One resembled a vertical line over three short bars. Another looked like nested squares. A third was a circle crossed by a diagonal mark.

Priya copied all of them.

Lucas used Identify.

[Transit Load Gate]

Nothing new. He tried touching the nearest panel. A pale projection appeared above the stone.

[Approach Integrity: 41%]

[Span Integrity: 73%]

[Load Verification: Unavailable]

[Passage: Denied]

Daniel’s expression changed. “That,” he said, “I understand.”

Lucas pointed at the first line. “Approach is the problem?”

“Part of it.” Daniel walked backward, studying the stone beneath their feet. “Look.”

Cracks ran through the last thirty feet of road. Several slabs had sunk. One pale structural rib was exposed where the edge had broken away.

“The bridge might hold,” Daniel said. “The ground feeding onto it might not.”

Priya looked across the fault. “Seventy-three percent span integrity sounds reassuring until you remember the alternative is falling forever.”

“Not forever,” Tessa said. “Eventually something happens.”

Megan gave her a look.

“What? Gravity is reliable.”

Lucas crouched beside the exposed rib. The structure reminded him of the broken spear. Strong span. Weak transition. Failure moved somewhere else.

Iterative Insight responded to the pattern, not with information but with the familiar sharpening of attention. He traced the crack with his eyes.

“Could we repair the approach?”

Daniel did not answer immediately. He walked the damaged section twice, testing slabs with a wooden mallet. Then he inspected the soil at both edges and dug beside one sunken stone.

“Repair is generous,” he said. “Stabilize, maybe. We’d need fill, bracing, and something to spread load across the cracked slabs.”

“How long?”

“With six people and what we’re carrying? Too long. And I don’t want to discover the gate expects a standard we can’t meet after dark.”

Lucas agreed. That surprised Daniel enough to be insulting.

“We camp back from the fault,” Lucas said. “No work tonight. We document everything, mark the Ravager detour, and return with a construction team.”

Priya looked at the far marker again. “We came all this way to stop here.”

“We came all this way to learn where here is.”

She considered that, then nodded.

Owen was staring across the bridge.

“What?” Lucas asked.

“Movement.”

Everyone turned. For several seconds Lucas saw nothing. Then a figure stepped from behind the far marker. Human. Too distant for details, but unmistakably human.

The person froze. Lucas lifted one hand slowly. Across eighty feet of impossible air, the stranger did the same.

No one spoke. The fault swallowed ordinary conversation at that distance, wind pulling sound downward and sideways.

A second person appeared. Then a third.

Priya whispered, “People.”

Lucas felt something in his chest loosen. Not relief exactly. Possibility.

The first stranger pointed at the bridge, then at the gate on their side, then made a broad crossed-arm gesture. Closed.

Lucas nodded and pointed at the damaged approach beneath his feet. The stranger crouched on the far side and gestured toward their own road.

Daniel squinted. “Their approach looks intact.”

Lucas thought so too. Which meant the bridge had not sealed because both sides were ruined. It was measuring conditions locally.

Priya was writing so fast her charcoal snapped.

The stranger disappeared for a moment and returned carrying a rectangular board or sign. They held it up. Marks had been painted across it. Not System symbols. English.

NORTHFIRE

Lucas read it twice.

Tessa laughed once, quietly. “Subtle name.”

One of the distant people pointed north, toward a faint smear of smoke Lucas had mistaken for cloud against the western horizon.

Northfire. A settlement. Another organized group close enough to reach the same road from the other side.

Priya raised both hands and pointed east, then toward herself and the others. The strangers waited.

“We need a sign,” Daniel said.

“We don’t have a sign.”

“You’re the one with a title about roads. Improvise.”

Lucas looked at the packs. Megan pulled out a folded piece of treated hide used for medical inventory. “You are not using the clean side.”

Priya seized it, flipped it over, and wrote in thick charcoal.

HEARTH / LOWBANK / STONEFIELD

They held it up between them. Across the fault, the Northfire group reacted visibly. One person turned to another. The first stranger raised both arms overhead. Then pointed at the bridge again.

Lucas looked down at the gate. Approach Integrity: 41%. A road between settlements existed. It just did not work yet.

Somehow that felt more significant than finding an open one. An open road would have been luck. This one required work.

The stranger across the fault held up another board.

TOMORROW

Then pointed at the bridge and mimed lifting something heavy.

Daniel smiled. “I like them already.”

Lucas nodded. “Tomorrow.”

They made camp two hundred yards east of the fault, beyond the damaged approach. No fire. The Ravager sign was too recent, and none of them wanted to advertise.

Priya spent the remaining light updating the map. The line west from Hearth no longer ended in blank space. It ended at a bridge. Beyond the bridge, she wrote one word.

Northfire.

Lucas sat beside Daniel with the borrowed spear across his knees.

“Think they have material?” he asked.

Daniel glanced at him. “For the spear?”

“Maybe.”

“You meet a new settlement across a broken bridge and your first thought is weapon components.”

“My first thought was people.”

“Second thought?”

“Bridge.”

“Third?”

Lucas considered lying. “Material.”

Daniel nodded. “Acceptable.”

The System chimed softly. Not a level.

[Explorer Progress Increased]

[Novel Route Discovery]

[Inter-Settlement Contact Established]

[Infrastructure Hazard Successfully Identified]

Lucas opened his status.

[Human Lv.7: 24%]

[Mana Spearman Lv.9: 72%]

[Explorer Lv.9: 58%]

Three Attribute Points remained unspent.

The gains felt right. No monster had died. No treasure chest had opened. They had followed an old road, avoided a threat they did not need to fight, found a broken bridge, and discovered people on the other side.

Explorer did not seem disappointed by the lack of violence. Neither was Lucas.

He dismissed the display.

Across the dark fault, a small controlled light appeared on the Northfire side, hooded from the surrounding land but visible to them. A signal.

Priya noticed and raised their shuttered lantern once in reply. One flash. Then darkness again.

Lucas looked west. The Hollow Reach had grown larger without adding a single mile. That was what maps did when they stopped showing empty space and started showing consequences.

Tomorrow, they would learn what the bridge counted. Tonight, there was a road beneath them, a settlement beyond them, and something enormous moving somewhere north of both.

For once, Lucas was content not to chase all three problems at the same time.
