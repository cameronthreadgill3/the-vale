# Chapter 29
## Where The Road Vanishes

Lucas learned three things about Arc Lance before breakfast.

The first was that eight paces was not eight paces.

At six, the projection held together often enough to be useful. At eight, it depended on whether his breathing was right, whether his wrist stayed aligned, whether he had rushed the concentration, and apparently whether the universe had developed a personal opinion about his morning.

The second was that repeated use was expensive in a way Kinetic Thrust was not.

Kinetic Thrust wanted a pulse.

Arc Lance wanted a shape.

Holding that shape beyond the spearpoint cost mana every fraction of a second it existed, and the cost became uglier when Lucas tried to force more range out of it.

The third thing was that Megan could detect stupidity from across a field.

“Again?” she called.

Lucas lowered the Channelwood Spear.

He stood on a flat patch outside Lowbank with six stakes driven into the ground at two-pace intervals. The nearest three had shallow cuts in them. The fourth had a blackened-looking mana mark where an unstable projection had struck sideways. The fifth was untouched.

The sixth was beginning to feel smug.

“I was checking consistency.”

“You’ve checked it fourteen times.”

“Fifteen.”

“That did not improve your answer.”

Megan crossed the grass and held out her hand.

Lucas gave her his right wrist.

She rotated it carefully.

“Pain?”

“Not really.”

“That is not a number.”

“Two.”

“Out of ten?”

“Out of however many makes you let me keep practicing.”

Megan stared at him.

“Two out of ten.”

“Better.”

She pressed along the outside of the joint.

Lucas winced.

“Three.”

“Four.”

“Three and a half.”

“You negotiate medical assessments like a man buying a used truck.”

Lucas looked toward the stakes.

“The fifth one is bothering me.”

“The fifth one will survive.”

“That seems premature.”

Megan released his wrist.

“No more Arc Lance until this afternoon. Forward Mana Step only, flat ground, three repetitions. If your hip tightens, you stop.”

Lucas nodded.

She pointed at him.

“Say it.”

“If my hip tightens, I stop.”

“And no combining the two.”

“I wasn’t planning to.”

“You looked at the spear before answering.”

“I own a spear.”

“You are exhausting.”

Megan walked away.

Owen, who had been leaning against a low willow and enjoying the entire exchange, waited until she was out of easy throwing range.

“I think she likes you.”

“She threatens to confiscate my legs.”

“Some people have a complicated love language.”

Lucas ignored him and opened Field Record.

ARC LANCE — CONTROLLED TESTING

Reliable useful range: approximately 5–6 paces. Possible range: 7–8 paces, poor consistency. Beyond: currently wasteful/unreliable.

Rapid repetition:

Second projection noticeably less stable. Third should not be attempted without necessity.

Observed failure indicators:

Wrist rotation.

Concentration widening before release. Spearpoint dropping during projection. Trying to add power after shape is already unstable.

Working principle:

Do not pay mana to preserve a bad projection.

He closed the record.

The western conduit expedition was leaving in twenty minutes.

That mattered more than the fifth stake.

Probably.

Lowbank had spent the previous evening arguing about whether anyone should follow the buried maintenance line farther northwest.

Claire Morgan’s position had been simple.

If ancient infrastructure crossed land people were already using, they needed to know where it went.

Daniel’s position had been more cautious.

If ancient infrastructure was causing the ground to collapse, following it was an excellent way to discover new places to fall into holes.

Nia Patel had pointed out that those positions were not actually contradictory.

So they had compromised in the traditional human way.

They would follow it carefully while complaining.

The expedition consisted of Lucas, Owen, Nia, Priya, Daniel, Marcus, and Megan.

No formal Party.

No dungeon entry.

No excavation.

No opening access plates.

The goal was route discovery and surface observation.

The western line had already become too important to ignore.

By midmorning, they were beyond Lowbank’s established fishing and gathering range.

The terrain changed gradually.

Wet meadow became scrub.

Scrub became broken limestone.

The ground rose in long shallow folds toward a ridge that had looked close from the western void and turned out to be irritatingly far away.

The conduit gave them something to follow, but not a road.

Every hundred yards or so, Daniel found another maintenance plate or a worked casing edge beneath eroded soil.

Some were obvious.

Others existed only because they knew what to look for.

Lucas used Field Record constantly.

Bent cedar.

Split limestone shelf.

Dry channel.

Maintenance plate.

Collapsed plate.

Blue seam visible through fracture.

No active illumination.

Then another.

Then another.

The line did not run straight.

It curved with terrain.

That interested Daniel.

“Whoever built this wasn’t just connecting two points.”

Lucas looked at the exposed casing near Daniel’s boot.

“Why?”

“Because if all they wanted was shortest distance, they’d cut straighter through this ridge.”

“Unless they couldn’t.”

“Right.”

Daniel pointed northwest.

“Or unless they were following something.”

Nia glanced back.

“Water?”

“Maybe.”

“Mana?” Owen asked.

Daniel shrugged.

“Maybe.”

“Ancient underground worm migration?”

Priya did not look up from her map.

“Write that one down so future civilizations know exactly where our scholarship failed.”

Owen grinned.

Lucas checked the casing again.

Environmental Resource Routing.

That was what Identify had given them.

Not water.

Not mana.

Not Glassroot.

Resource.

Broad enough to be almost rude.

They continued.

Around noon, the conduit disappeared.

Not underground.

Gone.

Daniel found the last access plate half buried beneath a slab of limestone.

Twenty yards beyond it, there was nothing.

No casing.

No plate.

No blue seam.

The terrain ahead dropped into a narrow valley filled with silver-gray grass.

Owen crouched beside the final plate.

“Broken?”

Daniel examined the ground.

“Maybe.”

Lucas walked a slow semicircle around the area.

Hazard Recognition gave him nothing dramatic.

No invisible warning.

No magical certainty.

But several observations refused to sit comfortably together.

The conduit had followed a consistent depth.

The soil here was thinner.

If it continued normally, portions should have been exposed.

They were not.

He opened Field Record and compared the last six observed plates.

Spacing varied.

Direction varied slightly.

Depth had not varied much.

Then the infrastructure vanished at the valley edge.

“Change,” Lucas said.

Everyone stopped.

Marcus looked around.

“What changed?”

“The infrastructure.”

Daniel nodded immediately.

“Last confirmed plate here.”

Priya joined them.

“Could have collapsed.”

“Could have,” Lucas said.

Nia pointed down the valley.

“Or it stops.”

They spread out without moving farther downhill.

Owen found the first clue.

“Here.”

A worked stone post lay almost completely buried beneath grass.

It was not part of the conduit.

Lucas cleared dirt from one face with his fingers.

Three vertical marks.

Not identical to the other carvings.

Same motif family.

Identify responded.

BOUNDARY REFERENCE

F-Rank Construct Condition: Damaged / Inactive Function: Environmental Delineation Language Markers Detected Language: Unrecognized

Lucas read it twice.

“Environmental delineation,” Priya said.

“That sounds suspiciously like a fence written by someone who hated normal words,” Owen said.

“Or a border,” Marcus said.

“Or a hazard marker,” Daniel added.

Lucas looked into the valley.

Silver grass moved in the wind.

Nothing else did.

No birds.

He realized that slowly.

Not because Hazard Recognition told him.

Because the last hour had contained birdsong almost continuously.

Here there was wind.

Grass.

Their breathing.

Nothing overhead.

“Change,” Lucas said again.

Owen’s expression sharpened.

“Quiet.”

Nia nodded.

“Too quiet.”

Marcus rested one hand on his weapon.

Megan looked at Lucas.

“No combat Mana Step.”

“I know.”

“Your face says you were about to discover an exception.”

“My face is neutral.”

“Your face is a liar.”

They did not enter the valley immediately.

Instead, Priya marked the boundary post while Daniel and Nia walked parallel to the rim.

They found two more posts.

Both damaged.

Both forming a broad arc.

The conduit did not cross the boundary.

It terminated against it.

Or disappeared beneath it.

The distinction mattered.

Lucas stood at the center post and looked across the valley again.

The far side was wrong.

Not dangerous wrong.

Visual wrong.

A line of dark trees stood perhaps half a mile away, but the spacing between trunks seemed inconsistent when he shifted position.

He took three steps left.

A gap disappeared.

He stepped right.

It returned.

“Owen.”

“Yeah?”

“Look at the trees.”

Owen did.

Then moved sideways.

“Huh.”

Marcus frowned.

“What?”

“The distance is lying.”

“That is not a thing distance does.”

“Apparently today it is.”

Priya joined them.

The group spent ten minutes testing sight lines.

The valley was real.

The grass was real.

The far tree line was real.

But landmarks shifted subtly depending on angle.

Not moving.

Revealing and concealing.

Daniel threw a fist-sized stone downhill.

It landed exactly where expected.

“No obvious spatial distortion,” Lucas said.

Owen raised an eyebrow.

“Comforting sentence.”

Lucas used Identify on the grass.

VEILGRASS

F-Rank Plant Environmental Adaptation: Mana Diffusion Additional information unavailable

That was new.

Nia crouched near the edge without crossing the line.

“Mana diffusion.”

Lucas extended his perception carefully.

Mana existed everywhere in the Hollow Reach, but here it behaved strangely.

Not stronger.

Spread.

Every attempt to focus on one point bled sideways into the surrounding environment.

He thought about Arc Lance.

A Skill based on concentration.

Wonderful.

Priya looked toward the boundary marker.

“Maybe this is what the posts mark.”

“Maybe,” Lucas said.

He refused to make it certainty.

They had enough evidence for a test.

Not a conclusion.

Marcus pointed to a limestone outcrop thirty yards inside the valley.

“We need to know whether walking in there does anything.”

Megan folded her arms.

“Strong argument. Who would you like me to medically disapprove of first?”

Lucas studied the route.

Flat ground.

No visible holes.

No creature sign.

Three boundary posts gave them a reliable return line.

“Cord,” he said.

Daniel smiled faintly.

“Finally, something normal.”

They tied a light cord to the center boundary post.

Lucas, Owen, and Nia would advance twenty yards.

Everyone else stayed outside.

No Mana Step.

No Arc Lance unless needed.

No pursuit.

If visual conditions changed enough that they could not see the boundary team clearly, they returned immediately.

Lucas stepped across the line.

Nothing happened.

His System did not announce a dungeon.

No notification appeared.

The second step felt normal.

The fifth did not.

Not physically.

Perceptually.

He looked back.

Daniel stood at the boundary.

Twenty feet away.

Then Lucas looked forward and back again.

Daniel seemed farther.

The cord said otherwise.

“Change,” Lucas called.

“Observed,” Daniel answered.

Owen turned slowly.

“Sound’s weird too.”

Lucas listened.

Daniel’s voice had arrived clearly.

But the echo had not come from behind Daniel.

It had come from the left.

Nia pointed down.

“Grass.”

The Veilgrass around their boots leaned away from them.

Not from wind.

Mana shimmered faintly along the blades.

Lucas’s Hazard Recognition pulled his attention toward the combination.

Changed visual reference.

Changed sound behavior.

Changed plant response.

Enough evidence to treat the environment itself as an active hazard.

Not enough to explain why.

They backed out.

The distortion faded within three steps of crossing the boundary.

Priya exhaled.

“So.”

Owen looked at the valley.

“I vote we call it Absolutely Normal Meadow.”

“No,” six people said.

They spent another hour mapping the boundary instead of entering farther.

The arc of markers extended nearly half a mile before terrain blocked them.

At the northern end, the ancient conduit reappeared.

That changed everything.

It did not cross the valley.

It curved around it.

Daniel found two access plates running north along the outer boundary.

“Routing around the environment,” he said.

“Or around whatever is inside it,” Marcus replied.

Lucas looked from the conduit to the silver valley.

Environmental Resource Routing.

Environmental Delineation.

Veilgrass adapted to mana diffusion.

Ancient infrastructure intentionally avoided the region.

That was evidence.

Still not an explanation.

They followed the conduit north.

The route climbed.

The silver valley remained to their left, widening as they gained elevation.

From above, it became obvious that the strange terrain was much larger than they had thought.

The first valley was only an eastern finger.

Beyond the ridge lay a broad basin.

Miles across.

Silver grass.

Dark forest islands.

Broken stone ridges.

Mist that did not move correctly with the wind.

And ancient boundary markers tracing the rim.

Lucas stopped.

Everyone else did too.

The System appeared.

REGIONAL ENVIRONMENT DISCOVERED

VEILED BASIN

Rank: F Status: Active Boundary Condition: Partially Functional Environmental Effects: Incomplete Identification Known Access: Eastern Boundary

Exploration contribution recorded.

The notification vanished.

Owen stared into the basin.

“Well.”

Marcus looked at Lucas.

“Please tell me your Explorer thing has a map.”

“It does not.”

“I knew that.”

“You ask every time.”

“One day I’m hoping the answer changes.”

Lucas opened Field Record.

VEILED BASIN — INITIAL OBSERVATIONS

F-Rank regional environment.

Active.

Boundary partially functional.

Environmental effects incompletely identified. Eastern access known.

Observed at eastern finger:

Veilgrass diffuses mana.

Visual landmark reliability decreases after crossing boundary. Sound direction may distort. Plant response changes around entrants. Physical thrown-object path appeared normal in one test.

Ancient conduit route bends around boundary rather than crossing eastern finger. Boundary Reference constructs use three-line motif family.

Unknown:

Purpose of boundary.

Whether Basin is natural, constructed, altered, contained, or some combination. Nature of “partially functional.” Cause of perceptual effects. Whether interior creatures are affected similarly. Whether conduit avoidance is deliberate safety design or unrelated routing constraint.

He paused.

Then added:

Do not trust distance before testing it.

Priya was already sketching.

Megan looked at the sun.

“We turn around in twenty minutes.”

Marcus glanced into the Basin.

“We just found it.”

“And it will remain found tomorrow.”

“Probably,” Owen said.

Megan pointed at him.

“Do not make geography worse.”

They used the remaining time to locate a safer observation point on the rim.

That was when Lucas found the broken spear.

Not a spear exactly.

A polearm shaft.

Ancient.

Gray wood or something that had once been wood, half fossilized by mana exposure.

It lay beneath a collapsed marker wall twenty yards north of the overlook.

No body.

No armor.

Just the broken shaft and a rust-colored metal fitting.

Identify gave little.

WEATHERED POLEARM REMNANT

F-Rank Material / Equipment Remnant Condition: Ruined Age: Unavailable Additional information unavailable

Marcus crouched beside it.

“Someone fought here.”

“Or dropped it,” Lucas said.

“Broken in half?”

“People drop things enthusiastically.”

Owen nodded. “I once dropped a phone off a roof.”

Priya looked at him.

“Were you fighting the roof?”

“It knew what it did.”

Lucas examined the ground.

No recent tracks.

The remnant could have been years old.

Centuries.

No way to know.

But the break angled toward the Basin.

The metal fitting carried three shallow grooves.

Not enough to call the three-line motif.

He recorded it without interpretation.

Then the Basin flashed.

Not Glassroot blue.

White.

A narrow vertical line appeared between two distant forest islands.

Gone almost immediately.

Everyone saw it.

“Lightning?” Nia asked.

“No thunder,” Owen said.

Daniel looked at the sky.

No clouds.

Lucas waited.

Nothing.

Hazard Recognition did not react because there was no pattern yet.

One flash.

Unknown cause.

Then Arc Lance twitched inside his awareness.

Not the Skill activating.

The mana around the spearpoint felt less stable.

Lucas raised the weapon carefully.

“Something changed.”

Megan’s head snapped toward him.

“Physical?”

“Mana.”

He did not cast.

He gathered the beginning of the Arc Lance shape without releasing it.

The concentration spread wider than normal.

Veilgrass mana diffusion.

Except they were outside the Basin.

Barely.

He canceled the Skill.

“Diffusion reaches the rim here.”

“Confirmed?” Priya asked.

“No. One attempt.”

She nodded.

They left.

That decision became more valuable ten minutes later.

The Ridgeback Stalker found them on the return route.

Owen spotted it first.

“Right ridge.”

The scarred animal stood above the conduit line.

If it was the same Stalker, it had followed them farther north than before.

If it was not, Hollow Reach had a deeply irritating surplus of scarred predators.

Identify gave the same result.

RIDGEBACK STALKER

Beast — Lv.8 F-Rank

Marcus moved to Lucas’s left.

“No arrows this time unless it commits.”

Tessa was not with them, but Owen had his bow ready.

“Happy to learn from other people’s mistakes.”

The Stalker descended.

Slowly.

Not charging.

Lucas felt the temptation to test Arc Lance at range.

Bad reason.

He resisted it.

The creature stopped near an access plate.

Its head lowered.

The conditioned Glassroot seam beneath the damaged casing lit pale blue.

The Stalker recoiled.

Same broad behavior as before.

Still unexplained.

Then the Basin flashed behind them.

White.

The Stalker’s attention snapped past the humans toward the ridge.

That was new.

Its posture changed.

Not fear.

Focus.

It moved sideways.

Lucas’s Hazard Recognition caught the sequence because he had now seen enough of its charge behavior to recognize the difference.

Shoulders lowered.

Rear legs compressed.

Not toward them.

Toward the Basin.

“Change,” Lucas said.

Marcus saw it too.

“It’s going past.”

The Stalker launched.

Owen did not shoot.

Good.

The animal crossed their route fifteen yards ahead, hit the slope, and bounded toward the Basin rim.

Then it vanished.

Not into trees.

Not behind stone.

One moment Lucas could see it.

The next, silver grass folded around empty ground.

Owen whispered, “Nope.”

Marcus took one step forward.

Lucas caught his arm.

“Don’t.”

“I wasn’t chasing it.”

“You took a chasing-shaped step.”

Marcus looked at Lucas’s hand.

Lucas released him.

“Fair.”

They waited.

Ten seconds.

Twenty.

The Stalker reappeared sixty yards north of where it had entered.

It was moving hard.

Too hard.

Something had happened inside.

A dark line crossed its flank.

Blood.

The creature did not look at the humans.

It ran west along the outer ridge and disappeared.

Nobody followed.

Lucas recorded everything.

Entry point.

Reappearance point.

Time estimate.

Visible injury.

No observed attacker.

No claim that the Basin teleported it.

No claim that the distance was physically impossible.

Perception inside the Basin was already unreliable.

The simplest explanation was that they could not see the actual path.

Probably.

He underlined probably.

They returned to Lowbank before sunset.

Claire listened without interrupting while Priya laid out the map.

The western conduit.

The boundary arc.

The Veiled Basin.

The Stalker entering and returning wounded.

The white flashes.

The ancient infrastructure routing around the Basin.

When Priya finished, Claire looked at Lucas.

“How dangerous?”

“We don’t know.”

“That answer is getting popular.”

“It keeps being accurate.”

“Can people accidentally walk into it?”

“Yes.”

That mattered.

Claire immediately assigned runners to update western route warnings.

No dramatic debate.

No claim of ownership.

No demand to exploit the Basin.

Just the first useful question.

Can someone walk into this by accident?

Lucas liked that.

After dinner, Megan checked his wrist again.

“Better.”

“My fifth stake is doomed.”

“Tomorrow.”

He tested one forward Mana Step on flat ground.

Clean.

No hip pain.

He stopped anyway.

Megan looked almost disappointed that she could not yell at him.

Then his System notification appeared.

Mana Spearman has reached Level 8.

Automatic Class growth applied.

+1 Agility +1 Intelligence +1 Wisdom

Free Attribute Points gained: 2

Arc Lance development recorded. Integrated ranged/melee combat pathway established.

Lucas read the notification twice.

Not because the numbers surprised him.

Because the phrase integrated ranged/melee combat pathway did.

The Class was recognizing what he had been trying to build.

Not a mage holding a spear.

Not a spearman with one ranged trick.

A fighting style where distance changed continuously.

He checked his progression.

Human — Lv.5

Progress: 84%

Mana Spearman — Lv.8

Progress: 17%

Explorer — Lv.6

Progress: 58%

Available Attribute Points: 4

He left them unspent.

The Basin mattered more.

The next morning, they would need markers that did not rely on sight alone.

Cord.

Sound signals.

Timed turns.

Physical references.

Maybe paired observers.

Maybe none of that would work once they went deeper.

But that was the point of testing.

Try.

Observe.

Change.

Repeat.

Lucas opened Field Record one final time before sleep.

VEILED BASIN — WORKING ENTRY PRINCIPLES

1. Mark the boundary physically before crossing.

2. Do not trust visual distance without confirmation.

3. Sound direction may be unreliable.

4. Maintain a return reference independent of sight.

5. Treat mana diffusion as a potential limitation on concentrated Skills.

6. Do not pursue creatures across the boundary.

7. A known route outside the Basin is more valuable than an unknown shortcut inside it.

He paused.

Then added an eighth.

8. The Basin does not need to be hostile to be dangerous.

Outside Lowbank, night settled over the river.

Far to the northwest, beyond the dark ridge, a white line rose briefly into the sky.

This time it lasted long enough for everyone on watch to see it.

And beneath it, for one impossible-looking moment, the mist over the Veiled Basin opened like a curtain.

Lucas saw stone.

Not a wall.

Not a ruin.

Something much larger.

A shape at the center of the Basin.

Then the mist closed.

Distance became uncertain again.

The only thing Lucas knew for sure was that the Veiled Basin was not empty.
