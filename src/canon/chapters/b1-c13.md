# Chapter 13
## What the Road Remembered

Lucas spent the next day doing exactly what Sarah told him.

This surprised everyone.

Owen checked twice to make sure Lucas had not been replaced.

Daniel accused him of trying to build credibility before doing something stupid.

Sarah simply looked suspicious.

Lucas accepted all three reactions.

The thigh wound had earned caution in a way lectures had not. He walked only inside Hearth. He changed the bandage when told. He did not test Kinetic Thrust. He did not spar. He did not follow anybody into the forest.

He spent most of the afternoon seated beside Priya with the bark map from the Sunken Road between them.

That was not rest, according to Sarah.

It was close enough.

Priya had improved the map again.

The main road ran west from Hearth's known territory.

The branch road split northwest at the broken junction marker.

The square symbol sat at the end of the branch.

Three vertical marks inside it.

The same three-line motif appeared on the first wayfinding marker.

Farther west along the main road, Priya had copied another symbol from the broken map. Something shaped like an open triangle with a horizontal bar.

Two destinations.

At least.

Maybe dozens beyond what the surviving marker showed.

"Could be a settlement."

"Could be a gate."

"Could be a quarry."

"Could be a cemetery."

"Also possible."

Good.

It was not a translation Skill.

Looking harder at unknown writing did not magically turn it into English.

That boundary mattered.

The System had called the language unrecognized.

Lucas intended to keep respecting the word.

"That's disappointing."

"No fighting unless you have to. You stop if the leg starts throbbing. You do not turn a four-hour trip into an eight-hour trip because you found something interesting."

Sarah's eyes narrowed.

"That last one was specifically for you."

"I noticed."

"And you come back before dark."

"Yes."

"Who is going?"

"Same group."

Owen, Daniel, Emily, Priya.

No Marcus.

His ribs were improving, but Sarah had banned him from anything more adventurous than lifting cookware.

Grant had wanted two additional fighters.

Lucas had argued against it.

Not because more protection was bad.

Because eight people moving through uncertain ruins became eight people making noise, touching things, stepping on things, and having independent opinions about what qualified as valuable.

Five was enough.

Six, technically.

Tessa joined them at the gate with her bow.

Lucas raised an eyebrow.

"Grant?"

"Grant."

"I said five."

"He said six."

Owen grinned.

"Authority structure."

Lucas looked at Tessa.

"You planning to shoot ancient architecture?"

"Only if it charges."

"Reasonable."

They left Hearth after sunrise.

The pace was faster than Chapter Twelve.

Not fast.

Better.

Lucas could walk without leaning on the Channelwood Spear.

He still used the butt cap for support on steep ground.

Sarah would have objected.

Sarah was not present.

This was not deception.

It was geography.

They reached the first exposed roadstone in a little over an hour and the intact marker twenty minutes after that.

Lucas stopped anyway.

The marker remained where they had left it.

Gray-black stone.

Three grooves.

Hooked symbol.

Two rows of unreadable script.

Nothing had moved.

Nothing had changed.

That felt important.

He had half expected the road to become less real overnight.

Instead, it waited.

Daniel crouched beside the drainage channel.

"Water came through last night."

A thin wet line darkened the stone.

The ancient culvert had worked.

Lucas stared at it.

However old the road was, the people who designed it had understood that rain would outlive them.

They reached the junction before noon.

Priya compared the broken marker to her map.

No mistakes.

The northwest branch road climbed through thicker forest.

Mostly buried.

Its stones were narrower than the main route, but the construction style matched.

Daniel pointed to the edge.

"No drainage here."

"Smaller road," Emily said.

"Or newer."

"Can you tell?"

"No."

Lucas liked that answer.

They followed the branch.

At first it was easy.

The road climbed steadily.

The forest thinned.

Ashwoods gave way to darker trees with red-gray bark and narrow leaves.

Identify called them Needle Crown.

F-Rank Flora.

No useful information beyond that.

The road passed between two low ridges.

Then disappeared.

Owen stopped.

"That's inconvenient."

The stone ended beneath a landslide.

Earth and broken rock covered the route from one ridge to the other.

Trees had rooted into the collapse.

Old trees.

Lucas studied the slope.

The road was beneath perhaps ten feet of debris.

"Go around?"

Daniel pointed uphill.

"Probably."

Priya looked at the angle on her sketch.

"If the road held direction, it should come out on the other side about two hundred yards northwest."

Owen smiled.

"Look at us doing navigation without a glowing map."

Lucas started uphill.

His leg pulled.

Not bad.

He slowed.

Tessa noticed.

No comment.

Appreciated.

The detour took twenty minutes.

Owen found the road first.

Not the paving.

A retaining wall.

Three feet of worked stone emerged from the hillside.

Same fitted construction.

The road itself had been buried almost completely under leaf litter.

Daniel scraped a boot through the dirt.

Stone appeared.

"Found it."

They continued.

Half an hour later, the road narrowed again.

Eight feet became six.

The trees closed in.

The grade steepened.

Lucas checked his leg.

Ache.

Not throb.

Good.

They had planned to turn around at two hours beyond the junction.

They reached that point and found nothing.

Owen checked the sun.

"We should mark here."

Lucas looked at Priya.

She was already cutting a charcoal mark into a piece of bark tied around a tree.

TURN POINT.

Efficient.

Emily sat on a stone.

Tessa watched the trees.

Lucas looked up the branch road.

It continued another hundred yards before vanishing around a ridge.

Nothing visible.

No square.

No ruins.

No answer.

Sarah's voice existed in his head now.

That was deeply concerning.

Owen said, "We turn."

Lucas nodded.

Then something caught the light.

Not metal.

Stone.

"What?"

"Maybe."

"No."

"Trees if you are very charitable."

Traitor.

"I see it."

"We agreed."

"Five minutes."

"You are supposed to be responsible."

"Exactly."

The road curved around the ridge.

And stopped at a wall.

Not a ruin.

A wall.

Twenty feet high.

Dark stone blocks.

Vines covering half the surface.

The straight pale line Lucas had seen was the top edge where sunlight struck exposed rock.

Everyone stopped.

No one joked.

The branch road ended at a broad stone terrace half swallowed by the forest.

Stairs climbed from the road to the wall.

Or what Lucas first thought was the wall.

Then he saw the opening.

An arch.

Twelve feet high.

Eight wide.

Beyond it, darkness.

Emily whispered, "Well."

Daniel said nothing.

Lucas felt the hair rise along his arms.

The stonework was different from the road.

Finer.

Smaller joints.

The blocks around the arch had been shaped into precise wedges.

No mortar visible.

At each side of the opening stood narrow pillars carved with the three-line symbol.

Lucas used Identify.

F-Rank Construct

Original function: Unavailable

Language markers detected.

Inactive.

Lucas read the word twice.

"What does yours say?" Owen asked.

Tessa looked into the opening.

"Inactive what?"

"Exactly."

They approached carefully.

The terrace was buried under soil in places.

Roots split between paving stones.

Daniel stopped beside the stairs.

"Look."

The steps had been worn down in the center.

Not by weather alone.

Feet.

Traffic.

Thousands of passages.

Maybe more.

Lucas put a hand on the nearest pillar.

Cold.

Mana?

He focused.

Something remained.

Not active.

Not flowing.

A faint density inside the stone.

More than ordinary roadstone.

Less than a living creature.

Structured.

Old.

Iterative Insight sharpened only the contrast with the road markers he had touched yesterday.

Different material response.

Different construction.

No explanation.

Good.

He withdrew his hand.

"There's mana in it."

Emily looked at him.

"Active?"

"No."

"Residual?"

"Maybe. Structured, but faint."

Daniel stepped through the arch.

Lucas's stomach tightened.

Nothing happened.

No light.

No trap.

No System prompt.

They followed.

The arch opened into a courtyard.

Lucas forgot the leg.

The forest had eaten a settlement.

Not a city.

Not even a village, based on what they could see.

But definitely a place built for people.

Stone foundations emerged from ferns.

Three walls stood waist-high around a rectangular structure whose roof had vanished.

Another building leaned into a massive tree that had grown through its center.

A line of broken columns crossed the courtyard.

At the far end, steps rose to a second terrace built into the hillside.

And everywhere, carved into walls and lintels, the unreadable symbols repeated.

Three vertical lines.

Hooks.

Angles.

Circles split in half.

Rows of writing.

No English.

No Latin.

No recognizable alphabet.

No convenient translation.

Lucas breathed out.

"Ruins."

Not loudly.

"I don't know what you're talking about."
