# Chapter 27
## The Ground Between

The hole west of Lowbank was not a hole.

That was the first useful thing Lucas learned about it.

The second was that somebody had already tried to throw a rock inside.

Nia Patel stood with her hands on her hips at the edge of the reed field and looked at Jacob Wells.

“I told you not to.”

Jacob pointed toward a shallow depression twenty yards ahead. “I threw it from back here.”

“That does not make it less thrown.”

“It makes it safer thrown.”

Lucas shifted the Channelwood Spear against his shoulder and looked past them.

The report had reached the neutral observation rise at dusk. Blue light. New hole. Half a mile west of the detour.

By morning, Claire Morgan had assembled a small group to verify it before rumor converted the thing into a glowing cavern full of magical treasure.

Lucas had heard three versions before breakfast.

In one, the hole was large enough to swallow Hearth.

In another, a trader had climbed into it and returned with a crystal worth fifty pounds of smoked fish.

The third involved a singing woman underground.

Nobody could identify the origin of the singing woman.

Lucas suspected boredom.

The actual site was less dramatic and more dangerous.

A shallow oval of lowered earth sat among waist-high reeds. Maybe thirty feet across. The ground had sagged rather than opened. Thin cracks spread through dark soil around the perimeter. Blue-white light showed through one crack only when Lucas crouched low enough to catch the angle.

Hazard Recognition stirred.

Not a warning bell.

A prioritization.

The inward lean of the reeds.

The dampness around the rim.

A patch of exposed roots pulled taut toward the center.

Three small slumps on the western side.

Lucas stopped six paces farther back than Jacob had.

“Change.”

Everyone froze.

Daniel looked at him. “What changed?”

“Nothing since we arrived. I’m calling the condition.”

He pointed with the spear.

“Western edge has multiple slumps. Reeds are leaning farther there. We approach from east if we approach at all.”

Nia nodded immediately. “Water runs west after rain here.”

Daniel crouched and pressed two fingers into the soil where they stood.

“Ground’s wetter than yesterday?”

“Had mist overnight,” Nia said. “No real rain.”

Claire had come from Lowbank with Nia and Jacob. Priya and Owen had joined from the route team. Daniel was there because holes in the ground had become his unwanted specialty.

Megan had come for Lucas.

Not the hole.

Lucas had objected to that description.

Megan had not cared.

She stood beside him now, arms folded.

“You’re limping less.”

“That sounded almost positive.”

“It wasn’t clearance.”

“I know.”

“No running.”

“I know.”

“No Mana Step.”

“I know.”

Owen leaned toward Priya. “I think she should write it on his spear.”

“I think she should write it on his forehead,” Priya said.

Lucas ignored them.

The hip was better.

Not good.

Better.

Walking no longer produced the deep pull that had followed the quarry rescue. He could rotate normally if he moved slowly. Megan had tested the joint before sunrise and upgraded him from no Mana Step to still no Mana Step.

Medicine contained many subtle distinctions.

Daniel used a long probe cut from straight timber to test the ground ahead.

Six feet from the visible depression, the pole sank four inches.

Five feet, seven inches.

At four feet, it punched through.

Daniel stopped.

Nobody moved closer.

He withdrew the pole.

The last foot came back wet and coated in gray clay.

“Void under the crust,” he said.

“How deep?” Claire asked.

“Don’t know.”

“Can we find out without standing on it?”

Daniel looked at Lucas.

Lucas looked at the spear.

“No.”

Daniel smiled. “Good answer.”

They backed away and circled north.

The depression continued beneath the reeds farther than it appeared from the route. Priya sketched as they moved. Field Record accepted Lucas’s observations, preserving the relation between the stony-rise detour, the old dry-ridge route, and this new sag.

No invisible map unfolded.

No line appeared telling him where to walk.

He still had to look.

That was becoming comforting.

On the north side, they found the first real opening.

A crack had widened beneath a fallen reed clump, exposing a wedge of darkness perhaps two feet across.

Blue-white light pulsed far below.

Lucas counted.

One.

Two.

Three.

Nothing.

Not the familiar double pulse.

A steady dim glow.

Daniel anchored a rope to two trees well outside the sag and tied the probe pole to its end.

They lowered it horizontally toward the opening.

The ground did not collapse.

The pole reached the crack.

Daniel rotated it.

Blue light flashed below.

Everyone stepped back.

Lucas’s Focused Mana Reinforcement activated almost reflexively along his hands and forearms before he caught himself.

No load.

No reason.

He released it.

Iterative Insight pressed the mistake into awareness.

Anticipation was useful.

Anxiety wearing anticipation’s coat was not.

“Contact?” Emily would have asked if she were here.

She was at Stonefield monitoring the quarry pulse sequence.

Lucas crouched beside Daniel, safely behind the rope anchor.

“What did the pole touch?”

“Couldn’t feel it.”

“Root?”

“Could be stone. Could be Glassroot. Could be the bottom of the crack.”

Claire glanced at Jacob.

“No more rocks.”

Jacob raised both hands.

“I have learned.”

Nia snorted.

They waited.

The blue light dimmed again.

Priya marked the time.

The plan was simple enough to sound foolish when explained.

Emily’s team at Stonefield would signal the next strong quarry pulse using red cloth relays. Midpoint watchers would pass the signal toward Lowbank. The group at the void would compare the human signal to anything observed underground.

It would not establish transmission speed.

It might not establish anything.

But synchronized ignorance was better than seven people remembering different times later.

The first relay came forty minutes after they arrived.

A red square appeared on the low rise east of them.

Priya called, “Quarry strong pulse.”

Everyone watched the crack.

Nothing.

Five seconds.

Ten.

Lucas felt ridiculous.

Then the light beneath the depression brightened.

Not all at once.

A line appeared from the eastern side of the void and moved west.

Nia whispered, “There.”

Lucas tracked it.

The glow split.

One branch continued west.

The other angled northwest beneath the reeds.

Then both faded.

Priya wrote.

Lucas opened Field Record.

WESTERN VOID — OBSERVED EVENT

Human relay reports strong quarry pulse before local illumination. Local blue-white line enters observed void from east. Line divides west / northwest. Delay unreliable due relay timing. No conclusion on physical connection or signaling.

He added:

Observed geometry extends beyond visible depression.

That mattered.

The void was not a destination.

It was a crossing.

Or part of one.

Maybe.

Claire looked west.

“If that line continues, where does it go?”

“Unknown,” Lucas said.

Owen sighed. “You’ve become extremely committed to that word.”

“It keeps surviving.”

Priya folded the corner of her paper map flat.

“The route west of here isn’t mapped.”

Nia pointed through the reeds. “I’ve gone maybe another mile along the water channels. Fishing. Ground rises after that.”

“Any blue light?”

“No.”

“Any holes?”

“Not that I saw.”

Claire looked at Lucas.

He disliked the direction of the look.

“You’re the Explorer.”

“Nia knows the ground better.”

“Then you’re an Explorer standing next to someone who knows the ground better.”

“That I can work with.”

They did not enter the void.

They did something more useful.

They followed the surface above the northwest branch.

Nia led.

Lucas and Priya documented.

Daniel tested suspicious ground.

Owen ranged twenty yards ahead only when terrain allowed.

Megan continued the sacred medical tradition of glaring whenever Lucas’s stride became too long.

The reed field narrowed into wet scrub.

Half a mile west, the ground rose exactly as Nia had described. Low limestone broke through the soil in pale shelves. The blue line was no longer visible, but the surface offered a pattern Lucas would have missed two weeks ago.

Depression.

Stone rise.

Depression.

A shallow channel with no water.

Then another stone rise.

Not proof.

A route worth checking.

Hazard Recognition highlighted one change before Lucas understood why.

The insects had stopped.

He raised a hand.

“Change.”

Owen froze ahead.

“What?”

“Quiet.”

Everyone listened.

No buzzing.

No reed clicks.

No distant birdlike calls.

Nia’s face tightened.

“That’s new.”

“Since when?” Lucas asked.

“Since about thirty seconds ago.”

Good.

Evidence.

Owen backed toward them rather than pushing forward.

A dark shape moved between two limestone shelves.

Lucas used Identify.

RIDGEBACK STALKER

Beast — Lv.8 F-Rank

“Stalker,” he said.

Marcus was not with them.

Lucas noticed the absence because Marcus had become the person he most trusted to absorb a bad collision without pretending it had been a good idea.

Then a voice behind them said, “Of course it is.”

Lucas turned.

Marcus Reed came down the eastern rise with two Ironbound hunters behind him.

Owen blinked. “Did you grow out of the rocks?”

“Claire sent a runner when you kept going west.”

Claire looked offended. “I sent a runner because we found a new hazard beside a shared route.”

Marcus pointed toward the Stalker. “And apparently a shared predator.”

The Ridgeback Stalker stepped into view.

Same species.

Possibly the same animal they had yielded the grazer carcass to days ago.

Lucas could not prove that.

The beast’s broad toes spread over limestone. Bony ridges rose along its back. Its jaw opened sideways enough to make human anatomy feel like a polite suggestion.

It did not charge.

It watched.

Lucas lowered the spearhead.

“Back east.”

Marcus looked at the terrain behind them.

“Wet ground.”

“North ridge.”

“Longer.”

“Dry.”

The Stalker took one step forward.

Hazard Recognition did nothing magical.

Lucas already knew a predator approaching was dangerous.

What the Skill caught was the ground.

A hairline crack beneath Marcus’s left boot widened as his weight shifted.

“Marcus. Right.”

Marcus moved without asking.

The limestone shelf under his old position dropped.

Only three inches.

Enough.

Blue-white light appeared in the crack.

The Ridgeback Stalker reacted instantly.

Not toward Lucas.

Toward the light.

Its head snapped down.

Its ridges lifted.

Then it lunged sideways.

Everyone moved.

Not a charge at them.

A violent retreat from the illuminated crack.

The beast bounded onto the higher shelf, claws scraping stone.

One of the Ironbound hunters loosed an arrow.

“Hold!” Marcus barked.

The arrow hit stone behind the Stalker.

The beast spun.

Now it charged.

“North!” Lucas shouted.

The group broke toward the dry ridge.

Lucas did not run.

Could not.

Megan was beside him.

Marcus saw it.

He turned back.

“Keep moving.”

“I am moving.”

“That is an aggressive walk.”

The Stalker closed on the rear.

Owen fired.

His arrow struck a shoulder ridge and glanced away.

Tessa was not here.

Marcus planted his shield.

Lucas hated the geometry immediately.

“Don’t stop it.”

Marcus glanced at him.

“Wasn’t planning to.”

Good.

They had learned the same lesson from different monsters.

The Stalker came in low.

Marcus angled right.

Lucas angled left.

A funnel with no wall.

The beast chose the larger target.

Marcus.

At the last moment, Marcus stepped aside and struck the Stalker’s jaw with the shield rim.

Not to halt it.

To turn it.

The animal’s head twisted.

Its body followed.

Lucas had one clean line to the forward leg.

He activated Focused Mana Reinforcement.

Hands.

Spear shaft.

Front shoulder.

Back.

Support leg.

The load path was familiar.

Kinetic Thrust waited.

He did not use it.

Too much force on uncertain footing.

Instead he stabbed low and withdrew.

The spearhead cut behind the Stalker’s wrist joint.

Shallow.

Enough to make it stumble.

The force changed as the beast twisted.

Lucas moved the reinforcement focus late.

The shaft kicked sideways in his hands.

Pain flashed through his right wrist.

Not injury.

Warning.

He released pressure.

Marcus drove his shoulder into the Stalker’s flank.

“Now!”

They moved north.

The beast recovered.

It followed.

Twenty yards.

Fifteen.

Then the ground behind them pulsed blue.

The Stalker stopped.

Actually stopped.

Its front claws dug trenches into the soil.

It stared at the light.

Then backed away.

Lucas noticed something else.

Its injured foreleg.

His spear had cut it, yes.

But an older scar crossed the same limb.

Long.

Pale.

Not from today.

Possibly nothing.

Possibly evidence this animal had encountered something sharp before.

The Stalker retreated west.

Nobody pursued.

Marcus breathed hard beside Lucas.

“You could’ve used the movement Skill.”

“Megan says no.”

Marcus looked at Megan.

She nodded.

Marcus considered this.

“I respect the terrifying small woman.”

“Correct,” Owen said.

Megan pointed at Lucas’s wrist. “That?”

“Fine.”

She took his hand anyway.

After a moment she released it.

“Actually fine.”

Lucas smiled.

“Write down the date.”

“Don’t ruin it.”

The fight had lasted less than twenty seconds.

It changed the expedition.

The Stalker’s reaction to Glassroot light joined the Marsh Strider’s earlier agitation.

Two species.

Two reactions.

Still not control.

Maybe avoidance.

Maybe mana sensitivity.

Maybe coincidence layered on fear.

Lucas recorded only what happened.

RIDGEBACK STALKER — WESTERN RIDGE OBSERVATION

Lv.8.

Approached during local quieting of insects. Retreated sharply from sudden Glassroot illumination. Re-engaged humans after arrow impact. Stopped pursuit after second local illumination event. Interpretation unresolved.

He added the older foreleg scar as a separate observation.

Marcus read over his shoulder.

“You always write this much?”

“Less than Priya.”

Priya did not look up from her paper.

“I heard that.”

They should have returned.

Lucas knew it.

Claire knew it.

Megan definitely knew it.

Then Nia found the stone.

Not natural limestone.

Worked stone.

A rectangular block protruded from the north ridge beneath soil and roots.

Daniel cleaned one corner with his fingers.

A groove appeared.

Then another.

Parallel.

Priya crouched.

“Roadwork?”

Daniel shook his head slowly.

“Too narrow.”

Lucas used Identify.

BURIED CONDUIT CASING

F-Rank Construct Condition: Damaged / Partially Exposed Function: Environmental Resource Routing Additional information unavailable

Nobody spoke for several seconds.

Marcus finally said, “That seems relevant.”

Lucas looked back toward the blue-lit depression.

The Glassroot vein beneath Lowbank territory.

The western void.

The branching light.

Now a constructed conduit casing on the surface line.

Still not proof that every Glassroot formation belonged to one system.

But this was different.

Infrastructure.

Not merely growth.

Daniel exposed another foot of the casing without disturbing the soil around it.

A narrow seam ran along the top.

Inside the seam, something pale blue glimmered.

Glassroot.

Or material that looked like it.

Lucas did not touch it.

“Stop there.”

Daniel nodded.

No argument.

Field Record accepted the discovery.

A notification appeared.

EXPLORATION DEVELOPMENT

Meaningful extension of established route knowledge recorded. New environmental hazard documented. Previously unknown constructed resource infrastructure identified beyond established settlement map.

Explorer progression increased.

Then another.

Explorer has reached Level 6.

Automatic growth applied:

+1 Perception +1 Endurance

Free Profession Attribute Point gained: +1

New Profession Skill pathway available through continued route-network and constructed-environment discovery.

Lucas closed the notice before the last line could become temptation.

No new Skill yet.

Good.

Explorer Lv.6 felt less like power than the sharpening of a lens.

His body did not suddenly know where the conduit went.

The ground did not become transparent.

He simply had more capacity to notice and endure the work of finding out.

“Level?” Owen asked.

“Explorer six.”

“Congratulations. You found plumbing.”

“Ancient magical plumbing.”

“Much better.”

Marcus crouched beside the casing.

“If this runs toward Stonefield...”

“We don’t know that,” Lucas said.

Marcus gave him a look.

“I was going to say if.”

“Sorry.”

“You’ve weaponized uncertainty.”

“It’s useful.”

Claire stood.

“We mark it. We don’t dig it. We tell Stonefield and Hearth.”

“And traders?” Marcus asked.

Claire considered.

“We tell everyone the same safe version. Construct found. Do not excavate. Route around it until reviewed.”

Lucas nodded.

That was enough.

The return trip took longer because they established a broad exclusion around the casing and the western depression.

The Ridgeback Stalker did not return.

Neither did the insects for almost half a mile.

When the buzzing finally resumed, Lucas marked that too.

By late afternoon, they reached the stony-rise detour.

A cart from Lowbank waited there with smoked fish for Stonefield.

A Stonefield cart approached from the opposite direction carrying clay vessels and cut stone.

Both stopped when Priya updated the route notice.

Blue-lit void west.

Constructed conduit casing.

Ridgeback Stalker sighting.

No excavation.

No Glassroot harvesting.

Old dry-ridge route still closed.

The drivers complained.

Then they changed course.

Information moved.

Not perfectly.

Not heroically.

But fast enough to keep two carts away from ground that might collapse beneath them.

Lucas thought that counted.

At the neutral observation rise, Emily was waiting.

“So,” she said, “we had another double pulse.”

Lucas set down his pack.

“When?”

Priya and Emily compared times.

The strong pulse had occurred while the group was west of the void.

The weaker east/down pulse followed at Stonefield.

At almost the same period, the western ground had illuminated beneath the Stalker encounter.

Almost.

Human timing remained too crude.

Emily looked at Lucas’s notes.

“Branching local line. Conduit casing. Creature reaction.”

“And no proof they’re connected.”

“I know.”

She smiled faintly. “I was going to say that.”

Apparently everyone was learning.

Fragment One sat inside its containment frame.

Dim.

No visible response now.

Lucas looked west.

Beyond Lowbank, beyond the new void, the terrain rose into broken ridges they had never mapped.

Somewhere beneath at least one of those ridges lay constructed infrastructure carrying, containing, or once intended to carry something mana-responsive.

That changed the question.

Glassroot might be alive.

It might be a resource.

It might be a dungeon organism.

It might also be growing through infrastructure built to move it.

Those possibilities were not mutually exclusive.

Marcus joined him at the edge of the rise.

“You know what I hate?”

“Several things.”

“The fact that you were right about not claiming the quarry network.”

Lucas looked at him.

Marcus continued before he could answer.

“Don’t enjoy it. We found the same problem under Lowbank and now west of Lowbank. If Ironbound had planted flags on the first hole, we’d look stupid.”

“We can still look stupid.”

“Good. I was worried you’d become unbearable.”

Lucas smiled.

Marcus nodded toward the spear.

“That thing you did with the Stalker. Your reinforcement moved.”

“Late.”

“It held.”

“Late.”

Marcus shook his head. “You know, sometimes a thing can work and still need improvement.”

Lucas stared at him.

“That sounded suspiciously wise.”

“I hate you too.”

There it was.

Reluctant respect, apparently, sounded mostly like insults delivered without hostility.

Lucas could work with that.

Megan approached from behind them.

“Walk.”

Lucas frowned. “I have been walking all day.”

“Walk ten paces.”

He did.

She watched his hip.

“Again.”

He did.

“Any pain?”

“Stiffness. No sharp pain.”

“Rotation?”

He tested it slowly.

Better than morning.

Megan thought for a moment.

“Tomorrow, controlled Mana Step testing. Flat ground. No combat. No diagonal use.”

Lucas almost smiled.

“Forward only?”

“Forward only.”

Owen overheard.

“The prisoner has earned yard time.”

Megan pointed at him. “I can restrict you too.”

“For what?”

“I’ll find something.”

Owen left.

Lucas opened his Status before dinner.

Human — Lv.5

Progress: 71%

Mana Spearman — Lv.7

Progress: 58%

Explorer — Lv.6

Progress: 21%

Available Attribute Points: 2

Strength: 11 Agility: 21 Vitality: 17 Endurance: 19 Perception: 24 Intelligence: 18 Wisdom: 19 Willpower: 10

The Class progress surprised him slightly.

Then he remembered the Stalker.

Focused Mana Reinforcement under changing force.

A controlled spear strike without Kinetic Thrust.

Fighting beside Marcus instead of around him.

Not enough for a level.

Enough to matter.

Human progress followed more modestly.

He left both free Attribute Points unspent.

The last light faded beyond the western ridges.

At Stonefield, watchers would be staring into the quarry.

At Lowbank, someone would be moving the route signs again.

At Hearth, Grant would be hearing that ancient infrastructure had appeared beneath another settlement’s travel territory.

And somewhere underground, the Glassroot Broodguard was still alive.

Lucas opened Field Record one last time.

He added the conduit casing to the western route.

Then he wrote:

The ground between settlements is not empty space.

Roads cross it.

Predators cross it.

Information crosses it.

Old systems cross it too.

He paused.

Then added one final line.

We only notice ownership after we notice value. The ground was already there.

He closed the interface.

Far to the east, too distant to see, Stonefield’s quarry pulsed.

A runner raised a red cloth on the first relay hill.

Another answered.

Then another.

The signal moved west by human hands.

Lucas watched the last cloth rise near Lowbank.

Under the dark ridge beyond it, something blue answered.
