# Chapter 18
## The First Descent

The Glassroot Depths looked different once Lucas stepped in knowing he meant to keep going.

Yesterday the threshold chamber had been a discovery. Today it was a door.

Behind him daylight narrowed through the quarry breach until it was just a pale rectangle framed by worked stone. Ahead, Glassroot spread across the cavern walls in branching veins, each translucent strand carrying its slow blue-white pulse into the dark.

Eight people stood just inside the chamber. Lucas counted them anyway.

Owen. Marcus. Tessa. Emily. Rachel. Daniel. Megan.

Eight.

Outside, Stonefield’s support team waited with rope, food, spare torches, and instructions not to become heroes. Lucas approved of that instruction enough that he suspected Grant had not written it.

Rachel tightened the strap on her pack.

“Left shelf?”

Lucas looked across the chamber. The route they had chosen yesterday ran along the cavern wall before bending behind a massive Glassroot trunk. The center descent was shorter but covered in broken stone. The right route disappeared beneath an old arch where visibility died almost immediately.

Nothing had changed overnight. That did not mean nothing had moved.

“Left,” he said.

Marcus rolled one shoulder beneath his shield strap. “Formation?”

Tessa answered first. “You in front. Lucas beside you. I want room behind you, not someone’s head in my line.”

“Charming.”

“Your head is large.”

Owen coughed into his fist.

Marcus looked at him.

“Nothing,” Owen said.

Lucas checked the Party interface. Eight names. All functional. No health bars. No promises. Good.

They crossed the threshold.

A System notification appeared.

FORMAL DUNGEON PARTICIPATION RECORDED

GLASSROOT DEPTHS

F-RANK

Exploration, combat, resource interaction, discovery, and dungeon-specific accomplishments may contribute to relevant progression.

Rewards are not guaranteed.

Survival is not guaranteed.

Lucas dismissed it.

“System says we’re officially making questionable decisions,” Owen murmured.

“It used more words,” Emily said.

“Same meaning.”

The shelf narrowed after forty yards. Marcus moved first with his shield angled toward the open cavern. Lucas stayed half a step behind, Channelwood Spear low enough not to catch the ceiling roots. Tessa followed with an arrow nocked. Rachel and Owen watched the rear and upper walls. Daniel, Emily, and Megan occupied the center.

It was slower than traveling aboveground. That was deliberate.

Field Record grew behind Lucas as they moved. Threshold chamber. Left shelf. Stable stone for twenty-three yards. Glassroot trunk at wall junction. Ceiling height reduced. Loose edge near outer drop.

Nothing appeared ahead of observation. The Skill did not paint a path through darkness. Lucas appreciated its honesty.

The clicking began after six minutes.

Everyone stopped.

Click.

Click-click.

A pause.

Then three rapid taps from somewhere below.

Tessa lifted her bow. Owen pointed down. A shape moved between two Glassroot clusters. This time it was close enough.

Identify.

Glassback Skitterer

Beast Lv.4

F-Rank

Dungeon-adapted arthropod.

Additional information unavailable.

The creature crawled into the light. Dog-sized had been accurate in the same way describing a bear as furniture-sized would technically be accurate. Six segmented legs held a flat body two feet off the ground. Its back was covered by overlapping translucent plates. Blue lines glowed beneath them, echoing the Glassroot around it. Two hooked forelimbs clicked against stone. Its head had no obvious eyes.

Owen whispered, “I liked it better blurry.”

A second Skitterer appeared. Then a third.

Marcus raised his shield. “They coming?”

Lucas watched. The nearest creature turned sideways. Its hooked limbs scraped a Glassroot strand. The strand brightened. Mana shifted. Lucas felt the pulse travel through the root. Then another root answered twenty feet farther away.

“Wait,” he said.

Marcus did.

The Skitterer clicked twice. A response came from below.

Emily’s eyes widened. “They’re using it.”

“Maybe,” Lucas said. Not certainty.

The nearest Skitterer moved toward them. Ten yards. Eight. Marcus braced. Lucas felt mana gather inside the creature. Not in the legs. Across its back.

“Something’s building.”

The Glassback plates flashed. A pulse snapped into the wall root. Every nearby Glassroot vein lit at once. The cavern became blue-white daylight for half a second.

Tessa cursed.

Then the Skitterer lunged.

Marcus caught it on the shield. The impact was harder than Lucas expected from something its size. Its forehooks raked across wood. Lucas thrust beneath the shield edge. Spear Familiarity aligned his hands. Mana Reinforcement followed the Channelwood grain. No Kinetic Thrust. Not yet.

The spearhead struck between two plates. Resistance. Then penetration. The Skitterer twisted violently. Lucas released pressure rather than let the motion wrench his shoulder. Marcus shoved. Tessa’s arrow hit a rear leg joint. The creature collapsed sideways. Rachel drove a short spear into its underside. It stopped moving.

The other two came immediately. Not fleeing. Not hesitating.

Owen’s arrow hit one in the front plate and bounced.

“Joints!” Lucas called.

Tessa had already learned the same lesson. Her second arrow punched through a leg hinge. The creature stumbled.

The third climbed the wall. That was new. It moved across vertical stone with disturbing speed. Emily ducked as it launched. Daniel slammed an ashwood pole upward. The Skitterer hit it, twisted, and landed behind Marcus.

Formation gone.

Lucas pivoted. His thigh pulled. Pain, not failure. He shortened his grip. The Skitterer lunged at Megan.

Kinetic Thrust.

Lucas timed the mana pulse with the forward drive. The Channelwood Spear accelerated. Too much. He knew it before impact. Iterative Insight sharpened the error. Pulse early. Tip drift. His spear struck the edge of the creature’s plate instead of the seam. The blow knocked it sideways rather than penetrating. Useful. Expensive. The rebound jarred his hands.

Megan moved away. Daniel hit the creature again. Marcus turned and buried the rim of his shield against its body, pinning it to the floor.

“Now!”

Lucas did not use Kinetic Thrust again. He placed the spearhead into the exposed underside and drove with both hands. The creature spasmed. Stopped.

Across the shelf, Owen and Tessa finished the injured second Skitterer with Rachel’s help.

Silence returned. Everyone breathed.

Lucas checked the Party. Eight functional. Megan had a shallow cut across one forearm where a hook had caught her sleeve. Marcus’s shield carried four deep grooves. Daniel’s pole had cracked. No one was dead. Good start.

The System waited until Lucas’s pulse slowed.

Mana Spearman — Lv.4

Progress: 8% -> 46%

Explorer — Lv.2

Progress: 94% -> Lv.3

Explorer Lv.3 achieved.

Automatic growth applied:

+1 Perception

+1 Endurance

Free Profession Attribute Point gained: +1

Human — Lv.3

Progress: 98% -> Lv.4

Human Lv.4 achieved.

Automatic racial growth applied:

+1 Vitality

+1 Perception

Human — Lv.4

Progress: 11%

Lucas read the notifications once. Then dismissed them. No fireworks. No transformation. But the world sharpened subtly. The edges of Glassroot pulses became easier to distinguish. His breathing recovered faster than he expected.

Megan looked at him. “You level?”

“Race and Profession.”

Marcus grinned. “Finally.”

“You say that like you were waiting.”

“Ninety-eight percent was annoying me.”

Owen nodded. “Same.”

Lucas looked at them. “My progression is emotionally difficult for both of you?”

“Deeply.”

Emily crouched near the dead Skitterer. “Can I touch this one?”

“After Megan,” Rachel said.

Megan cleaned her own cut first. Shallow. No Skill needed. That was preferable. Then she checked Lucas’s thigh. The old wound remained closed.

“Pain?”

“Some.”

“Worse?”

“No.”

“If that changes, you tell me.”

“Yes.”

Megan narrowed her eyes. “That sounded suspiciously cooperative.”

“I’ve grown.”

“By one level.”

“Exactly.”

She almost smiled.

Emily examined the Skitterer without cutting it open. Identify provided little more.

Glassback Skitterer Carapace

F-Rank Beast Material

Mana-responsive layered protection.

Harvest condition: Intact / Partial damage

Lucas touched one plate. Mana inside it was fading. The pattern reminded him of Glassroot. Not identical. Related somehow.

“The pulse before it attacked,” Emily said. “It entered the root.”

“Observed,” Lucas replied.

“And another root lit farther away.”

“Observed.”

“Communication?”

“Possible.”

Owen pointed deeper into the dungeon. “Which means the bugs may have just told every other bug we’re here.”

Nobody mocked him. That was also possible.

Lucas added the encounter to Field Record. Three Glassback Skitterers. Lv.4. Carapace resists direct penetration. Leg joints and underside less protected based on combat observation. Glassroot pulse occurred immediately before coordinated attack. Possible signaling mechanism. Unconfirmed.

The last word mattered.

Daniel replaced his cracked pole with one of the spare shafts. “Turn back?”

Marcus looked surprised.

Daniel shrugged. “Rule was we ask after meaningful change. We found hostile creatures and possible signaling.”

Lucas checked time. They were nowhere near the fixed turn-back point. Supplies good. One minor injury. Formation had broken but recovered. Retreat path clear.

“Continue,” Megan said before anyone could look at her. “My arm is nothing.”

Tessa nodded. Rachel agreed.

Lucas looked at Marcus. “Continue.”

Owen sighed theatrically. “Apparently democracy has failed me again.”

They moved.

The shelf bent behind the giant Glassroot trunk. Beyond it, the dungeon changed. Worked stone became more common. Not walls exactly. Terraces. Channels. Low retaining structures built into the cavern floor. Glassroot grew through them.

Daniel stopped at the first channel. “That was made.”

Identify.

Dungeon Conduit Channel

F-Rank Construct

Condition: Active / Overgrown

Function: Resource transfer

Additional information unavailable.

Emily stared. “Resource transfer.”

“Could be water,” Rachel said.

“Could be mana,” Lucas said.

“Could be bugs,” Owen offered.

Everyone looked at him.

“What? We have evidence for bugs.”

Unfortunately true.

The channel ran beside the shelf before disappearing beneath a Glassroot mass. Lucas felt mana moving through it. Weak. Steady. Different from the pulses. Iterative Insight helped him compare. Glassroot pulse: quick, directional, transient. Channel flow: constant, diffuse. He could distinguish them. He still did not know what either meant.

They reached the first branch in the left route. A narrow passage continued forward. Another descended through a worked arch. The arch carried symbols.

Lucas’s chest tightened. Three vertical lines inside a square. The Three-Line motif. Priya was not here to copy it. Field Record was.

Lucas stopped. Daniel saw it next.

“Same.”

“Looks same,” Lucas corrected.

Daniel nodded. Same distinction they had used at the Waystation.

Emily stepped closer without touching.

Identify.

Dungeon Transit Arch

F-Rank Construct

Condition: Active / Damaged

Language markers detected.

Language: Unrecognized.

Rachel exhaled. “So the people who built your road built this?”

“Maybe,” Lucas said.

Marcus groaned. “There it is.”

“What?”

“Maybe.”

Lucas smiled. “It’s a useful word.”

They did not descend through the arch. Not yet. The left shelf remained the agreed route. Field Record marked the arch as observed, unexplored.

They continued another hundred yards. The cavern narrowed into a corridor of Glassroot columns. The light here was brighter. Mana density rose. Lucas felt it against his skin. Not pressure. Presence.

Breathing after the Skitterer fight had already become easy. Now mana recovery felt different too. Faster? He checked. Not enough evidence. He waited.

Ten minutes later, after two controlled uses of Mana Reinforcement, the difference persisted. Mana replenished faster in the corridor than near the entrance.

System notice appeared.

ENVIRONMENTAL MANA DENSITY: ELEVATED

Sustained exposure may influence mana-sensitive development.

Effects vary by physiology, Class, Skills, duration, and adaptation.

No immediate benefit guaranteed.

Lucas stared at the last line. The System had apparently met humans.

Emily read her own notification. “Influence development.”

“Not free levels,” Lucas said.

“I know.”

Marcus looked between them. “You both said that too quickly.”

They reached a shallow basin. Glassroot grew in a circle around it. Inside lay bones. Many bones. Skitterer shells too. Some old. Some fresh.

Tessa stopped them with a raised fist. “Movement.”

Lucas saw it. Something larger shifted behind the far roots. Identify failed. Too obscured.

Then a Skitterer stepped into view. Lv.5. Another. Lv.4. Then four more.

Owen whispered, “That’s more bug than I ordered.”

The Glassroot around the basin began pulsing. Not one strand. All of them. Lucas felt mana gathering through the network. The pattern was stronger than before. Coordinated.

“Back,” he said.

Marcus did not argue. They withdrew twenty yards. The Skitterers followed to the edge of the basin. Stopped.

Interesting.

Lucas stepped backward another five yards. No pursuit.

Rachel watched them. “Nest?”

“Possible.”

“Food pile?”

“Possible.”

Marcus pointed at the bones. “Either way, not worth six at once.”

Correct.

Then the larger thing moved again. A broad shape passed behind the roots. Higher than Marcus’s waist. The Skitterers clicked. Every one of them turned toward the sound.

Lucas did not need Identify to make the decision. “We leave the basin.”

Nobody objected.

They backtracked to the transit arch. That created a choice. Return now. Or inspect the descending route.

Daniel checked time. “Forty minutes to turn-back.”

Megan checked her arm. Fine. Lucas checked his thigh. Sore. Still stable. Party functional. They had already discovered hostile ecology, possible signaling behavior, ancient infrastructure, elevated mana, and a defended basin. Enough for one expedition.

Curiosity argued otherwise. Lucas ignored it.

“Back to Stonefield.”

Emily looked at the arch. Then nodded. That mattered.

They turned around.

The dungeon objected halfway to the threshold. The Glassroot lights went out. Not dimmed. Out. Darkness swallowed the shelf.

Someone inhaled sharply.

“Torches,” Daniel said.

Flame appeared. Small orange circles replaced the blue-white glow. Lucas felt mana move through the roots. A massive pulse traveled from deeper below toward the entrance. One root after another awakened for a fraction of a second as it passed. Like something breathing backward through the dungeon.

Then the light returned.

System notification.

DUNGEON STATE FLUCTUATION OBSERVED

Cause: Unavailable

Lucas hated notifications that managed to be both informative and useless.

Clicking erupted below them. Many sources.

“Move,” Marcus said.

They moved. Not running. Fast. Controlled. Lucas’s thigh protested. He shortened his stride. Owen stayed beside him without comment.

They reached the site of the first fight. The dead Skitterers were gone. Not moved slightly. Gone. Blood remained. Carapace fragments remained. Bodies did not.

Rachel swore.

Lucas added it to Field Record. Corpse removal occurred within approximately one hour. Cause unobserved. No assumptions.

They crossed the threshold chamber. The quarry breach appeared ahead. Daylight had never looked so aggressively beautiful.

Lucas stepped through. The Party notification changed.

FORMAL DUNGEON PARTICIPATION PAUSED

Exit from active dungeon environment confirmed.

No completion state achieved.

No completion state. Interesting. Not failure. Not success. The dungeon apparently expected more.

Thomas Reed met them outside.

“Eight?”

“Eight,” Rachel said.

Only then did the Stonefield support team relax. Lucas understood. Counting people had become a ritual in the Hollow Reach. One day the number would come back wrong. Not today.

They spent the next two hours documenting everything before memory softened edges. Priya had come from Hearth with the support team and immediately attacked Lucas with charcoal and questions.

“Arch first.”

“Why first?”

“Because you said symbols.”

“Fair.”

He described the three-line square. Daniel described construction. Emily described Glassroot distribution. Tessa described sight lines. Owen described Skitterer movement. Marcus described impact force. Megan described injury risk. Rachel described the defended basin. Nobody described what the large shape was. Because nobody knew.

Priya drew a hard black line at the limit of observation. Lucas approved.

The System rewarded the work after they finished.

Explorer — Lv.3

Progress: 37%

Human — Lv.4

Progress: 19%

Mana Spearman — Lv.4

Progress: 46%

No extra level. No dungeon-completion chest. No magical shower of loot. Lucas preferred it. The progression felt connected to what he had actually done. Fight. Observe. Map. Withdraw. Learn.

His available Attribute Points now totaled seven. Six held before. One from Explorer Lv.3. He considered them while Priya finished the map. The dungeon had given him evidence. Perception mattered. Endurance mattered. Vitality mattered. Wisdom mattered for control. Agility mattered when formations broke. That was the problem with evidence. Sometimes it supported everything.

He kept the points. For now.

Sarah would complain. Then she would inspect his leg. That prediction required no Skill.

They returned to Hearth before dark. Grant listened to the report without interrupting. When Lucas finished, Grant looked at the copied dungeon map.

“How many people can we safely send tomorrow?”

“Eight inside,” Lucas said.

“I mean support.”

“More outside helps. More inside makes the retreat worse.”

Marcus nodded. Grant accepted it.

“Do we close the quarry?”

Rachel answered. “Stonefield already stopped work around the breach.”

“Guard?”

“Yes.”

“Permanent?”

Thomas had sent instructions with Rachel. “Rotating until we know whether anything comes out.”

Good.

Daniel pointed to the transit arch. “This is the bigger problem.”

Grant frowned. “Why?”

“Same symbol family as the Waystation. Maybe.”

Lucas smiled faintly. Daniel had learned.

Grant looked at Lucas. “Meaning the dungeon could connect to the road network.”

“Meaning somebody used similar markings in both places. Anything beyond that is guessing.”

“You make discoveries exhausting.”

“That’s because discoveries are mostly finding better questions.”

Owen leaned back. “Put that on a monument.”

“No.”

Sarah entered. Lucas sighed.

“Pants up enough to see the wound,” she said.

Grant looked away. Owen did not.

“Medical professionalism,” Sarah told him. Owen turned around immediately.

The thigh wound was swollen. Not badly. No blood. No reopening. Sarah pressed around it. Lucas hated medicine.

“Pain?”

“Four.”

“During the dungeon?”

“Six at the end.”

She stopped pressing. “And you kept walking.”

“We were leaving.”

That answer apparently passed.

“Tomorrow?” Lucas asked.

Sarah looked at him for a long moment. “If swelling is down in the morning.”

Not no. Progress.

Later, alone beside the fire, Lucas opened his Status again.

Human — Lv.4

Progress: 19%

Mana Spearman — Lv.4

Progress: 46%

Explorer — Lv.3

Progress: 37%

Attributes

Strength: 11

Agility: 15

Vitality: 14

Endurance: 13

Perception: 18

Intelligence: 13

Wisdom: 14

Willpower: 10

Available Attribute Points: 7

The numbers had moved. The dungeon had done what dangerous new environments were supposed to do. It had forced growth. Not twenty-two levels in an afternoon. Not a miracle. One Race level. One Profession level. Meaningful Class progress.

More importantly, Lucas now understood something he had not yesterday. The Glassroot Depths were not simply caves containing monsters and resources. They were a system. Creatures interacted with Glassroot. Glassroot interacted with old infrastructure. Mana moved through channels. Something removed corpses. Something caused the entire dungeon to pulse and darken. And somewhere beyond the defended basin, something large lived among the roots.

Field Record ended at the line they had chosen not to cross. Beyond it was blank. Lucas stared at that blankness in his memory.

Tomorrow, if his leg allowed it, they would go back. Not because the dungeon owed them levels. Because they had learned enough to know how much they still did not understand.

That was dangerous. It was also irresistible.

The Glassroot beneath Shiverstone pulsed somewhere beyond the dark. This time, Lucas knew it could pulse back.
